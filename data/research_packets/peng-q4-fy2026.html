What the quarter delivered

A material beat with a changing mix

Quarterly revenue was $566.7 million, up 67.7% year over year. Adjusted diluted earnings were $1.00 versus $0.43 a year earlier. The provider estimate snapshot was $521.0 million of revenue and $0.772 of adjusted EPS, making the reported beats 8.8% and 29.5%, respectively. These are comparisons against the captured estimate record, not a claim that every analyst used the same definitions or revised at the same time.

The improvement is visible before adjustments. GAAP operating income reached $69.5 million against $12.4 million a year earlier. Gross profit increased while operating expenses grew much more slowly than sales, producing operating leverage. Yet the gross-margin percentage slipped from the year-earlier level: a larger revenue base does not mean each dollar carries a better gross spread. That is why we separate volume, mix, gross profit and operating leverage rather than treating the earnings multiple as a single explanation.

Management now guides fiscal 2027 revenue growth to 40%, plus or minus ten percentage points, and adjusted EPS to $4.45, plus or minus $0.70. The release describes a roughly $2.43 billion revenue midpoint compared with the earlier preliminary $2.17 billion view. Applying 40% to the exact reported fiscal 2026 base produces $2.424 billion; the difference from $2.43 billion is rounding, not another guidance change. The wide range is material. A model that uses only the midpoint hides the execution risk in supplier availability, project timing and the memory cycle.

The FY2026 10-K was not yet in the source set. Current annual statements come from the unaudited earnings exhibit. That is enough to analyze the verified print, with a clear obligation to revisit the annual footnotes when filed. It is not permission to assume missing segment profit, contract economics or tax detail.

Where the growth actually came from

Memory leads the reported bridge

Integrated Memory generated $340.8 million of quarterly sales, compared with $132.2 million a year earlier. That is 157.9% growth. Its $208.6 million increase contributed 91.2% of the consolidated revenue increase. Advanced Computing grew to $154.0 million from $138.3 million, while Optimized LED reached $71.9 million from $67.4 million. The arithmetic is central to the thesis: the current reported growth is overwhelmingly a memory contribution, even while the forward narrative emphasizes AI infrastructure wins.

This is not an argument that the new customer wins have no value. The release identifies six new AI Infrastructure data-center customers in the quarter, including four neocloud providers; seventeen new customers and twelve expansions were reported for the full year. Those are encouraging activity indicators. They are not disclosed backlog dollars, contracted Penguin revenue, cash receipts, or guaranteed future margins. In particular, the customer's own signed compute contracts must not be described as Penguin's revenue backlog. The Norway deployment references 36,000 GPUs, but the release does not disclose Penguin's contract value or profit on that engagement.

The latest filed quarterly segment-profit disclosure is Q3, ending May 29. Its management non-GAAP presentation shows an Advanced Computing loss of $1.9 million, Memory income of $62.2 million and LED income of $4.1 million. The segment footnote also presents a different Advanced Computing measure including a $5.8 million insurance recovery; those measures must not be mixed. We use the explicit management non-GAAP presentation and label its period. Q4 segment profit is unavailable in the current release, so no margin is allocated by analogy.

The next evidence needed is therefore specific: convert the larger customer set into profitable Advanced Computing revenue while retaining Memory economics as input pricing and availability change. A customer count can rise before cash arrives. A differentiated service relationship could deserve a stronger multiple, but that premium must be earned in revenue quality and recurring economics, not assigned to all hardware sales automatically.

Follow profit to common shareholders

Tax, financing and participation change the answer

The operating improvement is real, but the route from operating income to EPS is unusual this quarter. Starting from $69.462 million of GAAP operating income, net interest income adds $0.980 million and other non-operating expense removes $33.028 million, leaving $37.414 million of pretax income. A $57.595 million income-tax benefit then lifts consolidated net income to $95.009 million. After noncontrolling interests, preferred dividends and the allocation to participating securities, $81.267 million is available to common stockholders under the reported presentation. Each step has a different economic meaning.

The tax benefit is especially important because it makes GAAP EPS higher than adjusted EPS. We do not call all of that reported profit a recurring operating run rate. The cash-flow reconciliation includes a substantial deferred-tax adjustment, and the annual balance sheet shows a larger deferred-tax asset. Without the new annual tax footnote, we do not assign the benefit to an unsupported specific cause or promise it will recur. The non-GAAP reconciliation removes tax effects alongside the other identified adjustments; this is a reason to inspect the bridge, not to dismiss either accounting measure.

The financing-related inducement expense of $33.248 million is also not a normal product margin. It relates to conversions of older notes. Excluding it may help compare operating periods, but the associated capital transaction is economically consequential: the July filing describes cash plus approximately 8.7 million common shares exchanged for old notes. Calling the expense noncash would not make that dilution free.

Preferred dividends and participating-security allocations explain why consolidated net income cannot simply be divided by a provider common-share count. The company reports separate GAAP and adjusted diluted denominators, with adjustments for securities and capped calls. Our EPS route uses the company's adjusted common EPS directly. Our enterprise routes handle preferred conversion explicitly and disclose the denominator, avoiding a second preferred deduction on top of conversion shares.

Earnings quality

Cash conversion is the principal weakness

The same nine checks yield a mixed picture. Stock compensation is manageable relative to current sales, and reported operating leverage is strong. Cash conversion is weak, the tax rate is distorted by a benefit, and the common-share capital structure requires care. A single composite quality score would conceal these differences. We show the checks separately so a reader can decide which weaknesses can reverse and which belong in the valuation permanently.

Receivables increased to $796.3 million and inventories to $748.8 million. Using ending balances divided by current-quarter sales or cost of sales, multiplied by 91 days, produces rough collection and inventory proxies, not audited days-sales-outstanding or average-balance turnover. The end-point method is deliberately labeled because rapid growth can make it look worse than an average-balance calculation. Even with that caveat, the cash absorption is large enough to matter. A claim of temporary investment needs subsequent collections and inventory shipment evidence.

For the year, receivables and inventory together consumed $982.0 million of cash. Higher payables and other liabilities funded a substantial part of that build. This funding can accompany legitimate growth, but supplier financing is not the same as customer cash conversion. If demand timing changes, inventory can remain while obligations come due. We would watch revenue growth together with cash flow and balance-sheet changes, not use any one line as proof of distress or success.

Reported FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $151.9 million. Subtracting $11.6 million of capital expenditures gives negative $163.5 million of free cash flow. This definition includes the cash effects of stock compensation through the accounts and does not add SBC back as free money in valuation. The latest annual earnings exhibit provides enough detail for the calculation; provider cash-flow fields currently contain zeros and must not be mistaken for zero investment. Primary statements override those incomplete records.

Balance sheet and financing

A bigger cash balance does not erase dilution

Cash and equivalents ended the year at $647.2 million, against $789.0 million of current and long-term debt carrying value. That produces $141.7 million of net debt before operating leases. There is also $202.7 million of temporary equity representing convertible preferred stock and $16.4 million of noncontrolling interests. Our enterprise bridge treats operating leases as operating obligations already reflected in operating cash economics, rather than silently mixing a lease-inclusive provider debt number with an unadjusted EBIT multiple.

The July transaction issued $750 million of zero-coupon notes due in 2031. The absence of a regular coupon lowers near-term cash interest, but the principal obligation remains. Proceeds funded old-note repurchases, repayment of the revolver, capped calls and general purposes. The old-note exchange also issued approximately 4.7 million and 4.0 million common shares in the two transactions. This is why a static provider share count is an unreliable denominator for the new valuation.

The 2031 notes begin with a conversion price around $116.70; the capped calls initially cap protection at $175.05. Those thresholds are contract details, not a prediction that the shares will reach them. Capped calls reduce some potential dilution under specified conditions, cost cash, involve counterparties, and do not forgive principal. We retain the debt claim and do not add all note-conversion shares to the same scenario, which would count the same financing twice.

The preferred stock has a different economic claim. The annual report gives a $200 million original investment and a $32.81 initial conversion price. For the enterprise models, we assume conversion into approximately 6.10 million shares and add those to the 63 million guided diluted common planning denominator. We then do not also subtract preferred carrying value. This is a modeled as-converted case, not a statement that conversion has happened. Changes to conversion terms, participating distributions or the actual diluted denominator are explicit reasons to update the model.

Valuation

Three routes, one cautious call

Our base fair value is $60, with a $35 to $100 scenario range and high uncertainty. At the $64.21 reference close, the price is slightly above our base case. The operating acceleration prevents a reflexively bearish conclusion, but cash conversion and financing complexity prevent a high-conviction BUY. We require a 20% margin below the base value, or about $48, unless new evidence supports a higher intrinsic value. This is an analytical decision rule, not a forecast of the next traded price.

The discounted cash-flow route starts with the exact FY2026 revenue base. Revenue grows 40%, 20%, 15%, 10% and 8% across FY2027–31. GAAP EBIT margins begin at 13.5%, rise to 14.5%, then stabilize at 15%. These are our assumptions, not company guidance beyond the first-year reference. Cash tax is 20%; depreciation and amortization are 2.5% of sales; capex is 1.8%; incremental working capital consumes 12% of each sales increase. GAAP EBIT retains stock compensation as an expense. The working-capital assumption requires a dramatic improvement from the latest cash absorption and is a principal model risk.

At a 12% discount rate and 3% terminal growth, the model produces about $4.265 billion of enterprise value. Subtracting $141.7 million net debt and $16.4 million NCI, then dividing by 69.10 million modeled shares, gives $59.43. Roughly 71% of enterprise value comes from terminal value. That dependence is why the sensitivity grid matters and why we publish a broad scenario range rather than pretending a cent-level output is accurate.

The earnings route is deliberately simpler: management midpoint adjusted EPS of $4.45 times an author-selected 15 multiple gives $66.75. This uses the company EPS after its stated common-holder allocations, with no extra debt or preferred subtraction. The third route applies 13 times modeled FY2027 GAAP EBIT of $325.5 million, using the guided gross-margin and expense midpoints; the same enterprise-to-common bridge gives $58.95. These routes cluster around the low sixties, so we round the base to $60. The market at $64.21 is already asking for much of that successful execution. It is not pricing a complete failure.

The bear case uses $3.75 adjusted EPS and a nine multiple, rounded to $35, with 30% weight. The base receives 50% at $60. The bull uses $5.15 and twenty times earnings, rounded conservatively to $100, with 20% weight. The probability-weighted value is $60.50. Probabilities and multiples are our judgments. They are not objective frequencies, and favorable guidance alone does not guarantee the bull case.

Management scorecard

Reward the execution; test the conversion

Management deserves credit for the second-half acceleration and the higher forward view. The release ties the change to demand, product work, supply arrangements and new customer relationships. Those explanations are plausible and have measurable outcomes. They should be judged against subsequent revenue, margins, collections and financing needs rather than against the number of AI references in a presentation. The most useful scorecard separates selling activity from cash results.

Capital allocation is more ambiguous. A zero-coupon financing extends maturity and supplies liquidity, but common shares were issued in the old-note exchange and cash was spent on capped calls and share purchases. The company can have improved financing terms and dilution at the same time. The newly appointed CFO, Stephen Cumming, starts during a period when working-capital discipline and clear disclosure are especially valuable. The appointment is a disclosed personnel change, not evidence by itself of a financial-reporting problem.

We would ask five questions. How much of the receivables increase is tied to contractual payment schedules versus overdue balances? What portion of inventory is committed to identifiable customers, and what cancellation protection exists? How do service and software economics change the margin of the new AI infrastructure wins? What is the expected cash-conversion path under the new revenue guidance? How should investors reconcile the forward diluted-share guide with preferred participation, old-note exchange shares and capped-call treatment? Answers would reduce uncertainty more than another headline customer count.

Ranked risks

The strongest bull and bear cases

The strongest bull case is that Penguin is building relationships and supply capacity ahead of a much larger deployment cycle. If customers pay on schedule, inventory ships and recurring management services grow, today’s cash absorption could precede a sustained improvement in cash earnings. Memory strength would fund investment while Advanced Computing customer wins mature. Under that path, a multiple above our base earnings route can be reasonable. We do not discard this outcome merely because current free cash flow is negative.

The strongest bear case is that investors are paying an AI-platform multiple for a large cyclical memory contribution and hardware working-capital burden. Receivables may take longer to collect, inventory may become less valuable, and supplier terms may tighten. A tax benefit and adjusted earnings growth could mask weaker owner cash generation. If financing repeatedly increases the common denominator, company growth need not translate proportionately into value per share.

Our position sits between those cases: recognize the operating beat, retain high uncertainty, and demand measurable conversion before increasing the valuation. The risk table ranks execution and liquidity ahead of generic market volatility. An unexpected loss of a major customer or supplier could make the downside nonlinear; the annual report describes meaningful customer concentration and dependence on a limited supplier set. Historical FY2025 concentration is evidence of exposure, not a claim that those exact percentages describe today’s quarter.

What changes the call

Dates are checkpoints, not invented announcements

The next filing and operating updates can change the valuation without waiting for a full year of results. The pending FY2026 annual report should supply the audited notes behind cash restrictions, taxes, segment economics and financing. We treat that as a filing to monitor, with no fabricated publication date. Our dated signposts use calendar review checkpoints so the thesis can be tested even when the issuer has not announced the next earnings release.

The first business test is whether subsequent growth is accompanied by improved cash conversion. The second is whether Advanced Computing turns a growing customer set into disclosed profitable revenue. The third is whether the forward diluted-share and preferred assumptions match actual reporting. A missed checkpoint is a prompt to investigate, not an automatic trading rule. Stronger sales alone will not resolve all three questions.

We would consider upgrading the call if the cash evidence improves enough to support the model while the price offers the required margin of safety, or if new disclosed recurring economics justify raising fair value. We would lower the valuation if cash absorption remains severe, guidance weakens, inventory loses value, or additional financing changes the per-share economics. These conditions make the call falsifiable: the same evidence that supports a higher value must be allowed to weaken it.
