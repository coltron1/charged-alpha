Reported growth of 17.4% combines 14.6 percentage points from acquisition, 0.9 points from currency and 1.9 points of organic growth. Within organic growth, pricing contributed 2.2 points while volume and mix reduced growth by 0.3 points. These are rounded company growth contributions, not exact dollar allocations. The acquired business is real, but changing the consolidation perimeter is different from selling substantially more product within an unchanged business.

Segments

A larger Consumer business and stronger Flavor Solutions margin.

Consumer delivered $1,215.4 million of sales and $241.1 million of segment operating income, while Flavor Solutions produced $809.4 million and $117.4 million. Segment income excludes special charges. It should be compared with adjusted consolidated operating profit rather than GAAP operating profit. The two segment profits total $358.5 million before the $141.5 million operating charge. This explicit reconciliation avoids presenting a segment margin improvement as though shareholders incurred no transaction or restructuring costs.

Consumer’s 24.9% reported growth includes a 23.2-point acquisition contribution. Organic growth was 1.1%, with pricing offsetting unfavorable volume and mix. The Americas organic result was negative 0.3%, despite very large reported growth. That is the most useful check on a simplistic acquisition-success narrative: accounting scale can grow while demand in an existing regional business remains soft. Consumer segment margin was approximately 19.8%, slightly below the prior rounded 19.9%.

Flavor Solutions serves food manufacturers and foodservice customers. Its 3.0% organic growth included positive volume and mix, and its segment margin rose to approximately 14.5% from 13.3%. That is a cleaner operating improvement, though acquisition and currency also contributed to reported growth. Customer concentration still matters: the annual filing says the segment’s three largest customers accounted for 49% of sales. A concentrated customer base can support efficient scale while exposing the supplier to customer-specific changes in product demand, negotiations or sourcing.

Profit bridge

Adjusted operating growth did not flow straight to common shareholders.

Gross profit rose to $794.9 million and gross margin reached 39.3%. Management attributes the expansion to Mexico, higher sales and productivity, partly offset by commodity and freight inflation. Below gross profit, SG&A was $436.4 million and operating special charges were $141.5 million. GAAP operating profit consequently fell to $217.0 million even as adjusted operating profit rose 22.1% to $358.5 million. Both measures are useful when their purposes are kept separate.

Quarterly interest expense of $68.4 million exceeds the prior $50.2 million. The quarter also had a higher adjusted tax rate, 22.6% versus 16.1%. Income from unconsolidated operations changed because Mexico is now consolidated, and outside owners retain a minority claim. These effects help explain why adjusted net income grew only 1.1%, to $231.7 million, while adjusted operating income grew much faster. Counting acquired operating profit without the financing and ownership claims would overstate the benefit to existing shareholders.

The year-to-date GAAP result points in the opposite direction because the January acquisition triggered an $866.8 million remeasurement gain on the previously held stake. The nine-month cash-flow statement reverses this gain. It is not new cash available for dividends, debt repayment or a second acquisition. A low trailing GAAP price-to-earnings ratio built around that gain would make the stock appear artificially cheap. Our earnings route instead starts with the company’s current adjusted full-year guidance and separately tests cash generation.

Valuation

Three transparent equity checks, with no capitalized Unilever synergies.

We set base fair value at $52 per existing share, with a $36–68 scenario range and high uncertainty. This is a standalone current-equity assessment, not a fully financed combined-company target. The transaction can change the share count, debt, earnings mix and required return materially. A future closing disclosure must trigger a rebuilt model. The present estimate gives no separate value to anticipated Unilever cost savings and does not pretend that today’s shareholders will own all of the enlarged business.

Company EPS midpoint $3.09 × analyst-selected 17× multiple = $52.53. The multiple allows franchise value but discounts low organic growth and transaction uncertainty.

Trailing OCF less capex $925.9M ÷ 269.4M shares = $3.4369. Multiply by 1.03, then divide by (10% required equity return − 3.5% terminal growth). The result is $54.46. This simplified perpetuity is sensitive to minority cash and working capital.

3

Dividend check: $49.44

Annualized quarterly dividend $1.92 × 1.03 ÷ (7% equity discount − 3% perpetual growth) = $49.44. This route values a relatively stable distribution stream; the assumed discount and growth are analytical choices, not promises.

The quote is 14.79 times the $3.09 guidance midpoint. At our 17-times valuation assumption, it requires about $2.69 of sustainable adjusted EPS, below guidance. That creates a plausible value argument. But the 13.8% gap to our base is smaller than the protection we require for high uncertainty. A 20% discount to $52 gives a price-only entry threshold of $41.60, conditional on the business value remaining intact. A falling price caused by deteriorating cash flow does not automatically meet that economic test.

The bear case uses $3.00 of sustainable adjusted EPS at 12 times, or $36. The bull uses $3.40 at 20 times, or $68. We assign 25%, 50% and 25% weights to bear, base and bull, producing a $52 weighted value. These are transparent judgment weights, not statistically measured probabilities. Higher interest expense, weaker demand and a lower multiple can occur together; successful integration and improved cash conversion can also reinforce one another. The range expresses that interaction instead of hiding uncertainty inside a precise point estimate.

Risks and counterarguments

The pending deal is a research obligation, not a reason to ignore current results.

The proposed combination would leave current McCormick shareholders with approximately 35.0% of the combined company; current Unilever holders would own approximately 55.1%, and Unilever could retain up to 9.9%. Unilever would also receive $15.7 billion in cash, subject to adjustments. These percentages describe expected ownership under the agreement, not completed dilution today. A larger business can create value, but the relevant question is the earnings and cash attributable to each existing share after financing, new equity, integration and tax effects.

The filing describes a bridge financing commitment originally up to $15.7 billion and an additional $2.0 billion term-loan facility intended to fund at closing. Permanent financing reduces the bridge commitment; these amounts must not simply be added as independent funded debt. The term loan is floating rate and matures three years after closing. Receipt of financing is not a condition to McCormick’s obligation to complete the transaction. Regulatory approvals, shareholder approval and an effective registration statement remain conditions. The release expects closing by mid-2027, not an accomplished event.

The strongest bull case is not merely that the stock has fallen. It is that flavor brands, customer relationships and productivity support dependable cash while Mexico improves the earnings mix. Successful Unilever execution could broaden the platform and improve margins. Management forecasts mid- to high-single-digit adjusted EPS accretion in the first year after closing and mid- to high-teens in year three. These remain company forecasts with financing and execution dependencies, not profits already available for distribution.

The bear case is that organic demand remains weak while interest, integration costs and a larger share base absorb much of the apparent operating growth. A transaction can also consume management attention and expose the company to costs even if it does not close. The filing specifies a possible $420 million termination fee or up to $75 million of expense reimbursement in particular circumstances. We do not treat either as an inevitable liability, but neither belongs outside the risk analysis. Our Hold balances the existing cash franchise against those unresolved economics.
