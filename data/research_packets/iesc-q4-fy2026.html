The price discounts some of the operating opportunity

Our call is BUY with conviction of 3 out of 5 at the November 20, 2026 closing price of $156.06, recorded at 4 p.m. Eastern. The main model estimates present common-share value of $199.88. Conditional bear and bull values are $66.01 and $357.06. Those endpoints are not a statistical confidence interval, a forecast trading band or a worst-case floor. A serious project failure or financing disruption could be worse than the modeled bear.

The base owner return is 16.2% annually through September 30, 2031, including modeled cash distributions after interest and debt repayment. It exceeds our 12% purchase hurdle. Today's gap to fair value is a different concept: it does not establish when the market will recognize value or how much cash shareholders will receive. We also apply an entry discipline of about 20% below base value, approximately $159.91. That purchase threshold is separate from the fair-value calculation and does not reduce the model's value a second time.

The stock closed down 6.49% from $166.90. That is an observed print-day price change, not proof that the earnings release alone caused the move. The retained after-hours observation was $158.27 at 7:54 p.m. Eastern; the valuation and rating consistently use the regular-session close. The share price has already been adjusted for the August two-for-one stock split. Old unadjusted targets and earnings figures should not be compared with it without a verified adjustment.

IES is growing around data-center construction and infrastructure, but it remains a collection of operating businesses with labor, materials, collections and execution risk. The buy thesis is that the current price allows a useful return after normalizing unusually favorable margins. The independent current-profit check is less enthusiastic, and DBM's acquisition accounting remains incomplete. Those limitations keep conviction moderate even though the main model offers upside.

The quarter delivered operating growth, not just investment gains

Fourth-quarter revenue was $1.390 billion, up from $897.8 million a year earlier. Operating income increased to $217.2 million from $104.3 million. Computed operating margin increased to 15.6% from 11.6%. The improvement therefore extends beyond revenue growth: the operating businesses earned more profit from each reported sales dollar. Management attributes the performance to execution, capacity investment and strong demand in the markets served by three of the four pre-acquisition segments.

Net income attributable to IES was $182.3 million, while net income attributable to common stockholders after the additional ownership adjustment was $181.8 million. Diluted EPS was $4.50. Adjusted net income attributable to IES was $165.1 million and adjusted diluted EPS was $4.07. All of those per-share figures reflect the two-for-one stock split. The adjusted result is lower because investment income removed from the measure exceeded the specified expense adjustments. Adjusted does not universally mean higher, or mean that every remaining dollar is cash.

Full-year revenue was $4.478 billion, compared with $3.371 billion. Operating profit was $605.7 million versus $383.5 million. The $536.6 million net income attributable to IES includes investment effects that do not belong in the recurring operating forecast. We use segment operating economics, normal taxes and reinvestment rather than simply capitalizing the reported net income or the strongest quarter.

Management expects continued strong organic growth in Communications, Commercial and Industrial, and Infrastructure Solutions. The release does not give a numerical fiscal 2027 revenue or EPS range. Our $6.836 billion fiscal 2027 revenue forecast includes the newly acquired Structural business and is a Charged Alpha estimate. A reliable post-release consensus aggregate was not available from the retained provider. We therefore make no claim that results beat or missed a numerical Street forecast.

Separate the economics of the five businesses

Communications generated fiscal 2026 revenue of $1.689 billion and operating profit of $290.0 million, a computed 17.2% margin. It designs and installs communications infrastructure, including systems within data centers. Reliable execution and trained labor are central to the value proposition. The September contracted remaining performance obligations of $2.003 billion support a strong demand outlook, but timing still depends on customer projects, workforce availability and conversion of contracts into completed work.

Commercial and Industrial revenue reached $799.5 million with $163.1 million of operating profit. Its full-year margin was 20.4%, and the fourth-quarter margin was approximately 27.0%. Management specifically identifies large, fast-moving jobs at favorable margins in the second half. We normalize that unusually strong performance rather than treating the quarter as a perpetual run rate. Base operating margin starts at 18% in fiscal 2027 and declines toward 14% by fiscal 2036.

Infrastructure Solutions reported $826.6 million of revenue and $194.4 million of operating profit. Capacity expansion supported demand for engineered products and services. Gulf Island, acquired in January, contributed approximately $151 million of revenue and $11 million of profit. The acquired contribution prevents interpreting the entire 66% revenue increase as organic growth. Newer facilities can also begin below mature margins while personnel, processes and utilization develop.

Residential revenue declined to $1.216 billion and operating profit fell to $40.1 million from $103.8 million. Housing affordability, borrowing costs and input inflation created pressure. Our base assumes another 3% revenue decline in fiscal 2027, followed by normalization, rather than giving the division a data-center growth rate. DBM adds the fifth business, Structural, after year end. Its reported trailing revenue was approximately $1.5 billion through June; our 6% initial operating margin is an assumption, not disclosed current segment guidance.

Investment income and ownership explain the profit bridge

The fourth-quarter operating result is the cleanest starting reference for the project businesses, but it is not the final claim of common shareholders. Interest expense was $0.4 million, the marketable securities result was a $3.8 million loss, and other income was $3.7 million. Pretax income before equity-method investment income was $216.6 million. After a $58.1 million tax provision and $24.3 million of equity-method income, consolidated net income was $182.8 million. Minority interests and a further ownership adjustment reduced the amount attributable to common stockholders to $181.8 million.

For the full year, the investment effects are more striking. Gains on marketable securities were $76.611 million, and Jett equity-method income was $28.490 million. Together those are approximately $105.1 million, although the tax and accounting treatment differs between the lines. They are not a second source of recurring project operating profit. The main valuation adds the dated securities and Jett investment balances once in the financial bridge and excludes their earnings from operating cash forecasts.

The release's adjusted profit reconciliation removes those investment effects and adds specified acquisition-related compensation. We retain normal employee economics in the model. A cost can be excluded from a company's adjusted earnings definition without disappearing from the owners' economic burden. In particular, cash-settled awards and stock awards need consistent treatment across expense, claims and shares.

Our forecast also includes corporate costs rather than valuing the operating segments as if headquarters were free. The base begins with $90 million of annual corporate expense, growing at 4%. Historical segment revenues sum to more than consolidated revenue because of eliminations. We scale the legacy elimination ratio in the forecast and make no separate Structural elimination assumption until better combined-company disclosure is available. This is a limitation of the initial integration model.

The cash statement needs a trading-securities adjustment

Reported operating cash flow was $364.516 million for fiscal 2026. Subtracting $163.081 million of property and equipment purchases gives $201.435 million. That simple cash-flow-minus-capex measure is useful, but the operating cash statement also includes $151.7 million of cash used in marketable securities trading. Adding that trading use back gives an analytical $353.135 million before acquisitions and financing. It is not reported company free cash flow and should not be presented as an interchangeable accounting measure.

Working capital remains important even after this adjustment. Trade receivables and retainage totaled $1.159 billion at September 30, compared with $652.1 million a year earlier. Billings ahead of work also increased substantially to $492.8 million. Advance billings help fund contracts, but they do not make the future work free. The company must still complete projects, pay labor and suppliers, and collect final balances. Growth can improve profit while consuming incremental cash.

Our scorecard uses average beginning and ending trade receivables plus retainage for collection days, divided by annual revenue; it is an approximate balance-sheet measure rather than a contract-by-contract collection schedule. Inventory days use average inventory divided by cost of services, even though labor-heavy and materials-heavy segments differ. Both calculations can be affected by acquisitions and rapidly changing activity. They should be read as comparison tools, not precise forecasts of when cash will arrive.

The model keeps future economic compensation in operating costs. Reported equity compensation of $14.969 million was about 0.33% of revenue, and liability-classified award expense was separately $20.888 million. The latter should not be silently folded into an equity-only SBC metric. The existing award allocation and accrued cash claims are discussed in the balance sheet section. We do not calculate a Beneish or Altman score with missing or incompatible inputs, and no numeric guidance record is claimed where management gave qualitative guidance.

Growth and normalized margins determine the main value

We choose a five-segment free-cash-flow-to-the-firm model because IES combines profitable contracting, engineered products, housing services and newly acquired structural steel capabilities. The segments share customers and execution requirements but have different margins, cycles and reinvestment needs. A uniform technology revenue multiple would not capture those differences. Quarterly earnings times four would also overstate the persistence of the strongest recent C&I projects.

Base fiscal 2027 growth is 25% for Communications, C&I and Infrastructure, negative 3% for Residential and 5% for Structural. Each growth rate fades to 3% by fiscal 2031 and stays there through the explicit forecast. Initial segment operating margins are 17%, 18%, 22%, 3% and 6%, respectively. They evolve toward 15%, 14%, 18%, 5% and 7% by fiscal 2036. These are author estimates. They use backlog, historical economics and the need for normalization, but they are not numerical management guidance.

After corporate costs and the existing-award normalization, base fiscal 2027 operating profit is $814.7 million. A 25% cash tax rate, depreciation at 1.5% of revenue, capex beginning at 2.8% of revenue and working capital equal to 8% of revenue growth produce $453.5 million of full-year FCFF. Only 314 days of that fiscal-year cash are included after November 20. The uniform proration is approximate; actual project collections are not uniform. Acquired opening operating working capital is treated as purchased and is not charged again as a new operating cash outflow.

The explicit forecast runs through fiscal 2036. Terminal cash is next-year after-tax operating profit multiplied by one minus terminal growth divided by return on new capital. Base terminal growth is 2.5%, incremental return on capital 20% and enterprise discount rate 10%. The terminal reinvestment rule replaces the explicit-period investment formulas, rather than stacking another full capex charge on top. Terminal value contributes 50.7% of enterprise present value, so long-run assumptions remain material.

The owner-return ledger is distinct from enterprise valuation. It pays assumed 6.5% interest after tax, uses available cash to repay borrowing and finance leases, and then distributes excess cash. In the base, $32.8 million remains at fiscal 2027 year end and is repaid in fiscal 2028, before the January 2030 maturity. The September 2031 residual price is $244.12, with $64.30 cumulative modeled distributions, giving a timed 16.2% annual return. These are equivalent distributions, not declared dividends or a commitment to that capital-allocation schedule.

The independent check uses external operating-profit valuation references and current earnings rather than the main model's future cash flows. The dated provider ratios were 13.37 times EV/EBIT for EMCOR and 16.28 times for Comfort Systems. Both have relevant construction and infrastructure exposure, but neither perfectly matches IES's portfolio, scale, control structure, lease accounting or growth. Provider enterprise-value definitions also may treat lease liabilities differently from our operating-rent convention.

We apply a lower 10–13 times range to normalized current legacy IES EBIT. We then add DBM at its approximately $691 million actual transaction consideration and apply the same financial and ownership bridge. The result is $147.01–$188.66 per share. Transaction consideration is not a verified debt-free enterprise appraisal, and incomplete acquired-claims disclosure limits precision.

This check is less enthusiastic than the main DCF, which requires growth to create more cash than today's normalized profit base. We do not average the two methods to manufacture a compromise target. The disagreement lowers conviction and tells us what must be demonstrated: profitable conversion of a much larger pipeline. If that growth fails, the current-profit check becomes a more relevant economic reference, though it still is not a liquidation floor.

Capital allocation and control deserve their own scorecard

Management has expanded capacity and completed acquisitions that broaden what IES can provide to customers. The evidence supports operating execution in several businesses, but capital allocation should be graded on later cash returns, not merely the size of the acquired revenue. Gulf Island required $143.1 million of cash during the year, and DBM required much more after year end. The main model includes the new business and the financing consequences together.

The company spent $163.1 million on capital expenditure in fiscal 2026, up from $67.3 million. Approximately $19.5 million purchased a production facility and related assets in Abilene. A larger physical footprint can remove bottlenecks, but utilization and project economics determine whether that investment creates value. The forecast continues spending on physical investment even after growth normalizes; it does not assume the current capacity expansion is a one-time cost followed by costless growth.

Post-year repurchases of 350,000 shares cost $86.8 million, at an average price of $248.00. Tontine controlled approximately 52% of outstanding shares using the October ownership filing and November share register. The operating sensitivity matters more than small bridge changes

The largest risks are project conversion, cost execution and growth persistence. A contractor can win work that earns less than expected if labor is scarce, material prices rise, schedules slip or estimates prove too optimistic. Fixed-price work can make cost overruns especially consequential. Backlog may also include agreements that cannot be enforced before work starts. We put these risks in segment growth, margin and working-capital paths rather than subtracting repeated generic risk discounts afterward.

A five-percentage-point reduction in the entire annual growth path of the three data-center-facing segments lowers present base value to $144.51. A two-percentage-point reduction in their operating margins lowers it to $173.80. Combining the two gives $125.96. The growth sensitivity applies in every explicit year, so after the base fades it can imply contraction; it is not merely one weak starting quarter. These tests show why apparently strong current profits do not make the stock risk-free.

The reverse calculation holds all other base rules fixed and solves for the common growth shift that matches the $156.06 quote. It requires initial growth of 21.2% rather than 25%, with the same 3.8-point reduction in later years. Once the base has faded to 3%, the reverse path is about negative 0.8%. That is one internally consistent interpretation, not a claim that investors literally forecast that sequence. Lower margins, a higher discount rate or different claims could also explain the price.

The bear combines slower demand, weaker project economics, housing pressure and more working-capital funding. It produces $66.01 present value and a negative 7.3% annual modeled owner return. The bull combines stronger growth, better margins and successful integration, producing $357.06 and a 31.6% return. Subjective weights of 25%, 50% and 25% give $205.71 weighted present value. They organize uncertainty but are not statistically calibrated probabilities. The base remains the principal rating reference.

Calculation references, history and source limitations

The actual earnings release was published on November 20, 2026 at 7:45 a.m. Eastern, or 6:45 a.m. Chicago. The covered fiscal quarter and year ended September 30. The nine-quarter table below uses SEC company-facts data for exact fiscal year-to-date intervals, with standalone quarters calculated from adjacent year-to-date values. Fourth quarters are annual totals less nine-month totals. The latest filing available through November 20 is selected for each exact interval. Revenue, operating income and reported operating cash are shown consistently; unavailable comparable net-income tags are not silently filled with another accounting concept. Acquisitions and rapidly changing activity limit simple period comparisons.

The full model separately records every annual segment path, corporate expense, tax rate, capital spending, working-capital charge, financing payment and share allocation. Dollars are in millions unless a per-share label says otherwise. Enterprise cash is discounted at scenario WACC; common equity is obtained through the bridge once. The return calculation uses timed owner cash and a remaining-business value after debt repayment. No weighted average of valuation methods replaces the base, and no guaranteed dividend or stock price is implied.

Important limitations remain: incomplete DBM acquisition accounting, dated investment values, a current-share register before some possible later repurchases, a proxy for existing equity dilution, approximate first-year cash proration and imperfect peer comparability. The $50 million acquisition allowance is a chosen sensitivity assumption, not proof of the acquired claims. The analysis should be updated when the combined balance sheet is reported. Business quality, model risk, present value and future return are separate judgments, and the BUY call depends on the dated price and the stated operating evidence.
