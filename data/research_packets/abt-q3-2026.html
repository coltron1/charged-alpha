What the quarter reports

The Q3 2026 earnings exhibit reports sales of $13.013B, up14.5% from $11.369B. Comparable growth is7.0%, while reported growth excluding currency is14.8%. These measures answer different questions. The comparable denominator adds $851M of historical Exact Sciences sales to the prior quarter and removes $23M related to the structural-heart agreement. Currency adjustment adds $40M to current reported sales. Thus $13.053B divided by $12.197B, less one, produces approximately7.0%. Simply subtracting current acquired revenue would not reproduce that calculation.

Adjusted net earnings rose8.7% to $2.477B and adjusted diluted EPS rose9.2% to $1.42. Weighted diluted shares fell from1,749M to1,738M, providing a small denominator benefit. GAAP net earnings fell44.1% to $920M and GAAP diluted EPS fell43.6% to $0.53. The divergence is large enough that neither the adjusted growth headline nor the GAAP decline alone describes the operating result. Both belong in the decision.

The current full-year comparable-sales outlook is approximately6%. Adjusted EPS guidance is $5.50–$5.55, a tighter range with an unchanged midpoint. Fourth-quarter adjusted EPS guidance is $1.62–$1.67. These are adjusted figures; the release does not supply a reliable forward GAAP reconciliation because certain specified items cannot be predicted. We do not turn the adjusted outlook into a fabricated GAAP forecast.

Four businesses and changing comparability

Medical Devices remains the largest reported segment at $5.826B, followed by Diagnostics at $3.305B, Nutrition at $2.262B and Established Pharmaceuticals at $1.614B. Those rounded reported segment amounts sum to $13.007B, leaving a $6M difference from consolidated sales. We retain the residual instead of assigning it to a business without a disclosed explanation. Current detailed segment profit is not supplied in this earnings exhibit, so segment revenue growth is not presented as segment profit growth.

Diagnostics reported growth of46.7% reflects the acquisition, whereas comparable growth is6.4%. Cancer Diagnostics generated $968M, up13.8% against the historical Exact Sciences comparative of$851M. Abbott completed that acquisition on March23. This is a meaningful new franchise, but its sales must fund integration, working capital, research and financing before they become common shareholder cash. The completed acquisition should not be described as an unresolved change of control of Abbott itself.

Medical Devices grew6.9% reported and7.8% comparable. Diabetes Care sales were $2.185B, while the release separately describes continuous glucose monitoring growth of7.3% reported and7.7% comparable. Those are not interchangeable measures. Amulet was reclassified from Structural Heart to Electrophysiology from January1; the prior Q3 category comparison was adjusted by$55M. Category trends must therefore use the issuer’s consistent comparisons rather than mixing historical labels.

Nutrition grew5.1% reported and4.9% comparable, and sales increased$118M sequentially. Established Pharmaceuticals grew6.8% reported and8.4% comparable. Currency affected these businesses differently. The newly announced Nutrition separation is a separate strategic event: its execution, debt allocation, legal liabilities and standalone cost base remain relevant even if current sales are improving. A revenue rebound alone cannot settle those transaction questions.

Follow the full earnings bridge

Operating earnings were $1.358B versus $2.057B, a34.0% decline. Current expenses include $5.628B cost of products sold excluding amortization, $841M intangible amortization, $920M research and development, and $4.266B selling, general and administrative expense. The increase in amortization and specified charges explains part of the gap, but those costs should not simply disappear from a shareholder’s assessment of acquisition economics.

The issuer’s reconciliation raises pretax earnings from$1.231B to$2.920B with$1.689B of specified items. Tax increases by$132M in the reconciliation, so the after-tax adjustment is$1.557B, taking net earnings from$920M to$2.477B. Intangible amortization is$841M; approximately$670M of legal settlements and$178M of other net charges complete the narrative breakdown. The legal amount is already within the detailed other-specified category, not a second charge to add again.

Adjusted gross profit of$7.448B less adjusted R&D of$892M and adjusted SG&A of$3.505B gives a derived adjusted operating result of$3.051B. This is a calculation from the reconciliation, not a separately disclosed segment measure. Net interest expense increased from$44M to$297M. Even when an analyst excludes intangible amortization from an operating valuation, financing expense still matters to equity cash and cannot be ignored in an EPS comparison.

The nine-quarter series preserves both GAAP and adjusted EPS. The unusually high $5.27 GAAP EPS in Q4 2024 reflects a significant noncash deferred-tax benefit; adjusted EPS was$1.34. Later tax expense includes effects related to that previously recognized deferred-tax asset. Treating the 2024 GAAP peak as ordinary earning power would distort growth, valuation and cash conversion. Which earnings deserve to be capitalized?

Revenue quality receives operating credit: comparable growth is positive across the four businesses, and new products and distribution can support recurring demand. But acquired sales are not organic growth. The cleanest starting point is the issuer’s explicit proforma comparable bridge, with its limitations visible. The quarter’s adjusted EPS increase also modestly exceeds adjusted net-income growth because diluted shares declined; buybacks and stock compensation should be examined together.

The specified-item bridge is essential but is not permission to treat every excluded dollar as irrelevant. Intangible amortization is noncash in the current quarter, yet it relates to assets acquired with real consideration. Legal settlements may be irregular and still require cash. Restructuring can recur. Our normalized operating-profit assumption retains a risk allowance and does not add back stock compensation. Investors who use a less generous normalization should expect lower values.

There is no current September balance-sheet or cash-flow statement in the earnings release. Accordingly, we do not claim a Q3 cash-conversion ratio, current receivable days, or fresh net-debt figure. The latest available June filing shows working-capital cash uses and acquisition funding, while audited annual statements show a stronger historical cash trend. That evidence supports analysis, but it cannot substitute for an unavailable quarter.

The annual report identifies Ernst & Young as auditor since2013. An audit of annual financial statements does not certify an analyst valuation or eliminate estimation risk in acquired intangibles, goodwill, taxes and litigation. Our evidence ledger retains primary statements, detailed reconciliations, raw tables and source hashes. Provider financial fields are used only as checks; where a provider label conflicts with primary disclosure or with its own headline, the conflict is disclosed rather than silently resolved in favor of a convenient story.

Cash, debt and capital allocation

Audited operating cash flow increased from$7.261B in2023 to$8.558B in2024 and$9.566B in2025. Deducting capital expenditure of$2.202B, $2.207B and$2.171B gives free cash flow of$5.059B, $6.351B and$7.395B. This is a substantial historical improvement. It is not a forecast that the acquired group will immediately generate the same margin or conversion rate after integration and financing.

Stock compensation was$644M, $673M and$664M over those years. A simple FCF-less-SBC proxy is useful for owner-cost awareness, but it is not GAAP cash flow and is not an extra deduction in every valuation model. Our operating model retains SBC in normalized profit and does not add it back. Deducting it again from those already burdened forecast cash flows would double count the cost.

The Q2 2026 quarterly filing reports first-half operating cash of$3.803B and capital spending of$896M, giving$2.907B FCF. Receivables used$428M and inventory used$721M of cash. The statement reports$19.962B of acquisitions net of acquired cash, $19.827B of new borrowing and$2.821B debt repayment. It also reports$2.200B dividends and$1.225B repurchases in the cash-flow statement. Financing and acquisition cash flows should not be confused with operating expenses or with ordinary FCF.

At June30, cash was$5.104B and short investments$499M, against current debt$3.005B and long debt$29.603B. The resulting$27.005B net debt is explicitly dated. Abbott assumed approximately$2.8B of Exact Sciences debt, nearly all repaid by June30. The preliminary purchase allocation includes$12.8B intangibles and$11.4B goodwill and remains subject to finalization. Debt repayment, purchase accounting and acquisition consideration are distinct; adding them indiscriminately would overstate the burden.

June receivables were$8.599B, inventory$7.315B and goodwill plus intangible assets$52.455B. These balances make collection, inventory productivity and acquired-asset returns important future tests. Point-in-time June shares differ from current quarter weighted diluted shares. Our per-share valuation consistently uses1,738M diluted shares and does not assume costless future share reduction. Dividend continuity is valuable only alongside sustainable cash coverage and a sensible debt path.

Three routes and the price-implied cash hurdle

The operating-cash DCF uses an explicit analyst FCFF path of$9.1B, $9.9B, $10.7B, $11.5B and$12.3B for2027–2031. These are nonuniform cash levels, not company guidance or a repeated constant-growth placeholder. The first year starts with analyst normalized operating profit of$12.5B, an18% cash-tax assumption, $1.6B depreciation addback, $2.3B capital expenditure and$450M working-capital use. That bridge produces$9.1B before debt financing.

Normalized operating profit excludes acquisition intangible amortization but retains SBC and an operating risk allowance. There is no additional intangible-amortization addback to this already normalized profit. The cash path requires execution: the latest reported annual FCF was$7.395B, on an equity cash-flow basis that includes interest effects, while the new model is an operating cash-flow scenario before financing. The two cannot be equated without explaining scope and financing. Legal outcomes and integration could materially reduce the forecast.

At a9% required operating return and3% terminal growth, the explicit cash flows have present value$41.085B and the terminal cash stream has present value$137.233B. Enterprise value is$178.318B. Deducting dated net debt$27.005B once and dividing by1,738M diluted shares gives$87.06. Approximately77.0% of enterprise value comes from the terminal component. This dependence is a reason for a wide range and lower confidence, not an obscure technical footnote.

The earnings cross-check applies17 times the $5.525 midpoint of adjusted2026 EPS guidance, giving$93.93. It adds no separate cash credit. The operating-profit cross-check applies15 times analyst2027 normalized EBIT of$12.5B, subtracts the same$27.005B net debt and uses the same diluted denominator, giving$92.34. Neither multiple is a verified peer median; both are explicit analyst judgments reflecting a valuable healthcare franchise alongside leverage and execution risks. The rounded central value is$90.

Reverse valuation holds first-year2027 FCFF at$9.1B and solves a distinct constant annual growth rate for2028–2031 that matches the saved$98.135 quote. The answer is approximately11.02%, ending at$13.825B in2031, with the same9% discount rate,3% terminal growth and dated debt. This is four annual steps after2027, not a perpetual growth assumption. The forward path remains the five separately authored cash levels. Changing the first-year base or debt would change the reverse answer.

Sensitivity is large. With3% terminal growth, changing the discount rate from8% to10% moves the DCF from$107.98 to$72.13. At9%, changing terminal growth from2% to4% moves value from$75.12 to$103.77. A one-point assumption change can exceed a quarter’s apparent earnings beat. We therefore use bear/base/bull values$65/$90/$125 with25%/50%/25% weights, yielding$92.50. The model values the consolidated existing business, without an unsupported Nutrition separation premium.

Execution, structure and the proposed separation

The October21 Nutrition separation announcement describes a plan to separate Nutrition into an independent company through a tax-free distribution, with completion targeted by the end of2027. The announcement describes about$8.5B annual Nutrition sales, approximately16,000 employees and14 manufacturing sites, while the remaining Abbott businesses represent about$42B sales. These are announcement scale figures, not a finalized standalone earnings model or a current-quarter reconciliation.

Approval, tax, financing and execution conditions remain. Shareholders need to know the distribution ratio, allocation of debt and liabilities, transition agreements, standalone overhead and retained Abbott costs. A separation can improve focus while creating duplicated expenses. Selling or distributing a business also changes the assets shareholders own. This packet neither excludes Abbott because of an ordinary continuing-parent transaction nor treats the strategic announcement as automatic value creation.

Reported employees were approximately114,000 in2023,114,000 in2024 and115,000 in2025. The current acquisition and eventual separation make simple per-employee comparisons unstable. Management’s capital-allocation test is whether new assets and retained research spending produce returns after financing and risk, while maintaining product quality and compliance. The March2026 proxy describes common shares as the only outstanding voting securities, with cumulative voting for directors and one vote per share on other matters. It reports BlackRock7.7% and Vanguard10.05% based on older filings; these are dated beneficial holdings, not verified October ownership or control percentages. We model no special control premium.

The risks that can change the conclusion

Legal risk deserves separate treatment from acquisition accounting. Abbott’s filings describe infant-formula and NEC litigation, appeals and other proceedings. The latest earnings release reports approximately$670M legal settlement charges, but the disclosure reviewed does not justify assigning that entire amount to one litigation category. The size and timing of future cash payments, insurance recoveries and any separation allocation remain uncertain. A GAAP charge is not necessarily a same-quarter cash payment.

Integration risk includes realizing the value of Exact Sciences without impairing product execution, research or compliance. Diagnostics sales growth must become durable earnings and cash, while added amortization and interest alter reported comparisons. Pricing pressure, reimbursement changes, emerging-market currency exposure and competition can affect the four businesses differently. Current positive comparable growth provides evidence of demand, but it is not a guarantee of future returns.

Valuation risk is amplified by terminal dependence and dated debt. Our9% discount rate and3% terminal growth are judgments, not observable facts. A higher required return or slower cash path lowers value sharply. The downside scenario is not a floor. Conversely, faster deleveraging, stronger cancer diagnostics or a clearly favorable separation can justify revisiting the cautious central estimate. Risk assessment must permit both directions rather than defending a fixed rating against new evidence.

What would change the call?

The next current cash-flow statement is the most useful missing piece. It can establish whether the acquired group’s cash generation supports the adjusted earnings story, how much working capital the larger business needs and whether settlement payments explain a temporary shortfall. A low cash number alone would not settle the thesis; the composition matters. Repeated weak conversion without an investment or timing explanation would reduce confidence.

The next two quarters should also clarify cancer-diagnostics growth, device category comparability and the full-year adjusted earnings outcome. For Nutrition, formal transaction terms matter more than a repeated completion target. Debt allocation and stranded costs may be as important as the headline valuation multiple assigned to the separated business. We will compare what management promised with what the statements and transaction documents subsequently show.

A price of$72 or below would offer a20% discount to our$90 central value and prompt a new review, provided the operating and legal facts remain intact. That is an analytical checkpoint, not a standing buy order. At a higher price, substantially stronger recurring cash or lower risk would be needed to support an upgrade. The current HOLD acknowledges the quality of the businesses while asking for more evidence before paying a premium for the next structure.
