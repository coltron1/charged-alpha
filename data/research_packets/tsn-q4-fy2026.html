The call: the recovery has a price

Our rating is SELL, or trim rather than add, at the dated $62.75 reference quote from November 16, 2026 at 12:12 p.m. Eastern. The main model estimates present fair value at $51.47 per share. This is a price judgment, not a claim that Tyson faces imminent financial failure or that the stock should be sold short. The company owns valuable brands and a major distribution network, but those assets do not make every purchase price attractive.

The decisive question is whether beef recovery creates enough incremental cash to offset chicken normalization and a larger capital budget. Management anticipates substantially smaller beef losses in fiscal 2027, potentially a modest profit. At the same time, the chicken profit guide is below fiscal 2026, pork is weaker, and capital spending rises. Investors need to follow those moving parts together. Looking only at beef improvement can miss the cost of the offsetting pressures.

Our base price at September 29, 2029 is approximately $61.46. Modeled dividends total $6.37 per share over the three fiscal endpoints, producing about 8.10% cumulative return and 2.85% annualized return from the reference price. That falls well short of our 10% equity-return requirement. The prospective price comes from remaining operating value and a forecast cash bridge, rather than mechanically compounding today's estimated fair value.

Uncertainty is high. Present bear and bull values range from $19.02 to $80.77. The bull demonstrates that a stronger recovery can justify meaningful upside; the bear demonstrates how debt amplifies a lasting shortfall in operating value. Our subjective weighted value is $50.68, but the base model leads the call. The weighted result is arithmetic across explicitly different operating cases, not an additional independent valuation or a calibrated probability forecast.

The reported print and the calendar adjustment

Fourth-quarter reported revenue was $14.585 billion, up 5.2% from $13.860 billion. Fiscal 2026 had 53 weeks, and its fourth quarter had 14 weeks. The comparison period had 52 and 13 weeks, respectively. Tyson estimates that the extra week contributed $1.049 billion of sales. Its 13-week adjusted quarterly sales were $13.536 billion. That adjustment is essential before interpreting the reported increase as demand growth.

Legal contingency accruals also reduced sales in both periods: $110 million in the latest quarter and $355 million a year earlier. Removing both the extra week and those legal reductions, the issuer reports a 4.0% comparable sales decline. Merely dividing adjusted sales by the prior reported number does not reproduce that particular operating comparison, because the legal adjustment is different in each period. The release supplies the definition, and we preserve it rather than casually mixing denominators.

GAAP operating income was $1 million, compared with $158 million. Adjusted operating income was $361 million, down from $608 million. GAAP diluted EPS was a loss of $0.22, versus profit of $0.13; adjusted EPS was $0.61, versus $1.15. Adjusted figures help isolate operating trends, but the reconciliation does not make restructuring, litigation or executive transition costs economically irrelevant. Some costs are noncash; others are cash obligations paid on different schedules.

For the full year, reported revenue was $56.419 billion, versus $54.441 billion. Adjusted 52-week sales were $55.370 billion. GAAP operating income was $1.100 billion, essentially unchanged, while adjusted operating income declined to $1.977 billion from $2.287 billion. Reported gross profit fell to $3.230 billion from $3.562 billion. Those measures show that the company has not yet converted nominal revenue growth into a broad profit recovery. The improving cash-flow result deserves separate analysis rather than being used to erase the operating deterioration.

Five businesses with different economics

Beef is a processing-spread business. Higher cattle prices can raise the selling price of beef while reducing the profit available to the processor buying those animals. Comparable annual beef volume declined 12.2%, while adjusted average price rose 10.0%. The adjusted annual segment loss widened to $654 million from $275 million. The GAAP loss was $1.215 billion. A network restructuring can improve economics by removing uneconomic capacity, but continued cattle scarcity can still reduce throughput and fixed-cost absorption at the remaining plants.

Chicken generated $1.859 billion of adjusted annual operating income, compared with $1.892 billion. It remains a major profit engine, but the quarter weakened: adjusted operating margin declined to 8.7% from 12.7%. Relative affordability can support chicken demand when beef is expensive, yet industry supply, feed costs, plant yields and product mix determine the spread. Demand and margin do not necessarily move together. Competitors can increase supply into the same attractive consumer market.

Prepared Foods generated $1.332 billion of adjusted annual operating income, up from $1.263 billion. Its adjusted margin was 12.9%. Comparable volume growth was only 0.1%, while price growth was 3.6%, so the revenue improvement should not be described as a large unit-demand acceleration. Brands such as Jimmy Dean and Hillshire Farm support distribution and consumer familiarity, but retailer bargaining power and household budgets still constrain pricing.

Pork contributed $228 million of adjusted operating income and International $180 million. These are useful sources of diversification, though smaller than Chicken and Prepared Foods. Starting in fiscal 2026, corporate expenses and amortization are reported outside the segments, and International is a separate reportable segment. The issuer recast prior periods. The five adjusted segment profits sum to $2.945 billion, from which $752 million of corporate expenses and $216 million of amortization are deducted to reach $1.977 billion of consolidated adjusted operating income. Valuing the segment sum without that deduction would materially overstate earnings.

How earnings become owner cash

Operating cash flow rose to $2.433 billion from $2.155 billion. Capital expenditure fell to $770 million from $978 million, producing reported free cash flow of $1.663 billion versus $1.177 billion. Lower investment explains part of the improvement. It is neither proof of permanently lower maintenance needs nor evidence that every dollar of capital spending would have been wasteful. Processing plants, refrigeration, transport and food-safety systems require continuing investment to remain competitive.

The annual cash-flow statement includes $1.398 billion of depreciation and amortization and $110 million of stock-based compensation. Receivables released $157 million, while inventory movements used $62 million. Accounts payable supplied $491 million; tax balances used $184 million, and other operating assets and liabilities used $358 million. The aggregate operating-balance movement was a modest source of cash, but the components show why one favorable quarter or year is not a permanent conversion rate.

Our model starts with segment operating profits and deducts corporate expenses and amortization. It then applies a 25% tax rate, adds depreciation and amortization, subtracts capital expenditure, and charges 10% of incremental sales as working capital. A sales decline creates a modeled working-capital release. That convention is an approximation, not a claim that every dollar of shrinking revenue releases exactly ten cents in practice. Inventory markdowns, payment terms and restructuring can change the relationship.

The first modeled capital budget is $1.250 billion, followed in the base by 2.4% of revenue. Depreciation and amortization begin at $1.300 billion and grow 2% annually. The model also reserves $100 million of future exceptional cash costs in each of the first two years and $50 million thereafter. Existing legal claims are deducted separately once because they are already liabilities. Future compensation remains an economic cost in margins. These unlevered model cash flows differ from Tyson's reported free-cash-flow measure, which includes interest and other operating cash items; the two should not be presented as identical.

Competitive strength and an independent market check

Tyson's scale, distribution access and branded prepared-food portfolio provide real operating advantages. Its exposure to multiple proteins can also support customer relationships when consumers switch between products. However, scale does not remove commodity risk, and brand economics do not apply equally to every segment. A large meat processor can have strong sales and weak returns if input availability, capacity utilization or customer bargaining power deteriorates.

Walmart represented 19.0% of fiscal 2026 consolidated sales, compared with 18.7% in fiscal 2025. The retailer also represented 18.6% of net accounts receivable at year-end. Concentration gives Tyson access to a very large customer base while making commercial terms and continuity important. We do not assume an immediate loss of that customer. Instead, the forecast's moderate growth and margin paths reflect a business that must continuously earn distribution and consumer preference.

Pilgrim's Pride offers an independent public-market reference, although it is not a perfect match. It reported September-quarter GAAP operating income of $191.503 million, down 61.1%. Using its November 13 closing price of $28.12, quarterly diluted shares of 239.033 million, carrying debt of $3.406 billion, cash of $936 million and minority interest of $12 million gives an enterprise-value reference near $9.204 billion. Dividing by four times quarterly GAAP operating income yields approximately 12.02 times.

Applying that observed multiple to the $1.925 billion midpoint of Tyson's fiscal 2027 adjusted operating-income guidance, then applying Tyson's cash and claims bridge, gives about $42.37 per share. This is below the main model's $51.47 because the main model explicitly credits later normalization. The comparison is not independent confirmation: it combines one weak poultry quarter with a different company's guided adjusted earnings, and portfolio, geography and adjustment policies differ. Both earnings measures retain compensation costs, and operating leases remain expenses. The reference helps test valuation scale; it is never averaged into the main value.

The main model and the dated return

The main model follows the economics of a cyclical meat processor with a branded-food franchise. It forecasts eight fiscal years, 2027 through 2034, with separate Beef, Pork, Chicken, Prepared Foods and International sales and operating margins. Intersegment sales are eliminated from consolidated revenue. It normalizes margins over four years instead of capitalizing the current trough forever or treating a recovery peak as permanent.

Base fiscal 2027 revenue is $52.950 billion: Beef $18.500 billion, Pork $6.000 billion, Chicken $17.400 billion, Prepared Foods $10.800 billion, International $2.450 billion and negative $2.200 billion of intersegment eliminations. Annual nominal growth rates thereafter are 1.5%, 1.5%, 2.5%, 3.0% and 2.5% for the operating segments; eliminations grow 2.5%. These are author assumptions, not a multiyear management forecast. Corporate expenses and amortization begin at $1.000 billion and increase 2% annually.

Base operating margins mature at 2.0% for Beef, 3.0% for Pork, 8.5% for Chicken, 13.0% for Prepared Foods and 6.5% for International. The bear combines weaker sales growth and lower margins; the bull combines stronger growth, better beef spreads and more durable chicken profitability. Every endpoint is derived from these cash-flow drivers. The model does not insert a selected equity value or apply an unexplained haircut to reach a desired stock call.

The discount rate is 8.5%. Terminal growth is 2.0%, with incremental returns on capital of 10%, 12% and 14% in the bear, base and bull. Terminal reinvestment is growth divided by the relevant return on capital. Base terminal value supplies 60.2% of operating value, making long-run economics a major source of uncertainty. Present common-equity values are $19.02, $51.47 and $80.77. Subjective 25%, 50% and 25% weights yield $50.68; these weights are not statistically calibrated.

Therefore, this is a mix of opinions from before the earnings release, observed on release day, not evidence that the Street had already incorporated the new results. Our $54.20 base estimate at October 2, 2027 is a broadly comparable future horizon; today's $51.47 fair value is a separate discounted estimate.

The reverse case asks which beef margin path would support the $62.75 reference price if all other base assumptions remain fixed. The answer is a uniform increase of approximately 2.09 percentage points throughout the forecast. Mature beef margin rises from 2.00% to 4.09%. The same controlled shift implies approximately $286 million of fiscal 2027 beef operating profit, above management's negative $300 million to positive $100 million range.

This is an interpretation of price, not a prediction that the market literally holds one common beef forecast. Higher chicken margins, stronger prepared-food growth, lower reinvestment or a lower required return can also support a higher price. Holding other drivers fixed isolates one disputed variable. Real-world operating changes may be correlated: better cattle availability can affect both volumes and spreads, while stronger consumer demand can change product mix and working capital.

The main operating sensitivity moves beef margins by one percentage point and chicken margins by one and a half points across the forecast. The strongest bull and bear arguments

The strongest bull case is operational rather than cosmetic. Tyson removes uneconomic beef capacity, preserves valuable customer relationships, improves the cost of the remaining network and benefits from better cattle availability over time. Prepared-food brands support margin and cash, while chicken remains more profitable than the cautious base assumes. Under those conditions, the cash formerly absorbed by beef losses can become available for shareholders without depending on perpetual revenue acceleration.

The bull model reaches a 3.5% mature beef margin and a 10.0% mature chicken margin, alongside stronger sales growth and prepared-food profitability. It produces $80.77 of present value. This upside requires several pieces to work together, including the investment budget. The model does not assume that closing capacity automatically raises value regardless of lost throughput, exit costs, customer service or the prices paid for livestock.

The bear accepts that restructuring may improve beef, but assumes chicken and pork pressures offset much of the benefit. Feed costs, industry supply and retailer resistance reduce spreads while fixed operating needs persist. Its mature beef margin is zero and chicken margin 6.5%. The resulting $19.02 value does not require insolvency. A large debt claim means a modest change in enterprise value can create a much larger percentage change in the value left for common shareholders.

Food safety, animal disease, labor availability, litigation, trade restrictions and weather can interrupt throughput or impose costs. We locate these risks in sales, margins, future cash leakage and financing assumptions, rather than adding repeated generic valuation discounts after the operating effects have already been modeled. Existing legal and benefit obligations remain separately visible. A particularly severe event could exceed the forecast allowance; scenario analysis cannot guarantee that every possible outcome lies within the published endpoints.

Evidence that changes the decision

The next earnings release should test whether beef is moving toward the annual negative $300 million to positive $100 million operating-income guide. A narrower loss accompanied by reasonable throughput and better operating cash would be stronger evidence than a higher beef selling price alone. We have not verified the date of the next fiscal 2027 release and do not invent one. The reporting event is the checkpoint; its precise calendar date remains open.

Chicken needs to be monitored against its $1.300 billion to $1.600 billion annual guide. If profitability holds near the upper end while beef improves, the combination could materially lift value. If chicken weakens while beef merely meets the lower end, apparent diversification may provide less protection than investors expect. Prepared Foods should preserve profitable pricing without prolonged volume erosion. Walmart concentration makes the stability of major customer relationships an additional qualitative check.

Cash discipline is equally important. Fiscal 2027 free cash flow needs to be assessed after the larger capital budget, not before it. Watch working capital, settlement payments, dividends and discretionary repurchases together. Low-end cash flow combined with aggressive buybacks would reduce flexibility. The $848 million of fiscal 2027 debt maturities creates an observable financing checkpoint; refinancing access and its cost matter even when liquidity is adequate at the start.

Our valuation would rise with a demonstrably better margin path, stronger cash conversion or investment that earns attractive incremental returns. A lower share price could also improve the prospective return without changing the business. Conversely, a lower price does not automatically improve value if operations deteriorate at the same time. We would revise the relevant drivers and claims together. The family's voting control is monitored through capital-allocation outcomes, not converted into an arbitrary second discount after the cash forecasts.

Definitions, limitations and reproducible inputs

The model uses US dollars in millions, except per-share values and explicitly labeled percentages. Reported figures come from Tyson's fiscal 2026 earnings release and Form 10-K. The release is dated November 16, 2026 and covers the period ended October 3. The company-issued availability announcement was timestamped 7:35 a.m. Eastern, or 6:35 a.m. Central; it establishes that results were available by that announcement, not the precise first minute the investor-relations page posted them.

The stock quote is the captured November 16 value of $62.75 at 12:12 p.m. Eastern. It is an intraday reference, not a closing price or a promise that viewers can transact at that amount. Pilgrim's comparison uses the prior November 13 close from the completed trading session before Tyson’s earnings release. The mismatched quote clocks and different reporting periods limit the relative valuation comparison. All market targets are context rather than primary evidence of company economics.

Fiscal cash flows are assigned to fiscal endpoint dates. The model uses the whole fiscal 2027 estimate even though part of the year had elapsed by the valuation date; no fresh interim cash balance was available. This is an explicit timing approximation. Principal is assumed refinanced, existing legal and tax claims are settled in the first horizon year, and no new common-equity financing or discretionary repurchases are assumed. Minimum operating cash is $500 million, and any modeled shortfall would require borrowing. The illustrated cases do not trigger that additional funding during the three-year horizon.

Existing restricted and performance awards are included at target on a conservative additional-share assumption, while options are aggregated at their weighted strike. This is not a security-by-security cap table. Future compensation is treated as economic cash expense, so the model does not add future grant dilution on top of the same compensation cost. The remaining service cost of outstanding awards is not deducted again as a separate cash charge. Changes in award outcomes or exercise behavior can change per-share results.

The linked sources and displayed forecast tables show the revenue, margin, investment and cash-flow assumptions behind each scenario. They do not eliminate judgment about margins, reinvestment, claims or the discount rate. These November 16 assumptions and the dated quote provide a baseline for later comparison with actual results. Operational forecast errors should be evaluated separately from stock-price returns, including dividends and corporate actions.
