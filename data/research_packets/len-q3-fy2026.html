Price and expectations

The relevant reference is a dated after-hours trade of $76.74 on September 16. The provider timestamp is 23:59:38 UTC. The regular-session close was $78.36 and occurred before this earnings release. The after-hours observation was therefore about 2.1% below that pre-release close. The regular-session decline of 2.14% is a separate move and must not be described as the reaction to results. Thin after-hours trading can change quickly; this packet fixes its valuation comparison to the recorded trade rather than implying a live price.

The stock's decline creates a tempting book-value argument. That argument needs two adjustments before it becomes useful. First, the Millrose spin-off removed assets and equity in 2025, so a chart of old and new book values is not a clean chart of operating destruction. Second, the remaining balance sheet includes goodwill and substantial deposits committed to land arrangements. A discount to headline book is not the same as a discount to cash available for distribution.

Our HOLD reflects that distinction. We estimate $75 of base equity value, with a $65–$80 central valuation range and much wider operating scenarios. The reference price is already near the base. There is room for management to earn a higher multiple by delivering better cash conversion, but an improvement in construction efficiency alone does not establish that outcome. A lower entry price would change the balance of risk and reward without changing the company's underlying quality.

The quarter and the reset

Lennar reported $8.046 billion of consolidated revenue, down 8.7% from the same quarter last year. Net earnings attributable to Lennar were $283.876 million, down 52.0%, and reported diluted EPS was $1.19 compared with $2.29. These comparisons use the current quarter first in the release's table, followed by the year-ago quarter. The two following columns are nine-month totals; they are not additional quarters. This header distinction matters because copying the wrong column can invert the trend.

Deliveries of 20,840 homes were within the previously stated 20,500–21,500 range. New orders fell to 20,879, compared with 23,004 a year ago. Deliveries and new orders were almost balanced in absolute units, which supports even-flow production, but that balance came at lower year-over-year demand and prices. The company reported a $372,000 delivery average selling price and approximately 12.0% incentives. The incentive percentage is a company disclosure, not an additional discount we subtract from already reported revenue.

The more consequential change was the full-year delivery target: 80,000–81,000 homes, down from 82,000–83,000. For Q4, management expects 22,000–23,000 deliveries, a $370,000–$380,000 average selling price and a 15.5%–16.0% gross margin. These are ranges, not point forecasts. Midpoint arithmetic can illustrate scale, but delivery mix, land costs and incentives determine the actual economics.

FMP records EPS actual of $1.23 against estimated $1.28, apparently aligning with the issuer's adjusted measure rather than its $1.19 GAAP EPS. Its $8.0 billion revenue actual is rounded; the filing's $8.046119 billion is authoritative. We show the distinction rather than blending adjusted actuals with GAAP commentary. The September 17 conference call was still ahead at the research cutoff, so this is a release-based analysis. Later explanations can refine the model; they are not invented here.

Better execution, weaker demand

Homebuilding dominates the economics, with $7.759 billion of segment revenue and $501.962 million of operating earnings. Financial Services is an important supporting business, but it is not a substitute for healthier homebuilding margins. Its reported operating earnings in the table were $130.316 million before the relevant noncontrolling-interest allocation. The narrative's approximately $129 million is after that allocation. Both are correct within their definitions; using one for a consolidated bridge and the other for an operating discussion requires a label.

Geography adds context that a national shortage thesis can obscure. The West delivered 4,529 homes versus 4,926 a year earlier, and its reported delivery average price was $571,000 versus $599,000. East deliveries rose modestly, while South Central and West deliveries declined. These regional unit and price figures come from the release's delivery table, which includes unconsolidated-entity activity and therefore does not simply reconcile to consolidated home-sales revenue. We retain that scope rather than forcing an artificial reconciliation.

Management's operational improvement is substantial: cycle time of 116 days, compared with 121 last quarter and 126 a year ago; construction cost per square foot down 1% sequentially and 6% year over year; and completed unsold homes reduced to 1.8 per community from 2.1. Those gains reduce construction time and improve scheduling. They can help protect earnings when pricing weakens. They do not eliminate land costs, sales incentives, marketing expense or the cash attached to land deposits.

Gross margin rose modestly from the previous quarter to 15.8%, but SG&A remained 9.2% of home-sales revenue. Year-ago gross margin was 17.5% and SG&A was 8.2%. The current business is therefore retaining less operating profit per home-sales dollar even as construction execution improves. The question for the next quarter is whether efficiency begins to reach owners through stronger margins and cash, rather than being passed to buyers to sustain volume.

Follow the complete profit bridge

The consolidated earnings bridge begins with the four operating segments, then deducts corporate general and administrative expense and the charitable-foundation contribution. Homebuilding earned $501.962 million, Financial Services $130.316 million, Multifamily lost $2.869 million and Lennar Other lost $83.607 million. Corporate expense of $137.883 million and the $20.840 million foundation contribution leave $387.079 million of pretax earnings. Tax expense was $101.592 million, and $1.611 million of earnings belonged to noncontrolling interests, leaving $283.876 million attributable to Lennar.

This bridge explains why segment operating earnings should not be casually called consolidated operating income or cash flow. Lennar's segment lines include equity-method and investment-related items. Homebuilding also capitalizes some interest into inventory and recognizes interest in cost of sales as homes close. The release's supplemental interest reconciliation is preserved separately. Adding every reported interest figure together would double count some economic costs.

The small gap between $1.19 GAAP EPS and $1.23 adjusted EPS does not mean unusual items were immaterial. Technology investments generated a $53.335 million pretax loss, while Financial Services included approximately $39 million of net one-time benefits, primarily a litigation-accrual reversal after a court judgment. Removing a loss increases adjusted earnings; removing a benefit reduces them. The two directions largely offset. The source reports rounded adjusted net earnings of $294 million. We retain the issuer's adjusted outcome without manufacturing a precise tax allocation from rounded narrative figures.

The same discipline applies to tax and shares. Dividing consolidated tax by consolidated pretax earnings produces approximately 26.25%; the narrative reports an overall 26.4% rate using its presentation. We do not silently replace one definition with the other. Reported EPS also uses the company's allocation method, so attributable net income divided by a selected average-share count is not a reliable substitute for the disclosed EPS. Our models label their denominator and use the published EPS series.

Nine earnings-quality checks

Quality here means understanding repeatability and conversion, not issuing a binary accounting accusation. The issuer's audited financial statements and effective internal-control opinion are useful foundations. They do not guarantee future economics. Our concern is the relationship among lower profit, investment marks, working capital and capital returns. The completed Millrose exchange is a comparability event: asset distributions and noncash share retirement should be separated from current operating performance.

Stock compensation is real but is not the central explanation for the cash shortfall. The latest complete fiscal-year disclosure is $163.494 million for FY2025. The latest interim cash-flow statement shows $88.648 million for the six months through May 2026. The Q3 release does not supply a full nine-month cash-flow statement or a new standalone SBC figure. We mark that limitation rather than repeating the half-year figure as if it covered nine months.

Receivable days and inventory days are deliberately simple diagnostic proxies. Dividing the current homebuilding receivable balance by homebuilding quarterly revenue and multiplying by 91 yields about 10.8 days; balances can include other homebuilding receivables and the ratio uses a quarter-end balance, so it is not a precise homebuyer collection-period measure. Owned homebuilding inventory divided by quarterly home-sales cost times 91 is approximately 161 days. A homebuilder's land and construction cycle is unlike a retailer's inventory cycle, and land deposits sit outside this ratio. Direction and definitions matter more than a universal good-or-bad threshold.

The strongest cash evidence is less flattering than headline earnings. FY2025 operating cash was $216.812 million versus $2.108 billion of consolidated net earnings. Deposits and pre-acquisition costs consumed $1.550 billion in that year. For the first half of FY2026, operations consumed $717.885 million, including $1.089 billion of inventory cash use and $554.766 million of deposit cash use. Seasonality means this does not establish the full-year result, but it makes cash conversion a necessary test of the recovery thesis.

Land-light, cash-heavy

The key distinction is between owning fewer land parcels and employing less cash. Lennar says fewer than 2.5% of roughly 488,000 owned and controlled homesites are owned on its balance sheet. That is a major operating-model change. It does not mean the other homesites are free, or that cash cannot be lost. Land-option deposits and pre-acquisition costs are real assets with real cancellation and recovery risks. Construction inventory remains on Lennar's books while homes are being built and sold.

At August 31, owned homebuilding inventory was $11.535 billion, up $1.614 billion from November 30, 2025. Deposits and pre-acquisition costs were $7.327 billion, up $943.560 million. Homebuilding cash fell $2.291 billion to $1.150 billion. These are balance-sheet changes, not a complete cash-flow reconciliation. Debt repayments, buybacks, dividends, acquisitions, distributions and noncash adjustments also matter. The missing Q3 cash-flow statement prevents us from attributing every dollar of the cash decline to inventory or deposits.

Homebuilding debt was $4.297 billion, producing computed homebuilding net debt of $3.147 billion after deducting homebuilding cash only. This definition does not mix mortgage-warehouse financing with unrelated consolidated cash. Financial Services has its own assets and liabilities, much of which supports loan activity. A mechanical enterprise-value calculation that subtracts every financing liability without considering the matching assets can misstate the equity economics. Our principal valuation routes are equity routes, so we do not subtract homebuilding debt again from an EPS-derived value.

Capital returns are another use of cash. Lennar repurchased three million shares for $256 million during Q3 and redeemed $400 million of senior notes. It also ended the quarter with $650 million drawn on its revolving facility. That combination does not automatically indicate distress, but it raises the standard for evaluating buybacks. Repurchasing below conservative value can help remaining owners; repurchasing while deposits grow and cash conversion weakens can also reduce flexibility. We want capital returns judged alongside the entire funding picture.

Three equity routes, one cautious call

The first route is normalized earnings. We assume $6.50 of sustainable annual EPS and apply a 12-times equity multiple, producing $78.00 per share. The EPS assumption is not current annualized quarterly earnings and is not company guidance. It assumes some normalization after weak FY2026 conditions, roughly consistent with the scale of the provider's FY2027 consensus EPS. Twelve times is our cyclical equity multiple, chosen to avoid paying a growth-stock valuation for earnings that remain sensitive to affordability, land costs and incentives.

The second route starts with tangible book. Reported stockholders' equity of $21.559 billion less $3.442 billion of goodwill leaves $18.117 billion. Dividing by 237.756 million latest-quarter weighted-average shares yields $76.20 per proxy share. Applying one times that amount gives the same value. This is a valuation anchor, not liquidation proceeds. Inventory and deposits may not realize their carrying amounts under stress. The denominator is a clearly labeled weighted-average proxy, not a claim about exact shares outstanding on the quote date. FMP's 248.295 million outstanding-share field differs and would reduce this anchor; we do not conceal that sensitivity.

The third route discounts cash available to equity. We assume $5.50 per share next year, grow it 4% annually for five explicit years, use an 11% cost of equity and 2% terminal growth. The result is $65.12. The explicit flows are $5.50, $5.72, $5.95, $6.19 and $6.43. This model assumes a substantial recovery from recent cash consumption; it is not derived by calling accounting earnings free cash. Because these are equity cash flows after financing effects, there is no second debt subtraction. Using enterprise cash flow would require a different capital bridge.

The three routes span approximately $65–$78. We round the central band to $65–$80 and choose $75 as the base, with High uncertainty. At $76.74, a 12-times multiple requires about $6.40 EPS. The market is therefore already paying for a meaningful recovery from the current run rate. Our $60 potential BUY threshold represents a 20% discount to base value, subject to the operating thesis remaining intact. Price alone is insufficient if future disclosures invalidate the cash-recovery assumptions.

Grade what management controls

Management deserves credit for the measured construction improvements. Shorter cycle time, lower direct construction cost and less finished unsold inventory reduce operational waste. Lennar's even-flow approach aims to keep starts and sales balanced so trade partners can plan work consistently. That is a coherent response to a difficult market. It does not give management control over mortgage affordability or guarantee that the savings remain with shareholders rather than being used to support demand.

The delivery record is mixed. Q3 deliveries were inside the stated range, so calling the quarter a wholesale operational miss would be unfair. The full-year target nevertheless moved lower as market conditions deteriorated. This distinction separates execution against a near-term range from the reliability of a larger annual outlook. The next review should score both, alongside margins and cash. A company can hit unit targets and still disappoint owners if incentives or land costs absorb the profit.

Capital allocation is the area requiring the closest scrutiny. The Millrose separation reduced owned land exposure, while the subsequent exchange retired shares without a cash purchase. Those completed transactions are economically different from current cash buybacks. FY2025 cash repurchases and withholding payments in the cash-flow statement also differ from the narrower open-market repurchase headline. We keep the categories separate. Otherwise a reader can mistake noncash retirement for operating cash generation or overstate the cash spent on the public buyback program.

Governance remains relevant to valuation. Class A carries one vote per share and Class B ten; the FY2025 annual report states Stuart Miller controlled approximately 42% of voting power at that year-end. Economic ownership and voting influence are different questions. We do not apply an arbitrary governance discount, but minority investors should understand the control structure. Our questions for management focus on deposit returns, the cash needed for controlled lots, buyback priorities, the repeatability of cost savings and the conditions required to restore growth without further incentives.

The strongest bull and bear cases

The bull case begins with housing supply rather than near-term mortgage rates. If underlying household demand remains unmet, a scaled builder capable of delivering homes quickly and at attainable prices can gain share. Lennar's lower direct construction costs and shorter cycle times are already observable. An improvement in financing affordability could allow those gains to show up in margins rather than being entirely absorbed by price support. Under that case, higher normalized EPS and a less skeptical equity multiple can reinforce each other.

The bear case is that volume stability has become expensive to maintain. Incentives, base-price reductions and rising marketing expense support unit sales while reducing profit per dollar. Deposits and partially built homes still require capital, even when the land itself is held elsewhere. If demand weakens further, the company may face a choice among slower production, lower prices, deposit losses and reduced capital returns. The ability to control lots is valuable optionality, but walking away from an option can destroy the deposit rather than preserve its carrying value.

Other risks include regional demand divergence, higher land costs, tariffs or supply disruption, trade-partner availability, investment marks and litigation outcomes. Mortgage operations introduce credit and financing complexity that requires separate analysis. The balance sheet has resources, and the reviewed source does not suggest that a liquidity crisis is the base case. The concern is the return earned on capital and the price paid for that return. Those can disappoint well before solvency becomes an issue.

Our position sits between the cases. We recognize tangible execution gains and avoid extrapolating weak quarterly earnings forever. We also avoid using old peak margins to justify a quick rerating. The probability-weighted scenario value equals $75, but that arithmetic should not disguise the width of possible outcomes: $50 in the bear case and $105 in the bull case. High uncertainty means sizing the required price cushion to those outcomes and revisiting the assumptions when cash-flow evidence arrives.

Source tables and method

This packet uses nine quarterly earnings releases from Q3 FY2024 through Q3 FY2026 and the FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 annual reports. The full raw filings, extracted tables, URLs, fetch timestamps and SHA256 hashes are retained with the research dataset. Quarterly income figures use the discrete three-month columns. Balance-sheet figures use the stated quarter-end snapshot. Financial amounts in the dataset are USD millions unless explicitly labeled as per-share amounts, homes, percentages or days.

The annual cash table reports the latest annual filing's FY2025, FY2024 and FY2023 cash-flow columns. Its free-cash-flow proxy is operating cash less net additions to operating properties and equipment. That source line is not gross capital spending and does not include every investment or distribution, so the proxy is not claimed to be a comprehensive distributable-cash measure. FY2025 also includes the Millrose separation, making casual comparisons with the old business structure hazardous. Employee counts are from each annual report: 12,284 in FY2023, 13,265 in FY2024 and 12,532 in FY2025.

Valuation calculations are reproduced in the episode's source-bound Python data preparation. Model assumptions are distinguished from reported facts. Share-based denominators deserve special attention: 237.756 million is the latest quarterly weighted average, used as an explicitly labeled proxy for book-value and cash-flow illustrations. It is neither the provider's 248.295 million outstanding-share estimate nor a newly verified point-in-time cover-page count. The proxy makes the calculation reviewable and exposes its sensitivity rather than implying nonexistent precision.

Reported adjusted EPS varies by quarter because the excluded items vary. The underlying source narratives and full financial tables are retained; there is no standardized invented adjusted operating-income series.
