A useful business with a narrow cushion

Our call is HOLD, with conviction of 2 out of 5, at the retained $37.35 ordinary-share quote on November 18, 2026, at 11:42 a.m. Eastern. The main cash-flow model estimates present value of $40.86 per share. Its conditional bear and bull cases are $11.76 and $92.60. Those endpoints are not a forecast trading range, a statistical confidence interval or a worst-case floor. A serious legal, payment or operating disruption could produce an outcome below the modeled bear.

Global-e offers a useful service: it helps merchants sell across borders without independently building every checkout, payment, tax, compliance and delivery capability. Its growing merchant base and Shopify distribution relationship can support attractive service economics. Passport expands the offering into logistics services beyond the traditional merchant-of-record model. The investment question is whether those opportunities produce enough cash per share after delivery costs, integration, taxes and employee claims to justify the price.

The base annualized owner return is 12.4% through December 2031, including modeled equivalent cash distributions. That only narrowly exceeds our 12% purchase hurdle. A 9.4% gap between present value and the quote is a different measure from a dated annual return. The former does not say when value will be recognized; the latter depends on both the future operating path and residual valuation. Neither is a promised outcome.

The independent current-revenue comparison produces a more generous $40.12–$55.88 range. We keep it separate because its transaction and platform references have important comparability limits. Subjective 25% bear, 50% base and 25% bull weights produce $46.52, but we do not replace the operating judgment with that weighted number. Strong growth, a useful product and a wide bull scenario do not eliminate the need for a reasonable entry price.

The print: growth, mix and a tax benefit

Third-quarter revenue rose 42% to $314.073 million from $220.779 million. Merchant-of-record gross merchandise value rose 35% to $2.042 billion. Service fees were $133.970 million, compared with $103.455 million, while fulfillment revenue increased to $180.103 million from $117.324 million. The faster fulfillment growth matters because delivery revenue carries a different cost structure from platform services. Passport was consolidated for the full quarter after closing on July 1.

GAAP gross profit was $133.905 million. Adjusted gross profit was $140.613 million, or 44.8% of revenue, compared with 46.3% a year earlier. A lower gross margin alongside faster revenue growth is not automatically evidence of deteriorating unit economics; product and acquisition mix can explain part of it. It does mean that the headline revenue percentage should not be used as a substitute for a margin and cash-flow analysis.

GAAP operating profit was $32.070 million after research and development of $36.062 million, selling and marketing of $41.111 million and general and administrative expense of $24.662 million. Net income reached $81.059 million, but included a $49.027 million noncash release of a tax valuation allowance. The full reported tax benefit was $50.947 million. This accounting change does not represent another $49 million collected from merchants, and it is not repeated in the forecast.

Our analytical operating reference adds $13.794 million of acquisition-related amortization to GAAP operating profit, giving $45.864 million. Stock compensation, transaction expenses and the quarter’s contingent-compensation expense remain costs in that reference. The adjustment is a starting point for understanding operations, not a claim that the entire amount is distributable or that acquired technology and customer relationships require no continuing investment.

A large holiday quarter remains ahead

Management guides fourth-quarter revenue to $464 million–$488 million and adjusted EBITDA to $113.5 million–$123.5 million. The GMV range is $3.085 billion–$3.245 billion. For the full year, revenue guidance is $1.329 billion–$1.353 billion, GMV is $8.957 billion–$9.117 billion and adjusted EBITDA is $290 million–$300 million. We use the $9.037 billion full-year GMV midpoint as the starting scale for the operating model.

These are management ranges for 2026, not a numerical 2027 forecast. The holiday period is materially larger than the reported third quarter. Merchant demand, shipping capacity, returns, currency and policy changes can influence both volume and margins during that period. A higher full-year midpoint is useful evidence, but it does not guarantee that every operating component has improved or that the acquisition will generate its expected return.

The forecast deliberately separates reported results, guidance and author assumptions. Our base 2027 merchant-volume growth is 24%, made up of an assumed 18-point contribution from existing merchants and 6 points from new merchants. That decomposition is not an issuer-disclosed current retention statistic. It is an analytical way to connect customer adoption to future volume, informed by the annual report’s separate discussion of existing and newly added merchants.

The return model includes only the remaining 43 days after the November 18 valuation date through December 31. It prorates normalized fourth-quarter cash from the guidance midpoint. Holiday seasonality makes uniform prorating approximate; removing the remaining stub cash altogether lowers present base value from $40.86 to $40.65. Unreported cash generated before the valuation date is not added to the dated September balance sheet.

What a merchant buys from Global-e

Global-e combines localized checkout, payment processing, tax and duty handling, fraud-related capabilities and delivery services. In its merchant-of-record business, it assumes responsibilities that would otherwise require a merchant to manage multiple providers and jurisdictions. A successful implementation can reduce friction for international shoppers while letting a brand concentrate on its products and customers. The platform’s value therefore depends on both merchant adoption and the quality of the shopper experience.

GMV is a volume measure, not Global-e’s own revenue or cash. It includes transaction components such as product value, taxes, duties and shipping under the company’s definition. Service fees and fulfillment charges monetize parts of that activity, but their revenue yields and cost structures differ. The 2025 annual report reports $451.205 million of service revenue and approximately $125.3 million of related cost, versus $510.990 million of fulfillment revenue and approximately $400.7 million of cost. Those imply roughly 72% and 22% gross margins for that historical year.

The annual report identifies 1,547 enterprise merchants at the end of 2025. Its largest merchant represented 8.5% of annual GMV. These are dated concentration indicators, not a claimed current customer count. New launches can add volume while existing merchants face weaker consumer demand, and a large merchant’s operating performance can matter even without a formal contract loss. Merchant counts alone cannot establish the growth rate of productive transaction volume.

Our base model therefore uses separate existing-merchant expansion and new-merchant contributions, fading total growth from 24% initially to 4% by 2036. It does not manufacture a current order count, average order value or retention rate that the quarter does not disclose. More granular future cohort information would help distinguish broad adoption from concentration, pricing and acquisition effects.

Shopify is an advantage and a dependency

Shopify distribution can help Global-e reach merchants efficiently through Managed Markets and related services. Management describes stronger growth in the newer offering and reports conversion improvements for merchants using specified pricing features. Such evidence is encouraging, but it should be attributed to the relevant participating users. It is not a guaranteed conversion improvement for every merchant, nor does it establish that all incremental revenue carries the same margin.

The relationship also creates dependence on a commercially powerful partner. Referral economics, product design, merchant access and future agreements can affect the value that Global-e retains. The model leaves ongoing partner-related costs in selling and operating expenses. It does not assume that a historical noncash charge ending means the distribution relationship has become permanently free.

Commercial-agreement assets were zero at September 30 after historical amortization. The annual notes describe Shopify warrants granted in earlier years and confirm that the specified 2021 and 2022 grants were vested and exercised by the dates disclosed. The additional 738,081 milestone warrants were exercised during 2023 and 2024. We do not add those historical warrants again as new claims on current holders. Equally, the expiry of their accounting amortization should not be confused with a new cash saving in the quarter.

Competition includes merchants building their own capabilities and alternative cross-border service providers, as well as platforms that can bundle more of the workflow. Changes in tariffs, de minimis rules, customs procedures, fraud or payment acceptance can raise the value of Global-e’s service while also raising the cost and difficulty of providing it. Our scenarios allow these forces to affect service yield, fulfillment margins and overhead rather than assuming every regulatory complication is automatically beneficial.

Passport: paid consideration and remaining risk

The Passport acquisition closed on July 1, 2026. The company describes $350 million of upfront consideration, approximately half in cash and half in Global-e ordinary shares, plus up to $75 million of contingent consideration based on 2026 performance. The closing transaction and the remaining contingent maximum are different obligations. We do not deduct the completed cash price again from September cash or add the approximate closing equity consideration a second time to a share average that already includes acquisition shares.

Passport extends the offering to logistics services that do not always use the merchant-of-record model. The announced expectation of approximately $100 million of calendar 2026 revenue is a dated acquisition expectation, not independently verified full-year revenue or a disclosed Q3 segment result. Management has discussed integration and future benefits, but the retained release does not supply enough separate information to reconstruct every acquired revenue and cost stream.

The model assigns $90 million of 2027 revenue to non-merchant-of-record logistics in the base. This is an author allocation, not a reported segment. Related GMV is not included in Global-e’s reported GMV, so using total revenue divided by GMV as one uniform platform yield would mix different economic activities. Service and merchant fulfillment revenue instead follow modeled GMV, while non-MoR logistics has its own growth and margin assumptions.

The $75 million maximum is reserved as a conservative cash-equivalent claim, without asserting that it is all due or necessarily cash-settled. Its current accounting fair value and location are not separately rolled forward in the compact release. We explicitly test whether the assumed amount has already been removed from accrued operating liabilities. This disclosure is important: acquisition consideration and employee compensation awards should not be combined into a single unexplained dilution or debt number.

Cash conversion needs a full-year view

Third-quarter operating cash flow was $68.071 million. After $1.649 million of physical capital expenditure, the company’s free-cash-flow measure was $66.422 million. For the first nine months, operating cash flow was only $69.155 million and physical capex was $2.462 million, giving $66.693 million of free cash flow. Nearly all of the nine-month figure arrived in this quarter, which makes a simple quarterly annualization particularly misleading.

Receivables, prepayments, taxes, payables and merchant settlement timing influence reported operating cash. The quarter included a $41.707 million cash use in prepaid expenses and other assets, alongside offsetting movements elsewhere. These movements do not make reported cash unreal; they mean that an investor should distinguish cash earned over a business cycle from cash temporarily released or absorbed by operating balances.

Our model retains economic stock compensation in operating costs. It adds back ordinary physical depreciation and deducts physical capex, while including capitalized contract costs in the operating working-capital base. The base physical capex allowance is 0.6% of revenue versus ordinary depreciation of 0.3%. These are forecast assumptions, not a claim that the recent very low quarterly capex ratio is permanently sufficient for a growing platform.

Acquisition-related amortization is excluded from the analytical operating margin, but research, customer acquisition, compensation and ongoing investment remain. The base cash tax rate is 21%, with 23% in the bear and 19% in the bull. The noncash valuation-allowance release does not recur. We do not separately capitalize investment income in operating earnings and then also add the associated financial assets without recognizing that duplication.

Unrestricted liquidity versus merchant money

September unrestricted cash of $147.084 million, short-term deposits of $117.871 million and marketable securities of $81.660 million total $346.615 million. The base recognizes securities at 99% of carrying value and retains $100 million as operating liquidity. That reserve is an author judgment about the needs of a growing, seasonal and internationally exposed business, not a claim that the company has legally restricted exactly that amount.

Merchant-funds receivables, including cash in relevant bank accounts, were $178.943 million. The matching merchant-funds payable was also $178.943 million. We exclude both from excess financial assets and selected operating working capital. The cash-flow statement’s ending cash figure includes restricted amounts associated with merchant activity. Adding that entire number to unrestricted balance-sheet cash would overstate shareholders’ financial assets.

The compact balance sheet does not separately report an interest-bearing financial borrowing line. This is different from saying that the company has no obligations: merchant payments, leases, operating accruals and acquisition claims remain material. We do not deduct $24.218 million of operating lease liabilities separately because rent remains in the forecast and we take no lease-depreciation addback. This is an approximate operating-lease treatment, not proof that cash rent and accounting expense match perfectly in each period.

The $13.565 million long-term investments and other-assets bucket receives no separate valuation credit because its composition and realizability are not fully established here. Deferred-tax assets also receive no separate cash credit. Goodwill and acquired intangibles are not added on top of the operating value; the modeled business must earn the returns that justify those accounting balances. Net financial assets and remaining claims add only about $185.3 million in the base, including hypothetical option exercise proceeds.

Acquisition claims and working-capital overlap

Selected starting working capital is negative $51.066 million. It comprises $102.126 million of receivables, $162.849 million of prepaids and other current assets and $4.019 million of noncurrent capitalized contract costs, less $94.208 million of payables and $300.852 million of accrued and other current liabilities, with an assumed $75 million acquisition claim removed from that last bucket. The claim is then deducted separately in the equity bridge.

The location and actual fair value of Passport’s remaining contingent consideration are not proven by the compact quarter-end disclosure. The removal is therefore an explicit modeling assumption. Holding the full $75 million claim deduction fixed, assuming none of it is embedded in operating accruals reduces value to $40.47; assuming half is embedded gives $40.66; the main full-overlap treatment gives $40.86. A no-claim, no-accrual-removal case gives $40.90. These combinations expose the accounting overlap rather than concealing it.

We separately reserve a dated $1.415 million legacy other-long-term liability proxy from December 2025. September’s corresponding category was $5.739 million, but its composition may include acquisition-related items already covered by the maximum reserve. This is a limitation, not a verified liability roll-forward. Future detailed notes should replace these proxies with identified obligations, settlement forms and current fair values.

Customer advances, indirect tax balances and ordinary accruals are operating funding, with their future service economics captured in revenue, cost and working-capital movements. The base working-capital ratio begins at negative 3% of 2027 revenue and fades to zero by 2031. Only annual changes enter FCFF. We do not both deduct the same advances as financial debt and model their eventual delivery or reversal through operations.

The share bridge is explicitly dated

The quarter reports 166.010068 million weighted-average basic ordinary shares and 172.214060 million weighted diluted shares. These are period averages, not a verified November share register. We use the basic average as a dated starting proxy. The market-data provider’s separate estimated share count is not substituted as if it were an issuer-confirmed capitalization table.

December 2025 notes report 5.709184 million options, all exercisable at a weighted average strike of $2.78. We model hypothetical immediate exercise, adding both those shares and approximately $15.872 million of strike proceeds once. The resulting denominator is 171.719252 million shares. All modeled scenarios remain above the weighted strike. Actual strikes and exercise timing vary, so this is an economic approximation rather than a grant-by-grant option valuation.

Later exercises could already overlap the Q3 basic proxy. Removing every dated option claim and its associated strike proceeds raises base value to $42.17. A 2% higher basic denominator produces $40.08, while a 2% lower denominator produces $41.66. These sensitivities do not establish the actual current share count; they show the scale of the disclosed uncertainty and prevent false precision.

December’s 2.299451 million unvested RSUs and $63.097 million of unrecognized compensation relate to future service in that dated schedule. Future economic compensation remains in operating expenses. We do not additionally allocate all unvested service awards as fully earned claims while charging that service expense, and we do not credit future new grants as an asset. Updated award notes would allow a more exact reconciliation of already-earned claims, subsequent settlements and remaining service costs.

Repurchases spend the cash they return

Global-e repurchased $53.426 million of shares in the quarter and $180.370 million over nine months. Those completed purchases already affect reported cash and share averages. The remaining September authorization was $447 million. An authorization permits purchases; it is neither a committed debt payment nor an additional asset available to common holders.

Repurchases can improve ownership per remaining share when executed below underlying value, but they can also offset employee issuance without creating the appearance of net shrinkage. Assessing their effectiveness requires a complete bridge between cash spent, average purchase price, awards settled, acquisition shares issued and the current share count. The compact release does not provide every element needed for a precise November reconstruction.

Our future-return model uses equivalent cash distributions rather than forecasting another round of buybacks. This keeps the denominator and cash bridge consistent. It does not deduct shares for an assumed repurchase, retain the same cash as a financial asset and award shareholders both benefits. The hypothetical option exercise cash is included once at the outset and remains within the financial bridge under the modeled constant-balance convention.

The retained operating reserve also remains constant in nominal dollars in the return calculation. If future scale requires a larger reserve, less cash would be available to distribute than modeled. Conversely, releasing an unnecessarily large reserve could create extra distributions. These are capital-allocation choices, not automatic consequences of higher revenue. Our hold judgment is deliberately less confident than the most favorable outcome from the repurchase authorization might suggest.

The main model follows economic activity

The base starts with $9.037 billion of guided 2026 GMV and grows that volume 24% in 2027. Service revenue uses a 6.55% initial yield and merchant-of-record fulfillment uses 7.65%. Separately modeled non-MoR revenue starts at $90 million. This produces $1.681 billion of 2027 revenue, rather than applying a single revenue multiple or treating all shipping activity as high-margin software.

Initial gross margins are 75% for service, 22% for merchant fulfillment and 20% for non-MoR logistics. Research, selling and administration together absorb 27.8% of total revenue initially. The combined result is $289.7 million of operating profit, a 17.2% margin, after economic compensation. Cash taxes, depreciation, capex and working-capital changes lead to $223.2 million of FCFF in 2027.

By 2036, total GMV growth has faded to 4%, service yield to 6.3% and merchant fulfillment yield to 7.3%. The base service gross margin reaches 77%, fulfillment reaches 24% and logistics reaches 23%. Operating-cost ratios decline with scale, producing $4.759 billion of revenue and a 24.5% operating margin. These are consequential assumptions: the model requires both growth and operating leverage, not simply continuation of the latest quarter.

The explicit cash flows and terminal period are discounted at 10.5%. Terminal growth is 2.5%, supported by an assumed 25% incremental return on capital. Terminal cash equals next-year after-tax operating profit multiplied by one minus growth divided by incremental return on capital. This terminal reinvestment requirement replaces the explicit forecast rules rather than being added on top. Terminal value contributes 56.6% of enterprise present value, so the duration and durability assumptions remain central.

Scenarios describe different operating worlds

The bear starts with 14% GMV growth and fades to 2%. Service pricing and gross margins are weaker, delivery economics are less favorable and overhead absorbs more revenue. A 12% discount rate, 2% terminal growth and 15% incremental return on capital compound the operating stress. Present value is $11.76, and the modeled annual owner return from the dated quote is approximately negative 13.2%. The bear is not a liquidation calculation and is not a guaranteed lower bound.

The bull begins with 32% GMV growth, fading to 6%, and combines stronger service economics with productive integration and more overhead leverage. It uses a 9.5% discount rate, 3% terminal growth and 30% incremental return on capital. Present value reaches $92.60, with a modeled annual owner return of approximately 32.1%. The result depends on several favorable conditions occurring together; it is not a target awarded simply for meeting the next quarter’s guidance.

The base lies between these operating worlds and is the principal decision reference. Our 25/50/25 scenario weights are subjective judgment aids. They are not estimated frequencies from a statistical model, and the resulting weighted present value of $46.52 should not be read as a promised expected price. A broad upside tail can lift a weighted value while the practical investment cushion in the central case remains small.

Risks are located explicitly in the model: merchant demand affects volume, partner terms affect yields and costs, logistics affects gross margin, integration affects overhead, and remaining claims affect the equity bridge. We avoid repeatedly subtracting generic risk discounts from cash flows that already embody those stresses. Some risks, including serious legal or operating interruption, are not adequately represented by smooth annual assumptions and remain outside the numerical scenario boundaries.

An independent comparison, with limits

The independent check uses current revenue rather than the DCF’s future operating profits. Trailing service revenue is $555.130 million, calculated as 2025 revenue plus the first nine months of 2026 less the first nine months of 2025. Trailing fulfillment revenue is $646.686 million on the same basis. These categories are valued separately because their gross economics differ substantially.

Passport’s $350 million upfront price divided by the acquisition announcement’s approximately $100 million calendar 2026 revenue expectation gives a 3.5-times reference. Including the full contingent maximum gives 4.25 times. That is a transaction reference, not a clean public trading multiple: it reflects control, potential synergies, a dated forecast and a business mix that does not exactly match Global-e’s fulfillment category. It can be too generous if the acquired economics or growth do not translate to the broader category.

For services, we select 8–12 times trailing revenue, below Shopify’s observed 12.53-times enterprise-value-to-revenue ratio. This is an author-selected range acknowledging different scale, partner dependence and revenue composition. It is not a claim that Shopify and Global-e have identical margins, accounting or growth. A platform comparator does not justify valuing every dollar of physical delivery revenue at the same multiple.

Applying the same financial-asset, claim and ownership bridge yields $40.12–$55.88 per share. The result is more generous than the main base and offers some support for the business franchise. Its weak point is comparability, so we do not average it into the cash-flow model to manufacture precision. If market multiples compress or Passport’s expected synergies disappoint, this check could be less reassuring even without an immediate change to reported revenue.

The quote already discounts healthy growth

The reverse calculation holds base service and fulfillment economics, logistics growth, tax, investment, claims and discount assumptions fixed. It solves for an equal additive change to annual existing-merchant growth that makes present value equal the $37.35 quote. The resulting initial total GMV growth is approximately 22.7%, compared with the base’s 24%.

This is not a prediction that investors collectively expect precisely 22.7%. Investors might instead use lower margins, a higher required return, different acquisition economics or a different current share count. The calculation provides one operating interpretation of the price. It shows that the quote already requires healthy growth under our other assumptions; it is not priced as if cross-border commerce has stopped expanding.

A combined three-percentage-point reduction in annual existing-merchant growth and three-point reduction in service gross margin lowers value to $31.26. With growth alone three points lower, value is $33.13. With service margin alone three points lower, value is $38.55. These are useful operating sensitivities because they show how a modest apparent valuation cushion can disappear without invoking the full bear combination.

Discount-rate and terminal-growth sensitivities are also displayed below, but they are not substitutes for the operating debate. Changing a discount rate can move a long-duration valuation substantially without explaining what merchants will buy, what delivery will cost or how much cash belongs to common holders. The central questions remain the quality of volume growth, the mix of service and fulfillment revenue and the cost of sustaining the platform.

Present value is not a dated future price

The base December 2031 residual price is $54.90 per share. That value represents remaining operating cash flows after the horizon plus the financial bridge, rather than today’s fair value grown at an arbitrary stock-return assumption. Modeled cash distributed before and at the horizon is included separately in the owner-return calculation. The resulting annualized return from $37.35 is 12.4% over approximately 5.12 years.

Our one-year November 2027 ex-distribution estimate is $44.80. The remaining 2026 distribution has already occurred by that future date and is therefore excluded from that price. Total investor wealth includes the distribution separately, with no assumed reinvestment. This distinction prevents the same cash from appearing both inside the future company value and as a payment already received by the holder.

The retrieved Street average is $51.38 from 13 analysts, with the latest listed table entry dated November 12. It is useful context but predates this release. Comparing that nominal future target directly with present intrinsic value would mix horizons. Even the one-year comparison remains imperfect because different analysts can use different forecast dates, methods and dividend assumptions. We do not present the aggregation as a new post-earnings consensus reaction.

The most useful future evidence is a clean acquired-versus-organic bridge, stronger service economics, integration benefits after costs and durable cash conversion through the seasonal cycle. Updated shares, employee awards and acquisition claims would reduce uncertainty in the equity allocation. A stronger business could still be an unattractive investment at too high a price; a lower entry price could improve returns without requiring the bull case. That distinction supports the present HOLD call.
