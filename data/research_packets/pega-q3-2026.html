Five things to know

  1. Cloud growth is only part of the contract story. Pega Cloud ACV rose 20%, but maintenance and subscription-license ACV fell. Total reported ACV growth was 6%, down from 17% at December 2025.
  2. The cash outlook deserves more weight than the adjusted EPS increase. Nine-month free cash flow fell 14.3% to $289.8M, and management says slower ACV may continue to hurt cash generation this year.
  3. Buybacks exceeded internally generated free cash. Cash-flow-statement program repurchases of $432.7 M plus $15.1 M dividends exceeded nine-month FCF by $158.0M. They reduced shares, but also drew on financial resources.
  4. The Appian dispute is not a settled liability or a closed issue. The old judgment was reversed. A January 2027 retrial remains pending, and an October 16 ruling permits an additional reasonable-royalty damages theory.
  5. Our prior BUY is not the new call. The current cash-based routes support a $28 central value versus a dated $34.83 indicative midpoint. We now rate PEGA SELL at 3/5 conviction, with very high uncertainty.

Mixing GAAP EPS of $0.08 with the adjusted consensus would create a misleading comparison.

Sequential revenue fell 6.5% from $420.716M. Subscription-license timing can make a quarter unusually strong or weak, so neither the sequential decline nor the cloud growth rate should be annualized alone. Nine-month revenue was $1.244B, essentially flat with last year. That longer comparison matters because it captures more of the contract-conversion cycle and prevents one revenue-recognition period from standing in for recurring demand.

The most useful question is what the quarter changes about future cash. Management identifies go-to-market execution problems and greater scrutiny of technology spending as impediments to ACV growth. It also says the third-quarter ACV addition was the strongest of the year. Both statements can be true: sequential improvement from a weak base does not establish a return to the growth rate embedded in earlier expectations. We require sustained total ACV improvement before paying for that recovery.

Business mix and contracts

Pega reports one operating and reportable segment. The following streams are revenue categories and their gross profits, not separately reported segment operating profits. Pega Cloud revenue increased to $218.925M, while maintenance, subscription licenses and consulting all declined from the year-earlier quarter. Cloud gross profit was $170.061M, a 77.7% gross margin. Consulting still lost $2.885 M at the gross-profit line, although that loss narrowed from $6.207M.

The migration can improve revenue visibility while temporarily obscuring the underlying demand trend. Cloud ACV reached $975.991M, up $160.621 M from a year earlier. Maintenance and subscription-license ACV together fell $64.238M, leaving only $96.383 M of net total ACV growth. Cloud expansion is therefore not wholly incremental to the company. Some of it replaces older arrangements, and the company must overcome those losses before the whole contract base accelerates.

Total ACV of $1.653B rose $33.375 M from June. The cloud portion is now 59.0% of total ACV. Backlog grew 14.5% to $2.009B, with $985.469 M expected within one year. Backlog is contractual future revenue, not cash already in the bank; ACV annualizes active contracts and is not reported revenue. Longer contract duration, migration, foreign exchange and the timing of billing can move these measures differently. We track all three rather than treating the highest growth rate as the single answer.

The profit bridge

Adjusted net income rose 8.3%, while adjusted EPS rose 16.7%. The difference is partly the shrinking diluted share count, down 8.4% year over year. Buybacks can improve per-share results even when the underlying earnings pool grows slowly, but they are not free. The correct analysis considers the cash used, the price paid and the remaining dilution from employee compensation. It does not award the full EPS increase to operating execution.

The $45.658 M gap between GAAP and adjusted net income includes $28.679 M of stock compensation, $13.272 M of legal fees, $16 M of settlement expense and smaller items, offset by tax and other adjustments. Some expenses may be unusual, but their exclusion is not evidence that shareholders will never bear them again. Legal spending has risen, and stock compensation remains part of how the company pays people. We therefore retain an explicit economic compensation charge in our cash valuation.

GAAP net income fell $29.272 M even though operating income rose $0.392M. Last year’s quarter had a $19.268 M tax benefit; this year recorded $3.686 M of tax expense, a $22.954 M adverse swing. Foreign-exchange income also fell $6.565M. This is why describing the entire GAAP earnings decline as an operating collapse would be wrong. Conversely, the roughly flat operating-income result is not proof of a strong turnaround. The bridge separates what actually changed.

Earnings quality

Cash conversion is positive, but its composition is important. Nine-month operating cash flow of $304.299 M includes $110.720 M of stock compensation and a $64.913 M contribution from changes in operating assets and liabilities. After capex, company-defined FCF is $289.825M. Subtracting the working-capital contribution leaves $224.912M; subtracting SBC from reported FCF instead gives $179.105M. These are different analytical lenses, not two adjustments to be casually combined into a new company-reported metric.

The receivables balance also requires a definition. Billed receivables are $130.121M, but current and long-term unbilled receivables add $230.595M. Dividing billed receivables by quarterly revenue and multiplying by 92 days gives a 30.4-day snapshot; including all receivables produces 84.4 days. Neither is a conventional average-balance DSO estimate. The distinction shows why a neat vendor receivables ratio can disguise long-duration contractual collection exposure. Contract assets of $56.858 M are disclosed separately and are not silently folded into those ratios.

The current quarter’s 7.3% SBC-to-revenue ratio is better than the year-earlier expense burden, but the trailing cash economics still depend on compensation policy. There is no inventory-cycle thesis for this software business, and no reported minority-interest leakage in the current statements. We mark those checks not applicable rather than manufacturing a ratio. Similarly, a tax benefit that enlarged a historical GAAP profit does not establish a sustainable future tax rate.

Its noncurrent operating-lease liability of $54.643 M is labeled as debt in the saved provider balance sheet; the filing instead identifies $69.025 M of total current and noncurrent operating leases. We disclose those obligations separately and retain lease expense in projected cash flow, avoiding an unexplained funded-debt deduction.

The revolver was increased to $200 M on October 16 and extended to October 2031. Borrowing capacity is not cash already owned, and it is not included in the valuation bridge. About 67.2% of reported cash and securities is held in foreign entities. We apply no unsupported repatriation haircut, but neither do we describe every dollar as immediately distributable excess cash. Operating needs, legal uncertainty, taxes and restrictions can affect actual availability.

Nine-month program repurchases were $432.700 M in the cash-flow statement, while the program note reports $432.199 M excluding its stated presentation difference. Valuation and implied expectations

Our central fair value is $28 per share, with a $14–$43 scenario band and $27.55 probability-weighted value. The three operating routes produce $26.29, $28.30 and $27.33; the central value rounds that cluster rather than implying a precision the assumptions cannot support. At $34.83, the central value is 19.6% lower. Very high uncertainty requires a 30% margin of safety before a BUY could be considered, implying a $19.60 price condition if the operating and legal thesis remains intact. A price decline alone does not satisfy the condition.

Route one discounts an analyst owner-cash sequence of $320M, $350M, $390M, $425 M and $460 M for 2027–2031 at 12.5%, with 3% terminal growth. The explicit cash present value is $1,355.490 M and terminal present value $2,767.635M. Adding $309.711 M cash and dividing by 168.642 M diluted shares gives $26.29. Forecast cash is after capex and an economic SBC charge. With no funded debt, we use it as an enterprise-style proxy; small cash interest remains in the historical starting point, which creates a modest upward bias. We disclose that approximation instead of pretending it is a fully unlevered forecast.

Route two applies 15 times trailing owner-cash proxy of $297.474M, adds cash and divides by diluted shares, giving $28.30. That proxy is reported trailing FCF of $442.237 M less $144.763M SBC. The multiple is our judgment for modest current contract growth and substantial uncertainty, not an observed peer median. Route three values total ACV of $1,653.309 M at 2.6 times, adds cash and divides by the same shares, giving $27.33. ACV is a revenue-like contract metric with no direct claim to profit; this route is a cross-check, not independent proof of value.

The quote implies approximately $5,873.801 M of equity value using our diluted denominator and $5,564.090 M after cash. Under the same 12.5% discount rate and 3% terminal growth, the price requires the $297.474 M starting owner-cash proxy to grow about 16.8% annually for five years. That is the market-implied analytical case, not a claim about investors’ private forecasts. Management’s $700M-plus 2028 company-FCF ambition is a different measure, before our SBC charge; treating it as owner cash would overstate comparability.

The litigation scenario is intentionally outside the base operating forecast. The bear illustration subtracts $350 M after applying 12 times to $200 M owner cash. The $350 M is not an estimate of probable damages: the company states that it cannot reasonably estimate possible damages, and the asserted claims are much larger. Scenario weights are subjective investment assumptions, not court probabilities. The sensitivity grid shows how ordinary discount-rate and terminal-growth choices alone alter value; it cannot make an unquantifiable legal outcome disappear.

Ranked risks and opposing cases

The Appian dispute is the largest discrete uncertainty. The original judgment was reversed and a new trial is scheduled for January 11, 2027. On October 16, the court allowed a reasonable-royalty theory seeking $284.6 M for accused marketing and sales activities and $1.36B for accused platform revisions. Those are asserted damages, not a final award or a booked payable. The filing says the company cannot reasonably estimate possible damages. The separate $16 M settlement accrual should not be misdescribed as a cap on Appian exposure.

The strongest bull case is that customers still need governed, reliable enterprise workflows even as model technology changes. Cloud adoption and backlog support that argument. Better go-to-market execution could restore growth while a lower share count magnifies per-share gains. Positive FCF and an undrawn revolver provide flexibility. If that operating progress arrives without a material legal payment, the bull scenario can justify a value above the current indicative quote.

The strongest bear case is that the AI opportunity changes purchasing behavior faster than Pega converts it into contracts. Cloud growth then mainly replaces legacy business, while compensation, legal costs and buybacks consume the cash that looks attractive before adjustments. Pricing a fast owner-cash recovery before total ACV accelerates leaves little room for disappointment. Our SELL is a valuation judgment under that uncertainty, not a prediction of insolvency or a statement that a legal loss is certain.

Data, sources and methods

All monetary figures in the research dataset retain their source units. Public tables generally use millions of U.S. dollars; per-share values use dollars. The saved dataset contains nine complete earnings-release table inventories, three annual-report table inventories, source hashes and the raw market snapshots. Quarterly income and balance-sheet extracts were cross-checked against the primary filings. We correct known provider classification errors rather than allowing convenient standardized fields to override the filed statement.

The June 2025 two-for-one split is reflected in historical comparisons. Original pre-split reports are retained, but share counts used in comparable series are restated; EPS rounded in later comparative releases takes precedence over simply dividing an already-rounded old EPS. The provider’s Q2 diluted share field used a six-month denominator, so the quarter-specific 171.765 M figure is used instead. These distinctions matter because a denominator error can change a valuation or make buybacks look more effective than they were.

Free cash flow means operating cash flow less property-and-equipment investment, following the company’s definition. Our owner-cash proxy further subtracts SBC as an economic compensation stress; it is not a second cash payment and is not GAAP. Cash and operating-lease liabilities are presented separately. The DCF, multiples, scenario probabilities and valuation thresholds are analyst assumptions. No court probability, transaction price, customer retention rate or post-release target revision has been invented to fill a missing field.

Annual workforce observations are 5,406 at January 31, 2024,5,443 at January 31, 2025 and 5,598 at January 30, 2026, from the respective annual reports.
