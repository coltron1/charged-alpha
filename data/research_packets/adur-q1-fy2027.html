Five things to know

  1. A positive bottom line can coexist with zero sales. The C$4.240M warrant gain more than offsets the C$3.496M loss before other items.
  2. The runway improved for a financing reason. Net share proceeds were C$30.281M; operating cash flow and capital spending together consumed C$3.244M.
  3. Technical progress is real but has a different denominator. A September 75-hour campaign processed about 758 kg. It is a subsequent operating test, not quarter revenue or a commercial contract.
  4. The timetable still contains execution risk. Management expects FOAK completion in early calendar 2028. Cost, permitting, uptime, licensing and customer economics remain separate tests.
  5. Price needs its own model. The dated $10.10 common share quote is far above the roughly $1.29 dated cash-minus-lease reference; that premium must be earned by future commercialization.

Commercialization: milestones need conversion

The financial statements identify one operating segment. We therefore do not invent plastics, bitumen or renewable-oil revenue segments, or assign a profit margin to a demonstration project. The strategy spans Hydrochemolytic technology applications, but a technology use case is not a separately reported business. Canada and the Netherlands appear in geographic asset disclosures; they do not provide a current-quarter revenue or segment-profit split that can support a sum-of-parts valuation.

Technical evidence improved. Management describes a September operating campaign lasting 75 hours and processing approximately 758 kilograms of polymer across several feedstock blends. This occurred after the August 31 quarter end. The earlier June campaign lasted 47 hours. These observations support progress in operating a pilot, yet their economic interpretation is limited: they do not establish annual commercial throughput, sustained customer uptime, license pricing, operating cost per tonne or a contracted royalty stream. A long run and a profitable industrial installation answer different questions.

The company’s proposed model emphasizes licensing, royalties and research services, while management continues to investigate alternatives. That can eventually reduce capital intensity relative to owning many processing plants. It does not eliminate the cost of proving the technology, engineering a first installation or funding commercialization. We treat the capital-light model as an intended destination. Applying a mature software-like margin today would move the desired endpoint into the starting financial statements.

The key transition is from evaluation to enforceable economics. At least one signed agreement should identify what the customer pays, who funds equipment, what performance guarantees apply and when revenue can be recognized. Nonbinding memoranda and confidential customer conversations are useful pipeline indicators, but they are not substitutes for those terms. The absence of a commercial agreement is a valuation risk to analyze; it is not an automatic reason to deny earnings coverage.

Profit bridge: the liability went down

Some warrants have US-dollar exercise prices while Aduro’s functional currency is Canadian dollars. Under the issuer’s accounting treatment, those instruments form a derivative financial liability rather than a fixed equity amount. The liability is remeasured through earnings. A decline in its estimated fair value creates income even though no customer paid for a product and no new cash arrived. This quarter’s C$4.240M gain is larger than the entire reported profit.

The gain is not fraudulent or an error; it is a consequence of the financing terms and IFRS classification. The analytical mistake would be capitalizing it as a recurring operating return. Removing only the derivative gain leaves a C$3.038M loss, still including interest and the small payable writeoff. The cleanest observable operating starting point is the separately disclosed loss before other items, supplemented by actual cash flow. We do not label that subtraction company-adjusted EPS or a forecast.

FY2026 illustrates the opposite direction: a C$9.980M derivative loss deepened the annual net loss to C$26.849M. Neither that annual mark nor this quarter’s reversal measures the commercial success of the technology. The same instrument can make an improving technical business look worse in earnings or make a pre-revenue business look profitable. This is why the packet keeps operating cost, financing, warrant dilution and the accounting liability in separate panels.

Cash: more runway, more shares

Cash rose to C$65.752M from C$38.298M. The increase was financed: net proceeds from common-share issuance were C$30.281M, more than the C$27.454M increase in cash. Operations, equipment purchases and leases consumed part of the proceeds, while interest receipts added cash. This is a stronger balance sheet than before the financing, but it is not evidence that the business has become self-funding. Cash raised from owners and cash earned from customers have different implications for a long-term return.

Quarter-end shares increased 15.0% year over year to 35.705M. The October 15 MD&A reports 35.777M outstanding, which we use for current per-share valuation. Cash per share must therefore be examined alongside the share count. A larger cash account can coexist with a smaller ownership percentage for continuing holders. Our illustrative 45M future-share denominator is an assumption about possible cumulative dilution, not the company’s current diluted EPS denominator.

Using the latest quarter’s OCF, capex and lease cash use unchanged, cash covers about 20.1 quarters. That arithmetic is intentionally labeled static and is not a runway forecast. The model ignores future project spending, commercial working capital, changing interest receipts and financing choices. Management states it has sufficient funds for the next twelve months, but that is a narrower assertion than funding the entire path to profitable commercialization. FOAK construction and licensing milestones still need a quantified capital plan.

The warrant liability deserves special care in enterprise-value arithmetic. Treating it as bank debt and also charging for all future dilution would risk counting the same financing burden twice. Our commercial model uses cash less lease liabilities, a separate cash-consumption reserve and a conservative future-share count without exercise proceeds. The accounting-capital cross-check uses reported book equity, where the warrant liability is already deducted. 30% at $1, 50% at $6 and 20% at $14 produce $6.10. These are editorial scenario probabilities, not empirically calibrated odds; this route tests whether the base hides an asymmetric failure outcome.

3

Accounting-capital downside cross-check

Current book common equity converts to about US$1.22 per latest disclosed share. Cash less all recorded liabilities is about US$1.02. Neither is a liquidation estimate, but both show how little of the quote rests on dated balance-sheet capital.

The first route is an end-state cash model for a precommercial company. It assigns no positive interim distributable cash flow, reserves US$20M for additional consumption, and places a 25× multiple on US$30M of normalized cash flow five years out. That multiple is generous for a business with no current sales; the 25% discount rate and 45M denominator recognize execution and dilution risk. These are author assumptions, not management guidance or borrowed analyst estimates. The purpose is to show what must eventually become true, not to claim a precise forecast.

The three routes are deliberately not presented as independent confirmations. Scenario values share the same commercialization thesis, and accounting capital does not price a successful technology license. The $6 anchor is the rounded conditional cash model, supported by a similar weighted scenario judgment; the low capital reference exposes the downside if commercial value fails to emerge. Averaging the three mechanically would hide their different meanings. Conviction is low and uncertainty very high because revenue, margins, capital requirements and adoption probabilities are not yet observable at scale.

Under the same assumptions, the current $10.10 price requires approximately US$52.29M of normalized annual FCF at the horizon, versus US$30M in our base. If a 20% cash margin applied, the base alone would imply US$150M of revenue. That margin is an illustration, not a forecast; royalty mixes could produce very different economics. Starting from no current-quarter revenue, the burden is substantial. Faster deployment, higher margins or less dilution could justify more value, while delays and project funding could justify much less.

Our 30% valuation margin of safety sets a $4.20 price threshold, but it is not an automatic buy order. We also require evidence of binding commercial economics and a credible funded implementation plan. A price decline caused by deteriorating feasibility would not make the same model safer. Conversely, a disclosed contract with attractive economics could justify raising the assumptions before the shares reach that threshold. We will change the model when the evidence changes rather than treating a low-confidence target as a fixed truth.

Management deserves credit for securing capital before the company ran out of room and for advancing the pilot beyond a simple laboratory claim. The challenge is to turn that financial and technical capacity into a repeatable customer proposition. A milestone-driven company can create value well before it reports large revenue, but only if each milestone reduces a specific uncertainty. More announcements without comparable operating metrics or commercial terms can leave investors with more activity and little additional ability to value the business.

We would ask five questions. What fraction of FOAK cost is now supported by firm supplier quotes? Which remaining permits control the critical path? What minimum commercial throughput, uptime and product specification must be achieved before a customer pays? How are license fees, royalties and performance guarantees allocated? Finally, how much of the present cash is committed to the plan, and what share count would management expect under a delayed scenario? Answers should be numeric, dated and reconcilable with cash expenditure.

The strongest bull case is that a technically differentiated platform earns high-margin royalties across many installations without Aduro financing every plant. In that outcome, early research revenue is a poor measure of long-term value, the cash raise buys enough time, and today’s dilution funds a much larger opportunity. The latest operating campaigns and institutional collaborations give that argument substance. A bearish analysis should recognize those facts rather than treating zero current revenue as proof that the technology has no value.

The strongest bear case is that technical validation does not translate into customer economics at commercial scale, while time and cash are spent proving the gap. Even a working process may face unfavorable feedstock, energy, logistics or customer-capital economics. Investors can suffer dilution before the company reaches the stage at which those issues become measurable. The present quote already embeds a large operating premium over dated capital. We land on SELL at the reference price because the commercial hurdle exceeds our conditional base, not because success is impossible.

These dates do not turn a forecast into a reported event. A fiscal period end is certain from the reporting calendar, but the distribution date of its earnings release is not yet confirmed. We will treat a new release as current only after checking the actual issuer document and filing, and will preserve the distinction between a conference-call time and the time results were distributed.

The next useful catalyst is one that closes an economic information gap. A signed customer agreement with pricing and performance terms can change the valuation more than a generic progress update.
