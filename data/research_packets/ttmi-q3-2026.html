The quarter’s largest individual bridge elements are stock compensation and the tax adjustment. Amortization reflects acquisition accounting, while derivative and foreign-exchange items can move in either direction. The sign of a hedge gain matters: the commodity item reduces adjusted income rather than increasing it. We retain every sign and reconcile the total exactly. A business doing repeated acquisitions can repeatedly incur expenses labeled acquisition-related. Excluding them may help compare a particular operating definition, but does not establish that owners will never bear those costs again.

The nine-quarter history makes the distinction visible. GAAP earnings and adjusted earnings both improve, so the whole advance is not merely a change in presentation. The size of the adjustment gap still affects valuation confidence. We avoid valuing the segment result, adding back corporate expenses, and then applying an equity multiple as if the whole amount belonged to common shareholders. Our earnings route instead starts with the issuer’s forward adjusted EPS and applies an explicit realization haircut; the cash routes then test whether that result could reasonably become owner cash.

A warning flag is a question to resolve, not an allegation of accounting misconduct. Contract assets represent revenue recognized before an unconditional billing right; a growing balance can be normal when production and customer milestones accelerate. What would improve confidence is collection evidence and a balance that grows no faster than the associated business over a comparable perimeter. The current expansion and acquisitions prevent a simple year-end-to-quarter comparison from proving a deterioration in credit quality.

Share compensation also deserves a two-sided treatment. It can align employees with growth and preserve cash during expansion, but it dilutes ownership unless the company spends cash offsetting it. Weighted diluted shares have risen, so a stable share-count assumption needs ongoing verification. We use 108M prospective diluted shares in the cash models, while the latest actual quarter averaged 107.699M. We do not treat the older 10-Q cover share count as interchangeable with diluted weighted shares. A buyback authorization by itself does not prove cash was spent or dilution was neutralized.

Our $95 is a rounded analytical anchor, not a precise appraisal. The terminal value contributes most of the cash model, and the mature starting cash level is substantially above current cash generation. This is why apparent agreement among the routes does not warrant high conviction. If capital expenditures remain elevated longer than expected or debt service consumes more cash, the equity cash base must fall. If cash conversion strengthens while growth remains durable, the haircut and required margin could be revisited. A valuation model should expose those dependencies, not conceal them behind a single target.

Cash-model timing: the DCF clock starts in FY2027 with $660M owner cash. It does not discount an additional waiting period for the company to mature into that cash level. This is a demanding forward assumption, not FY2026 actual cash or issuer cash guidance. A slower ramp would lower present value even if the same eventual cash level were achieved.

Capital allocation and accountability

Management is pursuing both manufacturing expansion and acquisitions. Those can be complementary: advanced electronics capacity supports growing demand, while RF and defense capabilities can broaden customer content. They also compete for cash, integration attention and execution capacity. The board’s allocation challenge is to make each incremental dollar earn a return after financing, stock compensation and the cost of delivering customer programs. Revenue scale alone does not answer that question.

Our management assessment is conditional. Successful acquisitions are measured by durable cash generation per diluted share and a credible deleveraging path, not merely adjusted accretion at announcement. We want management to explain the post-close debt and cash bridge, show how capacity spending translates into shipments and collections, and distinguish acquired growth from organic improvement when the information becomes available. These requests are specific enough to change the investment case when answered; generic confidence in long-term demand is not a substitute.

What can break the thesis

The bull case is substantive. Commercial segment profitability is rising, backlog is strong, and advanced electronics demand may reward suppliers with the right capabilities and manufacturing footprint. If expansion ramps efficiently, present investment can create future cash rather than represent permanent cash leakage. A&D offers a different customer base and program cycle, and the completed acquisitions may improve product content and margins. A low multiple on the preliminary earnings guide could then be a genuine opportunity.

The bear case is also concrete. A manufacturer can grow revenue and adjusted profit while customers, inventory and new factories absorb the cash. Debt introduced for acquisitions makes that delay more costly to equity owners. Customer concentration magnifies program changes: the annual filing says the ten largest customers represented 55% of FY2025 revenue. Capacity commitments may be difficult to reverse if demand softens. Integration costs, taxes and purchase accounting can also keep the adjusted earnings bridge wide. Our HOLD is not a claim that the shares must fall to the entry bar. It reflects the evidence required for a fresh commitment under this framework. Existing owners and new buyers have different tax, portfolio and time-horizon considerations that this general research does not evaluate. We would become more constructive with proof of cash conversion at an attractive price, and less constructive if the earnings base expands mainly through leverage while owner cash remains weak. The counterargument to waiting is that the market may reprice before the cash proof arrives; the counterargument to buying immediately is that optimistic guide-based multiples can remain cheap for a reason.

The next decisions

The next Q4 and full-year results are the first major operating checkpoint; an exact earnings-release calendar date is not established here. The company’s current lower bounds of $1.42B revenue and $1.77 adjusted EPS provide a clear delivery test, but we will also inspect the cash statement and reconciliation. A revenue beat accompanied by deteriorating collections would not resolve the central issue. A less spectacular print with improved cash and a transparent balance sheet could be more valuable to the equity case.

The expected Q2 2027 Ophir closing window is a second checkpoint, not a guaranteed date. Management’s leverage expectation of 1.5–2.0x by that closing gives a measurable capital-allocation claim to follow against the stated post-Epiq 2.8x starting point. The numerator, denominator and transaction perimeter must remain comparable. Any new acquisition, financing or material spending revision can change the test before then. We will update the case on new evidence rather than wait for a calendar milestone mechanically.
