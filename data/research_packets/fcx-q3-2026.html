Price and the question being priced

Freeport has a valuable collection of long-lived copper assets. This quarter does not settle what those assets are worth. Higher selling prices lifted earnings while copper shipments declined, Indonesian gold waited in the processing chain, and a new multiyear investment became a binding capital-allocation decision. The investment question is how much of the commodity windfall reaches common shareholders after partners, taxes and the next expansion are paid.

Our reference price is the saved November 10 provider quote of $60.30. The daily-history endpoint separately reports a November 10 close of $60.31; this one-cent provider difference is retained rather than silently rewriting either source. The first full earnings-day close was $65.95 versus $65.16 immediately before the October 27 release. That modest positive reaction does not explain subsequent daily moves, and we do not attribute the entire later decline to one corporate announcement.

We rate FCX HOLD, conviction three out of five, with high uncertainty and a rounded $58 base fair value. This is a valuation judgment, not a forecast that shares must trade at that price on a particular date. Our modeled bear-to-bull outcomes span $35 to $85. The probability-weighted result is $57.85, close to the base because the downside and upside assumptions broadly balance. A 25% margin-of-safety reference is $43.50, conditional on the business case remaining intact.

The prior Q2 episode focused on record commodity prices, a beat and a falling stock. This episode examines the ownership and reinvestment bridge instead: a shipment bottleneck, cash that belongs partly to partners, and the approved Bagdad spending schedule. The earnings print

The release explicitly labels the first two columns as the three months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025. Revenue rose from $6.972 billion to $7.546 billion, an 8.2% increase. Operating profit rose to $2.353 billion and its margin reached 31.2%, compared with 28.3% a year earlier. These are real improvements in the reported quarter. They are not evidence that physical copper sales grew.

Copper sales fell 23.3% to 749 million pounds. The average realized copper price rose 39.7% to $6.54 per pound. Production of 832 million pounds exceeded sales by 83 million pounds. Gold production of 234 thousand ounces exceeded sales of 105 thousand ounces by 129 thousand ounces. Production is not revenue: material can remain in process, await refining or be delayed before an external customer transaction qualifies for recognition.

The saved earnings feed reports adjusted EPS of $0.88 against an estimate of $0.815 and revenue of $7.546 billion against $7.361 billion expected. Those are provider comparisons with matching adjusted-earnings definitions, not SEC-certified consensus. GAAP EPS was $0.81 versus $0.46 a year ago. We display both measures because the costs excluded from adjusted earnings still have economic consequences.

Gold sales missed the July estimate of 160 thousand ounces by 34.4%. Copper sales essentially met the 750 million pound estimate. Management attributed the gold shortfall mainly to refined-sales timing in Indonesia. That explanation is testable in subsequent shipments and cash collections. Calling all of the difference temporary before the metal is sold would convert management's expectation into a fact. The current outlook instead calls for 300 thousand ounces of fourth-quarter gold sales, a demanding conversion checkpoint.

Assets, shipments and ownership

The mining portfolio spans the United States, South America and Indonesia, plus molybdenum mines and downstream conversion facilities. These businesses are not interchangeable. The United States copper division generated $869 million of operating profit, South America $1.014 billion and Indonesia $616 million. Corporate expenses and other divisions bring the consolidated total to $2.353 billion. The segment figures include management allocations and are not standalone public-company earnings.

External segment revenue can mislead if it is read without the intercompany column. Much of the United States mining output passes through the company's rod and refining operations. The mining division therefore shows only $50 million of unaffiliated revenue while the rod and refining division shows $2.359 billion. That does not mean refining creates most of the underlying copper economics. Our chart labels external revenue explicitly and uses operating profit as a separate lens.

Indonesia remains central to the recovery. Grasberg's ramp after the September 2025 mud rush, smelter restart and inventory conversion all affect the sales path. Smelting restarted in late August. The current release expects 2026 production to exceed sales by roughly 100 million pounds of copper and 70 thousand ounces of gold associated with processing inventory. These annual deferrals differ from the larger third-quarter production-minus-sales gaps because they cover different periods.

FCX consolidates subsidiaries it does not wholly own. Its PTFI share ownership is 48.76%; current Cerro Verde ownership increased to 55.66% in May. Morenci is proportionately consolidated at FCX's 72% interest. These arrangements require care when comparing consolidated volume, operating cash and common-share earnings. A dollar produced by a consolidated asset is not automatically a dollar available to the listed parent's shareholders. Our equity valuation uses common-owner cash assumptions rather than capitalizing the entire consolidated cash flow.

What common shareholders earned

The profit bridge begins with $2.353 billion of operating income. Net interest expense reduces this by $86 million, while other income adds $25 million, leaving $2.292 billion before tax and equity affiliates. The tax provision is $610 million, and affiliate earnings add $6 million. Consolidated net income is therefore $1.688 billion. Of consolidated earnings, $514 million is attributable to noncontrolling interests, leaving $1.174 billion attributable to common stockholders. Earnings attribution is not a cash distribution. Actual cash dividends and distributions to noncontrolling interests were $625 million over nine months; subtracting the first-half $359 million gives $266 million in the third quarter.

That partner share is about 30.5% of consolidated net income, not 30.5% of revenue. The release also presents noncontrolling income as a share of pretax income, a different denominator. These ratios cannot be swapped. A valuation using common EPS already incorporates the partner allocation. An enterprise-value model based on fully consolidated earnings would need a consistent treatment of minority claims and debt; subtracting only the headline policy net debt would be incomplete.

The company adds back $101 million of net after-tax charges to reach adjusted common income of $1.275 billion. Idle facility, direct recovery and restoration costs associated with the mud rush contribute $72 million after tax and partner allocation, with another $19 million from related depreciation. Other adjustments include legacy oil-and-gas obligations, tax matters and insurance effects. We preserve the issuer's reconciliation rather than describing the entire gap as stock compensation.

Nine-month earnings require another qualification. They include a $699 million pretax mud-rush insurance settlement gain and receipt. Adjusted earnings remove the gain, while operating cash flow includes the receipt. Insurance proceeds are real liquidity, but they do not represent a repeatable annual margin from mining. The quarterly and nine-month stories differ, which is why neither the latest EPS multiplied by four nor reported year-to-date cash is our standalone fair-value calculation.

Earnings quality and conversion

The strongest quality signal is that quarterly operating cash flow exceeded consolidated net income. Cash from operations was $1.981 billion versus $1.688 billion of consolidated earnings, or 1.17 times. Comparing operating cash with common net income instead would inflate apparent conversion because operating cash consolidates the partner-owned operations too. After capital expenditures, quarterly free cash flow was $822 million, before partner distributions and other financing uses.

The weaker signal is inventory conversion. Current inventory of $8.434 billion includes product, supplies and current mill-and-leach stockpiles. Dividing it by quarterly cost of sales and multiplying by 92 days produces a broad 155-day proxy. It is not a manufacturing turnover metric with a clean finished-goods denominator; ore stockpiles and consumables have different economic lives. Trade receivables of $880 million yield a much shorter 10.7-day proxy. We show the limitations alongside the computations.

Computed quarterly stock compensation was $28 million: the issuer reports $131 million for nine months less $103 million for the first half. That is approximately 0.37% of quarterly revenue. It remains an economic cost and is not the main adjustment story here. The effective tax rate of 26.6% also helped the common-income result, and the release discusses smelter-related tax benefits. Using that quarter's rate indefinitely would overstate certainty; management's current full-year assumption is approximately 30%.

The November 6 Form 10-Q concludes that disclosure controls were effective at quarter end and reports no material change in internal control. That finding supports reporting reliability; it does not certify future commodity prices or project economics. The filing also retains an unresolved outside-counsel investigation of PT Smelting activities under anti-corruption and other laws. The SEC indicated in March that it did not intend enforcement, but the company cannot predict the investigation's outcome. We retain that distinction in the risk assessment.

Cash, debt and the Bagdad bill

The balance sheet carries $9.398 billion of consolidated debt and $4.335 billion of cash, giving $5.063 billion of net debt before leases. FCX's financial-policy measure excludes $3.237 billion of PTFI downstream-processing debt and is therefore $1.826 billion. Both numbers are valid within their definitions. The lower measure governs the performance-based payout framework; it does not extinguish the excluded creditors' claims. Restricted cash is not added to unrestricted cash in our bridge.

The provider's balance-sheet debt total is higher because it includes $958 million of noncurrent lease obligations. We preserve that $6.021 billion lease-inclusive net-debt convention as a separate comparison. Our cash-flow equity model does not subtract net debt a second time: its assumptions are cash available to common owners after interest and financing needs with debt broadly stable. Readers should not mix a debt-free enterprise cash-flow stream with an equity discount rate or subtract debt from an already financed stream.

On November 4, after the earnings release, the board approved the $4.5 billion Bagdad expansion and modernization. Initial production is targeted for mid-2030. The anticipated increment is approximately 225 million pounds of copper and 12 million pounds of molybdenum annually. The project is expected to reduce long-term unit net cash costs by about 20%, but those benefits depend on completion, performance and the assumed by-product prices.

The stated spending schedule implies approximately $900 million in 2027, $1.350–$1.575 billion in 2028, and $2.025–$2.250 billion across 2029–2030. These are calculations from management's percentages, not new company guidance. The issuer describes break-even economics around $4 copper with $20 molybdenum; that is a project assumption, not a guaranteed shareholder return. Engineering, inflation and execution can change both timing and cost. The company says the expenditure will enter its updated capital outlook, so the older 2026 capex guide must not be treated as the future steady-state spending rate.

Three routes to common-equity value

Our discounted-cash route is explicitly an equity model. Assumed cash available to common owners is $4.0, $4.5, $5.0, $5.6 and $6.2 billion in 2027–2031. Those figures are analyst assumptions after maintenance and growth investment, interest, taxes and partner claims. They require recovery in volumes and retained margins while accommodating Bagdad's build. They are neither management guidance nor a mechanical extrapolation of consolidated free cash flow. Debt is assumed broadly stable and recurring compensation remains an economic cost.

Discounting those flows at 9.5% and using 3% terminal growth yields $19.048 billion of explicit-period value and $62.409 billion of terminal value. Dividing total equity value by 1.442 billion diluted shares gives $56.49 per share. Terminal value supplies 76.6% of the total, so the apparent precision is not confidence. A failure to reach the mature cash level matters much more than a small change in one quarterly EPS estimate. We do not add a separate full Bagdad net present value to a cash path that already assumes its contribution.

The second route capitalizes $4.5 billion of normalized annual common-owner cash at a 6% required yield. The arithmetic is $4.5 billion divided by 0.06 and then by 1.442 billion shares, or $52.01. This deliberately values a lower through-cycle cash level than the final year of the growth model. The third route uses the saved 2027 consensus EPS of $4.28611 at 15 times, giving $64.29. The multiple is our assumption and the EPS is an external estimate; neither is an issuer promise.

The three routes support a rounded $58 base within a roughly $52–$64 model interval. They are cross-checks, not independent observations: each remains exposed to the same copper cycle and operating recovery. At $60.30, the discounted model's fixed early cash path requires about $6.746 billion of mature common cash versus our $6.2 billion base. That reverse calculation is conditional on our discount rate and growth convention. Investors can rationally reach another value by changing those assumptions; the sensitivity grid makes the disagreement visible.

The fair assessment gives management credit for meeting the copper-sales estimate and making progress on the Grasberg ramp. It also records the gold-sales miss and higher-than-expected unit net cash costs. Copper cash costs were $2.07 per pound against the July $2.00 estimate, largely because delayed gold sales reduced by-product credits. That is a coherent explanation, but it should be judged against subsequent gold realization rather than permanently excused.

The outlook calls for fourth-quarter sales of 875 million pounds of copper, 300 thousand ounces of gold and 22 million pounds of molybdenum. The full-year operating-cash forecast is approximately $8.5 billion, net of $0.6 billion of working-capital and other uses, assuming fourth-quarter prices of $6.50 copper, $4,000 gold and $32 molybdenum. Subtracting nine-month operating cash implies about $2.976 billion in the fourth quarter. That is a conditional arithmetic checkpoint, not a new unconditional forecast.

Capital allocation now has a longer test. Bagdad is wholly owned, so its eventual project cash has different ownership economics from consolidated Indonesian cash. The spending arrives before the full production increment and competes with dividends, repurchases and other projects. What could invalidate the call

Commodity exposure is the largest recurring risk. A high copper price can hide weak throughput, cost escalation or excess investment. Lower prices can compress margins before spending is adjusted. Gold and molybdenum affect by-product credits as well as sales, so a simple copper-price chart does not capture all operating sensitivity. We do not assume the quarter's $6.54 realized copper price persists indefinitely or that every additional pound earns the same margin.

Execution and inventory conversion come next. The Grasberg restoration and smelter restart involve technical, safety and logistics risks. A forecast recovery can slip even when the resource remains valuable. Indonesia also has administrative and fiscal questions around refined gold exports. The release describes a conflict between the 10% export-duty regulation and the IUPK terms. We do not assume the favorable interpretation will prevail before the company receives clarity and reports actual sales economics.

Bagdad has the advantages of an established resource, infrastructure and workforce, but a brownfield project can still overrun. Capital inflation, engineering changes, water or power constraints and delayed commissioning reduce returns. The new investment's long life makes a short-term EPS multiple incomplete, yet its long duration also increases the importance of disciplined discounting. The unresolved PT Smelting investigation adds legal and governance uncertainty even though the latest filing reports effective disclosure controls and the SEC has indicated no intended enforcement action.

The bull case is credible: successful Grasberg recovery, inventory release, stronger leach recoveries and a disciplined wholly owned U.S. expansion could raise common cash materially. The bear case is not that copper has no future. It is that shareholders pay too much for cyclical earnings while future growth consumes capital and partner-owned assets absorb cash. Our HOLD recognizes both. A lower price alone would not fix a deteriorating project budget or evidence that the common-cash path is unattainable.

Methods and source tables

All current financial figures use the October 27 SEC-filed earnings release and the November 6 quarterly report, with the November 4 expansion announcement treated as a subsequent development. All nine quarters of normalized financial history now use their contemporaneous SEC-filed issuer releases. Conflicting raw provider financial statements are quarantined as audit evidence and do not feed the displayed history. Market prices and external estimates remain separately labeled provider inputs. The dataset retains complete primary income, balance-sheet and cumulative cash-flow rows plus operating, segment and adjustment tables. Cumulative cash-flow statements keep their year-to-date labels; disclosed quarterly operating cash and capex are taken from each release summary.

Dollar amounts in financial tables are millions unless the heading says per share or billions. Copper uses millions of recoverable pounds and gold thousands of recoverable ounces. Annual cash-flow statements use fiscal years ended December 31. The nine-quarter sequence runs from Q3 2024 through Q3 2026. Free cash flow is a computed analytical measure and does not subtract acquisitions, debt repayment or partner distributions unless expressly stated.

This illustrates why raw provider fields are evidence inputs, not unquestioned authority. We similarly distinguish the provider's lease-inclusive debt from the issuer's financing debt and the special payout-policy measure.

The valuation share count is a weighted-average diluted measure, not a claim about exact shares outstanding on the quote date. The resulting computed market capitalization therefore differs from the provider's live market cap. We retain both conventions explicitly. Model probabilities, discount rates, terminal growth, normalized cash and valuation multiples are analyst judgments.
