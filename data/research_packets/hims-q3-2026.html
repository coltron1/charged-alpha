Five things to know

  3. Subscriber metrics are not disclosed cohort retention. Ending subscribers rose 13% to 2.788 million. The aggregate count gives no separate retention, acquisition cost or contribution data for individual customer cohorts.
  4. Acquisition claims and share awards matter to common value. September 30 acquisition claims totaled $733.9 million. The valuation schedules assumed settlements and includes share awards in the ownership bridge.

Stock price and investment thesis

Our call is HOLD at the November 13, 2026 regular-session close of $19.73. Base present fair value is approximately $17.10 per common share. That is an estimate of current value, not a one-year price target. The stock has meaningful upside if customer economics improve more than our base assumes, but the current price does not provide enough protection against the acquisition, financing and regulatory risks to justify fresh buying on our assumptions. Business quality and investment attractiveness are separate judgments.

The decisive question is whether a larger customer relationship creates more sustainable cash per share. Quarterly revenue grew 49%, yet gross profit grew approximately 15%. The shift toward branded weight-loss products and a larger international footprint can increase sales while reducing the percentage of each sale available to fund marketing, support, technology and shareholders. We do not assume that the former gross-margin structure returns automatically, and we do not freeze the present weakness forever.

The main model follows average subscribers and revenue per average subscriber into gross profit, cash operating costs, investment and owner economics. It explicitly recognizes acquisition obligations, outstanding awards and assumed funding. Its base gives about $17 of current value; subjective scenario weights produce about $20. These are different outputs. We do not replace the main model with the weighted number merely because the latter sits closer to the market price.

The conditional base value at December 31, 2027 is roughly $19.97, assuming no dividends. That represents approximately 1% total upside from the reference quote, below our 12% annual equity-return requirement. This horizon value is calculated from the remaining operating business and the projected cash and claim balances, not by multiplying current fair value by a required return. Neither number guarantees a future trading price.

Quarterly financial scorecard

Hims reported $890.373 million of Q3 revenue, compared with $598.976 million in the prior-year quarter. Gross profit increased from $442.058 million to $509.199 million. The difference between sales growth and gross-profit growth is central: revenue rose approximately 48.6%, while gross profit rose approximately 15.2%. Gross margin was reported as 57%, down from 74%. Those margins are rounded issuer figures; dollar calculations use the reported unrounded financial-statement amounts.

The company reported a $66.146 million operating loss and a $33.040 million net loss. Adjusted EBITDA was $82.388 million versus $78.365 million. Adjusted EBITDA excludes several economically important costs, including stock compensation and acquisition-related expenses. A reconciliation is useful for analyzing operations, but the adjustment label does not determine whether an item costs shareholders money. Stock compensation must be recognized through either cash economics or ownership dilution, and acquisition obligations must be funded.

Operating cash flow was $65.393 million in the quarter. The company's free-cash-flow definition subtracts property, equipment and intangible purchases plus software investment, producing $25.974 million. A year earlier, quarterly free cash flow was $79.364 million. Nine-month free cash flow was only $10.813 million, after $107.997 million of capital spending and software investment. The nine-month view avoids treating seasonal working-capital timing as a permanent run rate.

The tables below preserve the distinction between reported GAAP results, reported non-GAAP measures and calculated growth rates. Our forecast cash flow is a separate economic FCFF measure, with new-grant compensation and normalized tax treatment. It should not be presented as if the issuer reported or guided that measure. A persuasive thesis must explain how the company moves from these actual figures toward the stronger modeled cash path.

Customer economics and business environment

Hims is best analyzed as a healthcare subscription and commerce platform, not a drug developer with a patent portfolio. It connects customers with clinicians, treatment options, fulfillment and continuing support. Its customer relationship can be valuable when it lowers friction, improves appropriate follow-up and makes repeated purchases more efficient. The economics depend on retention, acquisition spending, product mix and the portion of treatment revenue retained after drug and service costs.

Domestic revenue was $657.089 million, up 21%; international revenue was $233.284 million, up 321%. These geographic comparisons are not organic-growth measures. Eucalyptus contributed approximately 15% of consolidated Q3 revenue, according to the acquisition note. The transaction expanded the international customer base, but it also created substantial deferred and contingent claims. Acquired growth belongs in the operating forecast and its purchase obligations belong in the equity bridge; counting only the first half would overstate value.

End-period subscribers were 2.788 million, up 13%. Monthly revenue per average subscriber was $105, versus $81. The issuer defines this as total revenue divided by average subscribers and months in the period. Total revenue includes one-time purchases; the average subscriber denominator is the average of beginning and ending subscriber counts. Therefore this is not a pure subscription price, a retention rate or proof that pricing rose 30%. Higher-priced branded drugs and acquisition mix affect it.

Manufacturers retain important economic power through product supply, contracts and price. Better access can strengthen the platform's appeal while compressing its margin. Competition also includes other direct-care platforms, established healthcare channels and manufacturer-supported access pathways. We do not assume that customer convenience automatically produces an enduring monopoly or that all drug spending is high-margin platform revenue. The useful question is whether the relationship improves lifetime contribution after marketing and service costs.

From contribution to owner cash

Gross profit less marketing is a practical, limited checkpoint. It increased from approximately $209.908 million to $261.919 million. This is not a disclosed segment profit measure, EBITDA or free cash flow: it is a calculated contribution before other operating costs. It nevertheless shows that lower gross margin did not erase every benefit of growth. The platform had more gross profit dollars left after marketing than a year earlier.

Marketing expense was $247.280 million, or approximately 27.8% of revenue. Dividing marketing by net subscriber growth would not produce a valid customer acquisition cost. Net growth combines gross additions and churn, and reported marketing includes more than direct acquisition spending. The company does not supply the full cohort retention, gross-addition and lifetime-value data needed for a robust cohort model in the materials reviewed. We leave those measurements undisclosed rather than manufacture precise LTV/CAC ratios.

Our base uses average subscribers of 3.15 million in 2027, rising to 6.3 million in 2036. Revenue per average subscriber increases gradually from $105 to $114 per month. Together, those drivers generate $3.969 billion and $8.618 billion of revenue in the respective years. Gross margin recovers from 57% to 61%, marketing intensity falls to 20%, and cash support, technology and administrative costs gain scale. These are author assumptions, not guidance.

The model charges new future compensation grants economically at 4% of revenue. It also deducts capital expenditure, incremental working capital and normalized taxes. Existing awards are handled in the share bridge, so their future accounting expense is excluded from cash operating costs. Depreciation is excluded from cash expense ratios and enters EBIT separately before taxes; capex is then deducted in FCFF. This prevents both pretending asset investment is free and subtracting the same cost twice. The strong later cash path is an execution requirement, not a guaranteed outcome.

Independent acquisition reference

The June 2026 Eucalyptus acquisition provides an independently motivated reference for the scale of operating value. The purchase price for accounting purposes was $968.539 million. Its contribution was approximately 15% of consolidated third-quarter revenue, implying roughly $133.6 million for that quarter. Annualizing that quarter purely as a comparison denominator gives approximately $534.2 million. Consideration divided by that denominator is approximately 1.81 times.

Our base Hims enterprise value divided by Hims' own Q3 revenue annualized is approximately 1.69 times. This comparison does not establish that the stock is worth an exact transaction multiple. Eucalyptus consideration is accounting purchase price, not independently verified enterprise value. The filing does not give every adjustment necessary to convert it into a clean market-observed EV benchmark. Geography, product mix, acquisition control, acquired cash and debt, and post-combination employment payments can change comparability materially.

The useful conclusion is modest: the main model's operating-value scale is not obviously detached from the recent transaction context. The comparison does not validate the equity target, since Hims' own debt, acquisition claims and share structure still matter. We never average the transaction ratio with the DCF to force agreement. A single acquired company cannot substitute for a well-matched public peer set or observed customer lifetime economics.

For quality assessment, watch whether the platform can convert relationships into recurring gross contribution while reducing the need to reacquire customers. Management's discussion of improved engagement and retention is a hypothesis to test against subsequent disclosure. Without measured cohorts, confidence should remain limited. Evidence of durable cross-specialty retention, better service outcomes and lower marketing cost per retained gross-profit dollar would strengthen the thesis. Growth purchased with consistently heavier spending would weaken it, even if revenue targets were met.

Cash, debt, acquisitions and ownership

At September 30, cash and equivalents were $605.604 million and short-term investments were $106.617 million. The $712.221 million combined total is not all excess cash. Our model retains $125 million as required operating liquidity. That reserve is an operating asset, not a second penalty on cash already consumed by forecast losses. Incremental working capital is modeled separately as the business expands.

Convertible note principal was $1.4025 billion, compared with $1.367393 billion carrying value. The $1 billion tranche matures May 15, 2030, and the $402.5 million tranche June 1, 2032. Both have zero regular coupons. Face principal is deducted once from enterprise value; no interest expense or duplicate principal repayment is also subtracted from FCFF. Capped-call protection offsets conversion exposure up to the contractual caps, with net exposure above those caps included in the share-value solver. Operating leases remain operating costs, so their liabilities are not deducted again.

Deferred acquisition and earn-out claims totaled $733.864 million, including $565.233 million current. These are separate from note debt and cannot be ignored. The explicit Q4 bridge uses the issuer's revenue and adjusted-EBITDA guidance to estimate economic cash after compensation, tax and investment. It splits 44 days through November 13 from 48 remaining days. It assumes a $125 million acquisition payment on December 18, reducing cash and claims together. That date and amount are planning assumptions, not assertions of an announced payment schedule.

The base assumes $250 million of gross equity funding on November 16 at $19.73, with 3% fees and about 12.67 million new shares. This is unannounced hypothetical financing. Remaining current acquisition claims are phased through 2027 and the remaining balance through 2028. The resulting minimum cash is approximately $156.6 million, above the $125 million floor. The bull's minimum is approximately $138.1 million without new equity. The severe bear cannot finance its losses. Different settlement timing, prices or operating cash would change these results; the revolver is a contingency, not free permanent capital.

There were no repurchases during the first nine months. Common shares rose by 7.153 million from December 31 to September 30. We count both economic share classes, unvested awards and in-the-money options with exercise proceeds. Existing awards are counted once; new-grant costs are charged once. Tax withholding on net share settlement is not an additional buyback benefit layered over those claims.

Present value and dated outcomes

The base is a ten-year economic FCFF model. Operating cash is discounted at 11.5%, with a short explicit Q4 stub and annual forecast cash through 2036. Terminal growth is 3%, supported by an assumed 18% incremental return on capital. Terminal reinvestment therefore consumes one-sixth of next-period NOPAT. That terminal formula already includes investment needs; we do not deduct a second perpetual capex amount from it.

Approximately 63% of base enterprise value comes from the terminal period. This is a material limitation. The model requires customer growth, improved gross economics and operating leverage to endure beyond the explicit horizon. Terminal dependence does not automatically make the stock overvalued, but it reduces the confidence justified by a precise-looking point estimate. Operating sensitivities and subsequent evidence matter more than superficial precision.

Base present value is $17.10 per share. Bull present value is approximately $46.54. The severe bear produces no common-equity recovery because operating losses and acquisition obligations cannot be funded on the assumed path. Its unfunded continuation arithmetic is diagnostic, not a claim that the business has a negative liquidation sale price. Common value is floored at zero. The weights are subjective: 25% severe bear, 50% base and 25% bull. The weighted current value is approximately $20.18, not an additional model or a statistical confidence interval.

The dated base value at December 31, 2027 is approximately $19.97. We value the remaining operating cash flows at that date, then add projected cash after financing and operations and subtract the debt and remaining acquisition claims. Current acquisition payments and the accrued legal payment reduce cash, and the corresponding claims are removed. There are no assumed dividends. The base total return is about 1.2%, while the bull is much higher and severe recovery is zero. The scenario-weighted future price is a separate arithmetic expectation, not a promise or a substitute for the underlying distribution.

The entry discipline is distinct from valuation: a 20% cushion below base value would be around $13.68. That is not another haircut to fair value. A stronger operating evidence set could justify a higher model before the stock ever trades there. Conversely, a lower market quote alone would not repair a deteriorating cash outlook.

Street context and market-implied hurdle

The dated Street reference is a $28.31 average one-year target from 15 analysts, shown by the market-data provider as updated November 11, 2026. Our own approximately $19.97 value at December 31, 2027 uses a broadly comparable horizon, about one month later. The Street aggregate can mix methods, assumptions and update times. It is useful context rather than independent confirmation of our model. Our current $17.10 fair value should not be compared with a future target as though the clocks were identical.

The reverse model asks a more specific question. Keeping the base subscriber, revenue-per-subscriber, operating-cost, investment, tax, funding and discount assumptions fixed, what gross-margin path supports the actual $19.73 quote? The answer is approximately 1.54 extra percentage points in every forecast year. Mature gross margin rises from 61% to approximately 62.54%. This is not an assertion that today's 57% margin needs only a small improvement: it is additional improvement on top of the recovery already included in our base.

The hurdle is economically plausible, but it requires evidence. A more favorable branded-product mix, retention benefits or negotiating power could support it. Continued supplier pass-through, aggressive pricing and expensive acquisition could prevent it. The reverse result is neither management guidance nor a consensus forecast.

The operating sensitivity grid changes two levers that shareholders can investigate: average subscriber scale and gross margin. A 3-point increase in margins across the forecast produces value around $22.23. A 10% increase in subscriber scale with unchanged economics produces around $19.27. These are controlled experiments, not new scenario probabilities. The rate sensitivity offers additional model-risk context, but should not distract from the observable business drivers that can make the call right or wrong.

Management outlook and the tests it creates

Fourth-quarter revenue guidance is $875–895 million, with adjusted EBITDA of $70–90 million. The sales midpoint is slightly below the third-quarter result. Full-year revenue guidance is $3.13–3.15 billion, and adjusted EBITDA guidance is $257–277 million. Management narrowed the sales range and reduced the EBITDA range. Guidance is a forward-looking issuer expectation, not an audited promise or a direct guide to common-owner cash.

Management described investment ahead of platform expansion and stronger marketing activity in selected international markets. The earnings-call materials also discuss broader branded-drug availability, international growth and the potential for customer experience to strengthen retention. Those initiatives can improve the long-term platform while putting pressure on near-term margins. The analytical task is to determine whether the later contribution is large enough to justify the early spending.

A useful evaluation should separate timing from economics. A membership-payment deferral can change when revenue is recognized without identical effects on cash collection. Branded-drug mix can change the margin earned on a recognized sale. Increased marketing can reduce current profit while creating either valuable retained customers or unproductive spending. All three can occur together, and a single adjusted-EBITDA figure does not distinguish them.

We would not declare success solely because the company reaches the revenue range. Subscribers, gross profit, marketing intensity, working capital and acquisition payments must support the story. Nor should one quarter of deliberate investment automatically prove failure. The next useful test is whether the following cohorts and cash measures improve as management expects. Our model allows time for improvement, but it charges the investment and funding needed to get there. The next reporting date was not independently verified for this packet and is therefore left as the next scheduled issuer release, not an invented calendar date.

Bull, bear and the stock call

The strongest bull case is that Hims becomes a more useful relationship for consumers who want convenient, supported access to multiple forms of care. Branded choices, international expansion and a broader customer base can increase the number of customers and the value of retained relationships. Lower gross margins do not automatically mean lower long-run owner cash if acquisition efficiency and repeat purchases improve enough. The bull model explicitly permits that better outcome.

The strongest bear is not simply that demand disappears. A platform can grow revenue while giving too much of the economics to manufacturers, marketing channels and other claimants. Acquisition payments can arrive before integration benefits, and stock issuance can limit the benefit per existing share. Legal and regulatory friction can affect costs, product availability, customer conversion and retention at the same time. These are mechanisms that connect to model drivers, not a generic list of frightening words.

The FTC matter involves allegations concerning privacy, subscription and cancellation practices. Hims disputes the allegations and intends to defend itself. The 10-Q lists an approximately $60 million accrual and a January 14, 2027 hearing on its motion to dismiss. The outcome, additional loss and hearing timing are uncertain. We deduct the accrued claim once and place additional legal cash only in the severe scenario. We do not present an accrual as a final settlement, a finding of guilt or a reliable ceiling on loss.

Our HOLD call means existing exposure is a high-risk judgment, while new money lacks sufficient cushion on the base. It does not mean every shareholder should take identical action regardless of portfolio or circumstances. At the reference quote, the base horizon return does not meet the stated annual requirement. Greater evidence of retention, contribution and funding control would improve the investment case; more revenue alone would not. The intended discipline is to change the model when the evidence changes, rather than choose a target because an audience wants a more optimistic rating.

Evidence that changes the thesis

The next earnings release provides the most direct operating checkpoint. Compare revenue and adjusted EBITDA with the published fourth-quarter ranges, but also track the sequence of subscriber growth, gross margin and marketing spending. A subscriber rebound is stronger evidence when it comes with better gross contribution and manageable working-capital demands. Gross-margin stabilization is more convincing when it reflects recurring mix and supplier economics rather than a one-off accounting benefit.

Acquisition settlement is an equally important catalyst for equity holders. Cash payments reduce liquidity; stock payments change ownership. Either can be sensible, but both should be reconciled against the claims already carried in the model. Future reports should make it possible to replace our assumed installment timing and financing with actual cash flows and shares. The model must then be updated without charging the same acquisition twice.

The legal calendar is a checkpoint, not a forecast of a favorable ruling. The currently disclosed January hearing could be rescheduled, and an intermediate decision might not resolve the underlying economics. We would examine both cash consequences and operational restrictions or changes in customer processes. A favorable legal development would not by itself prove the branded-product margin recovery; an adverse one should enter the relevant cash or operating driver rather than a repeated general discount.

The following signposts state what would improve or weaken the thesis. Each has a timing basis and a link to an economic consequence. The revolver expiration and note maturities deserve advance attention, even though they are not the next-quarter headline. A company with positive adjusted EBITDA can still face constrained financing flexibility. The combination of operating evidence and a funded ownership path, rather than a single catalyst announcement, determines whether the current value estimate should move.

The cash and ownership conventions are deliberately explicit. Class A and Class V shares have the same economic rights. The model does not assume that the unused revolver or repurchase authorization is cash owned by shareholders. Severe-case negative liquidity is an unfunded diagnostic and results in zero common recovery, not a negative cash balance that could actually persist indefinitely.
