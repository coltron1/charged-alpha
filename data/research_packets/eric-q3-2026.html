Price and the decision

The October 15 ADS close was $10.71, compared with $9.58 before the report, a computed 11.8% gain. That reaction is consistent with investors looking through the prior disposal gain and rewarding current operating execution. It is not proof that every improvement will recur. We use the closing price rather than mixing the earlier ranking quote into the valuation. The quote and foreign-exchange snapshots have separate timestamps; the model makes that distinction explicit.

The quote implies an equity value near $34.8bn. Our valuation denominator uses the issuer’s September 30 diluted outstanding shares,3.267bn, rather than silently reverse-engineering shares from the vendor market capitalization. The resulting model capitalization differs slightly from the provider’s current capitalization because date and share conventions differ. That is a reconciliation item, not a reason to force one number into the other. A US holder also owns Swedish currency exposure through the ADS even though the screen price is in dollars.

The previous episode centered on an earnings beat followed by a sharp selloff. Repeating that angle would miss this quarter. Here the important question is whether a cleaner underlying comparison also produces durable cash. The older price reactions provide context for uncertainty, not a trading rule. We do not infer a reliable pattern from nine observations, nor equate a positive first-day return with a margin of safety.

The businesses behind the result

Networks remains the economic engine. It generated SEK 38.696bn of sales and SEK 8.951bn of adjusted EBITA, a 23.1% adjusted margin. Cloud Software and Services contributed SEK 15.829bn of sales and SEK 2.029bn of adjusted EBITA. Enterprise remained loss-making at an adjusted EBITA loss of SEK 0.550bn on SEK 4.560bn of sales. A strong group result therefore does not mean every part of the portfolio has reached an acceptable return.

Networks benefits from deployment demand and intellectual-property licensing. Licensing revenue was SEK 3.9bn for the group, partly including revenue for previously unlicensed periods;82% sat in Networks. The recurring annualized licensing run-rate was approximately SEK 13.5bn. Multiplying this quarter’s total by four would overstate that recurring signal. We treat the difference as a quality and timing question, not evidence that the licensing franchise lacks value.

The announced organization changes combine two Asian market areas from January 1,2027 and redistribute Enterprise Wireless Solutions activities. This can improve accountability and cost efficiency, but it may also change segment comparisons and make targets harder to follow. The listed parent and current financial statements continue. We analyze execution and comparability instead of excluding the company simply because an ordinary restructuring is pending. Future packets should bridge any revised segment presentation to this baseline.

A bridge through the comparison

The cleanest bridge begins with current adjusted EBITA of SEK 10.121bn. Deduct SEK 0.893bn of restructuring to reach reported EBITA of SEK 9.228bn; deduct approximately SEK 0.364bn of amortization to reach EBIT of SEK 8.864bn. Net financial expense of SEK 0.910bn reduces pretax income to SEK 7.954bn. Tax of SEK 2.157bn leaves SEK 5.797bn of consolidated profit. This bridge keeps operating adjustments, financing and tax in their proper places.

Last year’s adjusted EBITA was SEK 15.819bn, including the approximately SEK 7.6bn disposal gain. Removing that disclosed gain leaves roughly SEK 8.219bn. Against that limited comparison, current adjusted EBITA increased about 23.1%. This is our computed comparison, not a new company performance measure. It isolates the largest distortion while leaving other differences visible. It is more informative than either calling the quarter a collapse or pretending the reported decline never happened.

Financial net worsened, including provisions for interest associated with tax matters and currency-hedge effects. The 27.1% effective tax rate is a quarter-specific result, not a tax assumption that must hold forever. Profit attributable to parent owners was SEK 5.806bn, slightly above consolidated income because non-controlling interests recorded a small loss. Rounding produces a SEK 1m difference across these rows. We retain the filed numbers rather than manufacturing an exact reconciliation.

The cash-conversion ratio is reassuring in isolation: operating cash flow exceeded net income. It is not a substitute for looking inside working capital. Inventories used SEK 4.086bn, contract liabilities used SEK 5.399bn, and other operating assets and liabilities supplied SEK 4.922bn. The net operating-asset movement was a SEK 3.728bn outflow. A single headline ratio can hide several large and offsetting movements. We want cash generation to survive without repeatedly needing favorable offsets.

Restructuring is elevated this year. Excluding it may help compare operating capacity, but shareholders still fund cash settlements and disruption. Annual compensation also deserves precision: the latest annual report separates share-settled programs from cash-settled plans. In 2025 total compensation expense in the disclosed program table was SEK 1.790bn, of which only SEK 0.175bn related to share-settled programs. Calling the full total dilution, or treating a vendor’s zero SBC field as verified absence, would be misleading.

Our quality judgment is mixed rather than mechanically bullish. Recurring licensing is attractive, but retroactive revenue can flatter a quarter. Inventory can support future deliveries, but it ties up capital today. Historical impairment and divestment effects make reported earnings volatile. The next report should show whether stronger sales release inventory into cash while the adjusted margins remain near the guided range. That combination matters more than another favorable adjusted-profit headline.

The result is SEK 4.902bn. This is the definition used throughout our charts and starting cash analysis. A vendor’s simpler FCF calculation is not interchangeable.

The bullish case deserves a fair hearing. Ericsson can gain share in strategic markets while customers modernize networks, recurring intellectual property can support attractive margins, and enterprise or network-API investments could finally contribute better returns. If those improvements convert to cash without another working-capital buildup, our normalized base will prove too conservative. A company with significant net cash can also absorb difficult quarters and invest through a cycle.

The bearish case is that the quarter’s strongest signals are timing-sensitive. Retroactive licensing can boost margin, inventory may reflect harder-to-sell stock or a deliberate expensive buffer, and cost savings may be offset by components or customer pricing pressure. A flat equipment market leaves less room for indiscriminate growth. Repeated restructuring can become an ordinary cost rather than an exceptional adjustment. Our HOLD call acknowledges both sets of evidence and demands cash confirmation before paying a premium.

Security structure adds another dimension. The ADS represents a B share; A shares carry greater voting rights. Economic ownership through the US listing does not deliver proportional influence over every corporate decision. The annual report’s governance and compliance disclosures therefore belong in the research even when they do not invalidate current earnings. No allegation is treated as resolved merely because it is old, and no ordinary pending transaction is treated as an automatic exclusion of the continuing issuer.
