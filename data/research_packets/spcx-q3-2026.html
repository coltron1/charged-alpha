The reported quarter

Revenue was $14.575 billion versus $5.576 billion a year earlier, an increase of 161.4%. Sequential growth was 86.5% from $7.814 billion. Operating income reached $2.791 billion against a $273 million loss in the comparable quarter. The change is economically substantial even before discussing management adjustments. The quarter produced a 19.1% operating margin while spending $4.596 billion on research and development. This is not a case where every sign of progress disappears when one removes a promotional metric.

The important qualification is composition. SI supplied more than half of current revenue, and new cloud agreements, acquired Cursor revenue, and Grok adoption all contributed. Cursor added $608 million from its August 14 closing through September 30. Removing that disclosed contribution leaves $6.952 billion of SI revenue, still far above the prior $813 million. That arithmetic is useful but is not a certified organic-growth rate: the company has not supplied a complete constant-perimeter bridge for all portfolio and contract changes. We label the subtraction precisely instead of calling it organic.

Three businesses, different economics

Connectivity is the strongest demonstrated operating engine. Revenue rose to $5.248 billion and operating income to $2.367 billion, a 45.1% margin. Consumer revenue was $3.121 billion; enterprise and government revenue reached $2.127 billion. The latter grew faster year over year, and its contracts can differ from ordinary household broadband economics. Starlink ended the quarter with 13.6 million consumer subscribers, up from 12.0 million in June and 7.2 million a year earlier. Monthly consumer ARPU improved sequentially to $69 but remained below the prior $83.

SI is now the largest revenue segment at $7.560 billion, with operating income of $431 million. Its 5.7% operating margin is much smaller than its $4.953 billion adjusted EBITDA suggests. Nameplate compute increased from 1.4 to 1.7 gigawatts during the quarter. Nameplate capacity describes installed potential, not an independently verified utilization rate, contracted cash yield or customer credit quality. Cloud agreements need to translate that capacity into collectible high-return revenue. The release does not disclose enough contract-level terms to capitalize every gigawatt at a fixed franchise value.

Space revenue recovered sequentially to $1.767 billion, while its operating result was a $7 million loss. Twelve customer launches and twenty-three internal launches served different economic roles. Internal launches support Starlink and should not be counted again as outside customer sales. The first revenue-generating Starship mission delivered twenty-six V3 satellites, a meaningful operational milestone. Its broader economic value depends on repeatability, costs, regulation and customer deployment. A successful flight is evidence of progress, not proof that mature launch margins have already arrived.

The six-billion-dollar earnings bridge

Adjusted EBITDA exceeded operating income by $6.018 billion. The bridge is visible: $3.644 billion of depreciation and amortization, $1.146 billion of ordinary share-based compensation, $1.217 billion of acquisition-related share compensation, $8 million of acquisition costs, and $3 million of restructuring charges. Adding those items to $2.791 billion of operating income produces $8.809 billion. The arithmetic is not an allegation about accounting. It shows why two truthful measures answer different questions. EBITDA describes earnings before major asset and employee-equity costs; operating profit retains them.

The SI bridge is particularly large because infrastructure depreciation and Cursor compensation sit within this business. Excluding noncash costs can help compare an acquisition quarter with an earlier one, yet employee equity still allocates ownership. The release values acquisition awards attributable to post-acquisition service at $23.4 billion, subject to future forfeitures. That is not an immediate cash payment, and it is not the same as GAAP purchase consideration of $42.5 billion or the headline implied equity value of $60 billion. Those three numbers cannot simply be added as though they were a single cash invoice.

Net income is a different bridge again. Operating income of $2.791 billion plus $873 million of interest income, minus $675 million of interest expense, plus $1.025 billion of other income, produces $4.014 billion before tax. A $390 million tax benefit lifts net income to $4.404 billion. Thus $1.613 billion of the gap between operating and net profit came from below operations and tax. The business improved, but annualizing the entire net-income number as recurring operating earning power would capitalize benefits that the release does not establish as repeatable.

Liquidity is a runway, not earnings

The company has substantial financing capacity already on its balance sheet. Cash and equivalents were $51.294 billion, and marketable securities were $38.561 billion. Against $38.844 billion of current and noncurrent debt and finance leases, simple net cash is $51.011 billion, or about $3.76 per current economic share. This is a deliberately stated definition. It does not subtract every operating liability, capitalize every future commitment, or assume that all cash can immediately be distributed. It also excludes restricted cash and avoids counting digital assets as operating liquidity.

Nine-month PP&E purchases were $48.069 billion versus $13.097 billion of operating cash flow. Our simple free-cash-flow measure is therefore negative $34.972 billion. The corresponding quarter measure is negative $9.962 billion. Product rebates, capitalized interest and intangible purchases are disclosed separately; including them would change the exact cash-investment definition, so the packet labels its arithmetic rather than implying a company-defined FCF figure. SI alone consumed $16.444 billion of the $19.593 billion quarter capex, about eighty-four percent.

This is not an immediate insolvency thesis. The IPO brought in $85.675 billion net during the first nine months and substantially enlarged the liquidity buffer. Financing is why the company can pursue several infrastructure programs together. The valuation question is whether incremental deployed capital earns enough to compensate existing owners after maintenance, obsolescence and dilution. A large bank balance can make a strategy feasible while the purchase price still leaves shareholders with an unattractive prospective return. We therefore add net cash to modeled enterprise value but do not use it as proof of capital efficiency.

Three generous routes, one demanding price

Our base fair value is $85, with a practical $75–$100 valuation band, against the $147.79 completed-close reference. These are analyst scenarios, not company forecasts, and their generosity is intentional: a negative call on a powerful growth franchise should survive a serious growth case. The three routes yield approximately $79, $98 and $82 per share. We round a judgmental base inside that cluster instead of presenting an average as scientific precision. Conviction is three out of five and uncertainty is Very High. Fast contract growth, young trading history, acquisition accounting and long-duration cash flows create wide outcomes.

The first route is an eight-year unlevered cash-flow model for 2027 through 2034. Annual cash flows in billions are minus twenty-five, minus ten, ten, thirty, fifty-five, eighty, one hundred five and one hundred thirty. These are after reinvestment and intended economic compensation costs, not adjusted EBITDA. Discounting at eleven percent and assuming four percent perpetual growth produces $178.8 billion of explicit-period present value and $838.1 billion of terminal present value. Add $51.0 billion net cash and divide by 13.579 billion shares: $78.65. More than eighty-two percent of enterprise value is terminal. That concentration is a risk disclosure, not a reason to hide the model.

The second route values ambitious 2030 segment sales: $20 billion Space at six times sales, $65 billion Connectivity at twelve times, and $150 billion SI at seven times. The undiscounted enterprise total is $1.950 trillion. Discount it four periods at eleven percent, add current net cash and divide by current shares to obtain $98.35. Sales multiples already assume attractive mature margins; no extra value is added for the same cash flows under a separate AI or Starship label. The third route assumes $60 billion of annual owner earnings in 2031, values that at thirty times and discounts five periods at eleven percent. It yields $82.42 per share.

The reverse calculation is tougher. At the reference price and current shares, enterprise value is approximately $1.956 trillion. With the same thirty-times terminal multiple and eleven-percent return requirement, the market needs about $109.9 billion of annual owner earnings in 2031. That is not impossible, but it is substantially above our already optimistic $60 billion case. Our bear/base/bull values of $40/$85/$180 receive 25%/50%/25% probabilities, producing $97.50 probability-weighted value. The bullish case explicitly allows execution to outrun our models. Even then, the weighted value remains below the reference price.

What management must demonstrate next

The CFO emphasizes capital efficiency, returns on invested capital and an investment-grade balance sheet. The balance sheet gives that strategy time, while quarter free cash flow shows that the returns are still being built. We grade execution positively for the first commercial Starship deployment and Connectivity growth, but hold capital-efficiency judgment open. A bigger capex budget or a larger nameplate compute number alone cannot earn a favorable return-on-capital grade. We need revenue retention, utilization, maintenance spending and durable cash margins.

Cursor expands the application layer and brings a real contributed-revenue figure. It also introduces large purchase-accounting and retention-award obligations. An integration score should ask whether the acquired customers remain, whether software economics improve, and whether cross-selling generates returns above the cost of the shares issued. The August completion removes the old pending-deal uncertainty. It does not erase execution risk or make acquired revenue directly comparable with an earlier standalone quarter.

Governance warrants a clear discount in confidence, even without an arbitrary numeric haircut. The prospectus gives Class B ten votes per share against one for Class A, with separate director-election rights. Together they reinforce why outside shareholders should demand transparent terms, durable cash evidence and consistent reporting definitions. The strongest bull and bear cases

The bull case deserves substance. Connectivity already has a sizeable high-margin base and the consumer network keeps growing. SI revenue accelerated sharply even after subtracting the disclosed Cursor contribution. Commercial Starship flights could reduce internal deployment cost and expand usable network capacity. If these reinforcing businesses turn infrastructure spending into durable customer cash flows, the long-run earnings base could surpass our forecasts. A thirty-times multiple on a much larger owner-earnings number could justify a price well above our base value.

The bear case is that investors capitalize capacity and adjusted earnings before the assets have demonstrated lifecycle returns. GPUs and data centers can become obsolete quickly; satellites require replacement; launch systems require development and regulatory access. Equity compensation lowers the ownership claim on any eventual cash. Cloud customers may concentrate revenue or front-load payments in ways that look favorable early in a deployment cycle. The share price is particularly sensitive when most of a DCF’s value is beyond the explicit forecast horizon.

Our stance is therefore a valuation SELL with moderate conviction, not a prediction of a near-term collapse and not a judgment that the technology lacks value. We would change the call if evidence raised sustainable owner earnings toward the market-implied hurdle or if the price offered a meaningful discount to a conservatively updated model. The current cash reserve reduces financing stress but does not eliminate execution, governance or valuation risk. Keep the operational and market-price questions distinct when interpreting subsequent headlines.
