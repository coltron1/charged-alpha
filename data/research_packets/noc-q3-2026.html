Segments and backlog

Aeronautics generated the largest incremental contribution. Sales rose $433 M to $3.575 B, and operating income increased $72 M to $377 M. F-35 contributed a $195 M sales increase, alongside B-21 and B-2 sustainment. The release attributes some improvement to material-cost timing and favorable contract-margin adjustments, so the whole increase should not be treated as a permanent step-up in underlying unit demand. Contract accounting recognizes progress and revises estimates; it is not the same as counting finished aircraft deliveries.

Defense sales grew 12% to $2.307 B, including a $180 M Sentinel increase, but its margin declined to 10.8% as the mix shifted toward cost-type work. That trade-off matters: a larger contract book can create more revenue without proportionate margin expansion. Mission Systems sales increased 2%, while operating profit fell 3%. The comparison includes a favorable $68 M estimate-at-completion adjustment in the prior-year restricted microelectronics portfolio. Space revenue rose 2% and operating profit 5%; restricted-program growth offset lower Commercial Resupply Service mission sales related to launch timing.

Net awards totaled $16.4 B, or 1.47 times quarterly sales on a computed basis. Total backlog reached $109.597 B, up 14.5% from year-end 2025. Yet only $50.095 B is funded; $59.502 B, or 54.3%, is unfunded. Backlog supports the growth runway but is not cash in the bank, a guaranteed delivery date or a margin guarantee. The measure excludes unexercised options and indefinite-delivery contracts until exercised or awarded. Those definition limits are why our signposts track both funded conversion and operating performance.

For the historical charts, the 2024 segment figures use the recast comparisons disclosed in 2025, rather than mixing the original organization with the current segment structure. Consolidated revenue is unchanged by that presentation change. Intersegment sales are eliminated to reach consolidated revenue, and intersegment profit is eliminated to reach segment operating income. The dataset retains those eliminations and verifies both sums quarter by quarter.

Profit bridge

Segment operating income of $1.345 B is not GAAP operating income. Add the $15 M FAS/CAS pension adjustment and subtract $91 M of unallocated corporate expense to reach $1.269 B. Corporate expense includes $13 M of acquisition-related amortization and depreciation and $78 M of other corporate costs. Below operating income, the $167 M non-operating pension benefit slightly exceeds $158 M interest expense; other income contributes $20 M. These items explain why a valuation based only on segment margin can overstate the simplicity of shareholder earnings.

The tax rate fell to 13.4% from 16.9%, principally because of higher research credits. This is a legitimate earnings benefit, but it does not establish faster cash-generating growth. The net FAS/CAS pension adjustment contributed a reported $0.96 per share after tax, versus $1.06 a year earlier. That recurring pension accounting contribution differs from year-end mark-to-market adjustments, which management excludes from its guided EPS measure. We do not remove both categories indiscriminately or describe all pension effects as cash.

Share reduction supported historical EPS, but recent repurchases slowed substantially. Nine-month 2026 buybacks were $68 M versus $1.168 B in the prior year, while dividends increased to $1.035 B from $964 M. We use actual quarter-end shares of 142.064 M in per-share valuation and weighted-average diluted shares only for the reported quarterly EPS context. Those denominators serve different purposes and should not be silently interchanged.

The cash baseline is intentionally conservative. Company guidance includes equipment-sale proceeds, and the release defines why it treats them as adjusted free cash flow. We remove the known $371 M from the $3.3 B midpoint to test cash generation without that transaction. It is possible that equipment transfers recur or free capacity in a way that deserves more value; in that case this model understates sustainable cash. Conversely, weaker working-capital collection or future program losses could make even $2.929 B too optimistic. Neither possibility is resolved by the pending call.

At $510.65, the same discounted-cash-flow setup requires approximately 13.3% annual equity cash-flow growth for five years, versus 6% in our base route. This is a computed reverse-model result, not a company forecast. It holds the 9% discount rate,2.5% terminal growth and share count constant. A lower required return or a higher starting cash baseline could support today’s price with less growth. The point is to expose what the valuation asks of operations, rather than presenting a single intrinsic-value number as certain.

The scenario band is deliberately wider than the base-route rounding. A stress case of $22 normalized EPS at 15 times yields $330; a successful execution case of $31 at 20 times yields $620. The 25%/50%/25% weighting gives $465, still below the prior close. Our 20% margin-of-safety screen is $364. We would also reconsider the call at a higher price if sustainable cash evidence materially improved: a valuation threshold is a conditional decision rule, not an instruction to buy mechanically.
