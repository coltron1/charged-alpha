The tape — strong operations, demanding ownership price

Our call is AVOID for fresh buying, with three-out-of-five conviction and High uncertainty. The rounded $210 fair-value anchor comes from three separately specified valuation routes, not from a target-price consensus. Against the October 27 completed close of $270.79, it implies 22.45% downside. That is an analytical comparison, not a prediction that the share price must reach our estimate on a particular date. The much wider $150–$300 scenario range recognizes how sensitive steel profits are to pricing, utilization and investment returns.

The bullish evidence deserves to lead the discussion. Nucor has just generated $1.607 billion of quarterly cash after capital expenditure and is bringing major capacity into service. Its balance sheet offers liquidity and investment-grade financing. An investor who expects these improvements to persist can reasonably reach a higher valuation. Our disagreement is about how much of the latest earnings pace should be treated as durable, not whether the reported cash exists.

Our prior verified public episode covered Q1 2026 under an EPS-beat headline. The available public catalog verifies the title and identity but not a prior fair-value number or a numerical signpost table. We therefore do not invent a previous rating, claim a successful target, or calculate a return from an undocumented entry price. The present packet establishes explicit checkpoints that can be graded in the next edition. Its focus shifts from the size of an EPS surprise to cash ownership and the price paid for the cycle.

The print — cash outpaces the headline debate

Third-quarter sales reached $11.249 billion, up 32.02% from $8.521 billion a year earlier. Net income attributable to Nucor shareholders was $1.319 billion, compared with $607 million. Diluted EPS rose from $2.63 to $5.80. The quarter is a clear operating improvement: both realized selling prices and external shipment volumes are higher than a year ago, while the share count is modestly lower. It would be misleading to reduce that result to financial engineering.

The estimate comparison is less dramatic than the growth comparison. FMP records $5.84 of expected EPS against $5.80 actual, a small miss, while revenue exceeds its $10.836 billion estimate by about 3.81%. Neither comparison defines intrinsic value. Expectations move, accounting conventions differ and a cyclical company can report excellent current results at a demanding stock price. We preserve reported EPS throughout the historical table rather than replacing selected quarters with a provider-adjusted number.

This convention matters for Q2. The company removes a $46 million after-tax Helion investment gain, equivalent to $0.20 EPS, to show adjusted earnings of $4.84 versus reported $5.04. Separately, $130 million of procurement refunds reduced Q2 steel-mill costs. Those refunds did not recur in Q3, and the company’s Helion-only adjusted EPS does not remove them. We do not call $4.84 fully normalized operating earnings. Nor do we invent the refund’s exact EPS effect without a disclosed tax and ownership bridge.

Nine-month sales are $31.142 billion and parent net income is $3.218 billion. The business is operating above the weak 2025 base, so growth percentages are large. Our nine-quarter history shows both the downturn and the rebound. It is the level of sustainable cash after new capacity, maintenance and working capital that must justify a long-term valuation, rather than the easiest year-over-year comparison.

Segments — price strength outruns sequential volume

Steel mills produced $1.788 billion of pretax segment earnings, versus $1.556 billion in Q2 and $793 million a year earlier. External mill tons were essentially flat sequentially at 5.662 million, while the external average selling price rose from $1,145 to $1,233 per net ton. That combination makes pricing the central sequential driver. Total mill shipments rose, but include internal transfers; they are not interchangeable with sales to outside customers.

Steel products contributed $465 million of pretax earnings, up from $353 million sequentially. Its external tons increased to 1.363 million and average selling price reached $2,576 per ton. Raw materials earned $105 million, down from $146 million. The portfolio provides multiple earnings sources but does not eliminate cyclicality: several businesses remain tied to construction, manufacturing and steel spreads. Corporate costs and intersegment eliminations of $491 million must still be deducted before consolidated pretax income reaches $1.867 billion.

The operating tables report selling prices per net ton and scrap costs per gross ton. We show the series separately and do not subtract them as though they were a directly comparable dollar margin. A real conversion analysis would also require metallic yield, energy, labor, product mix and other production costs. Likewise, multiplying rounded tons by rounded ASP produces only an approximation; it is not substituted for exact reported segment revenue. The annual external revenue chart is clearly labeled fiscal 2025 context.

Mill utilization was 89%, down from 91% in Q2 but above 85% a year earlier. This is healthy activity, yet leaves a different message from uninterrupted acceleration. New West Virginia capacity and the Berkeley galvanizing line create an opportunity to earn more from the investment base. They also introduce ramp costs, yield risk and possible market supply effects. Our next review asks whether pricing and customer demand remain strong as those assets become productive.

Profit bridge — distinguish the business from its owners

The quarter begins with $2.326 billion of gross profit after $8.923 billion of product costs. Subtract $444 million of marketing, administrative and other expense, add $9 million of equity-method earnings, subtract $17 million of impairments and $7 million of net interest expense, and pretax profit is $1.867 billion. Tax consumes $394 million. Another $154 million belongs to noncontrolling interests, leaving $1.319 billion attributable to Nucor stockholders. Each step describes a different economic claim.

Minority interests are not a rounding detail. They take 10.46% of quarterly consolidated net earnings before that allocation. The consolidated cash-flow statement also begins before minority allocation and includes the cash of controlled businesses with outside owners. We therefore distinguish consolidated free cash flow from cash potentially available to parent shareholders. Subtracting actual minority distributions provides a useful ownership crosscheck, although it is not a complete GAAP measure of free cash flow to equity.

The latest quarter contains $124 million of pre-operating and startup expense. That burden could decline as projects ramp, which supports the bull case. We do not add it all back to our valuation automatically. A business that continues investing can incur repeated startup costs, and the new assets must prove returns after depreciation, maintenance and operating costs. Removing today’s expense without allowing for tomorrow’s reinvestment would favor the model twice.

The annual bridge is different from the quarterly snapshot. In 2025, parent income was $1.744 billion and consolidated pretax earnings were $2.568 billion. The Q3 2026 annualized EPS pace of $23.20 is far above both 2025’s $7.52 and trailing-four-quarter EPS of $15.71. Such dispersion is normal for a cyclical business but makes a single-quarter multiple unreliable. Our normalized $18 EPS assumption lies above the trailing result while remaining below the current annualized quarter and next-year consensus.

Earnings quality — separate cash recovery from cash timing

Nine-month operating cash flow increased from $2.435 billion to $4.522 billion. Capital expenditure fell from $2.620 billion to $1.861 billion. Together those changes move free cash flow from negative $185 million to positive $2.661 billion: $2.087 billion comes from more operating cash and $759 million from lower investment spending. Both improvements are real, but they have different persistence. A lower construction bill cannot fall indefinitely, while operating cash remains exposed to the steel cycle.

The third-quarter cash calculation subtracts first-half totals from nine-month totals. Operating cash is $2.236 billion, capex $629 million and FCF $1.607 billion. We label that as computed rather than presenting a nine-month total as a quarter. The same method is applied throughout the series, including a separately collected Q2 2024 release to derive Q3 2024 correctly. Fourth-quarter residuals can carry small differences caused by issuer rounding when annual statements change presentation units.

Working capital is mixed. Nine-month receivables absorbed $1.203 billion and inventory absorbed $560 million. Payables added $485 million, wage accruals $458 million and tax balances $167 million. The resulting cash total is not simply earnings converted at a constant rate. Some support from accrued obligations reverses when paid. Ending-balance receivable and inventory days are useful directional checks, but seasonality and a changing price level limit what they can establish. They are not evidence of improper revenue recognition.

The scorecard uses comparable ownership bases: operating cash divided by consolidated earnings before minorities, and separate minority leakage. Stock compensation is modest relative to sales, but remains an economic cost. Current-quarter SBC is $38 million, derived from $129 million nine-month less $91 million first-half expense. We show cash FCF without pretending it automatically charges shareholders for dilution. The three annual cash observations reveal why the latest quarterly cash number needs a cycle-aware valuation.

Balance sheet — liquidity buys time, not immunity

Nucor holds $3.467 billion of cash and $173 million of short-term investments. Against that sit $7.093 billion of debt and finance-lease obligations, including the current portion. Net debt is therefore $3.453 billion. The company also has an undrawn $2.25 billion revolving credit facility expiring in March 2030. This is meaningful liquidity for a capital-intensive cyclical company, and current investment-grade ratings support access to funding. It does not convert the business into a net-cash investment.

At the $270.79 reference price and 226.876 million spot shares, market capitalization is $61.436 billion. Adding net debt and $1.280 billion of minority book equity yields a $66.169 billion enterprise-value proxy. The minority adjustment matters because our EBITDA and cash routes value consolidated operations. Book minority value is only an approximation to fair value, so the bridge has an explicit limitation. The parent-EPS route avoids this particular adjustment because earnings already belong to Nucor shareholders.

Property, plant and equipment is $16.103 billion, compared with $15.306 billion at year-end. Goodwill and other intangibles total $6.983 billion. These assets represent substantial prior investment, but their carrying amounts are not a guaranteed liquidation floor. Return on invested capital depends on utilization, product economics and future maintenance. New productive capacity can improve earnings or add depreciation and competitive supply before adequate returns emerge.

Our debt convention includes finance leases and uses the reported net interest line when reconciling operating earnings. It does not add all operating lease liabilities to enterprise value without changing the earnings denominator. Nor does it add available revolver capacity to cash as if already owned. Strong liquidity supports the ability to weather a downturn; valuation still requires an estimate of what the assets can earn across that downturn.

Valuation — normalize the cycle before choosing the multiple

The earnings route uses $18 of normalized parent EPS and a 12-times multiple, producing $216 per share. The earnings assumption is above trailing EPS of $15.71, below the latest quarterly annualized pace of $23.20, and below FMP’s approximately $20.74 next-year consensus. It allows a stronger business than 2025 without assuming the current quarter repeats indefinitely. The multiple is our judgment for a capital-intensive cyclical producer, not a quoted peer median or an issuer promise.

The enterprise route assumes $7 billion of normalized consolidated EBITDA at 7.5 times. Subtracting $3.453 billion of net debt and $1.280 billion of minority book equity, then dividing by 226.876 million spot shares, yields $210.54. The EBITDA starting point is below the $9.116 billion annualized Q3 pace and the provider’s next-year $8.761 billion consensus. This is a deliberate cycle haircut. Because the metric includes consolidated operations, omitting minority value would overstate the parent’s equity claim. The book-value proxy remains a model limitation.

The cash route models $3.8 billion of first forward-year unlevered cash, followed by 3% growth through year five, a 10% discount rate and 2% terminal growth. Subtract the same net debt and minority proxy once. The result is $195.42. This first cash assumption exceeds trailing accounting FCF of $2.658 billion and allows lower construction spending plus improving operations. It is an analyst FCFF assumption after reinvestment, working capital and economic compensation costs; historical OCF minus capex is only a crosscheck because it includes interest and cash-tax timing. We do not identify the accounting measure as unlevered cash by definition.

These routes place a practical base around $210, with a $195–$216 cluster and a wider scenario envelope. Their agreement does not make the risk small because all three depend on the same industry cycle. At $270.79, the cash framework would require about 12.45% annual cash growth after its first forward year, holding the discount rate, terminal growth and ownership bridge fixed. That is a conditional reverse-model result, not a unique market forecast. High uncertainty calls for a 25% discount to our base value, or $157.50, with operating assumptions still intact; a lower price alone cannot repair a broken thesis.

The difference is not resolved by averaging our target with the Street. Management — capital spending must become capital returns

The investment cycle has a tangible milestone: first heat at the West Virginia sheet mill in September and startup of the second galvanizing line at Berkeley. These are necessary steps toward earning a return on construction spending, but not sufficient proof of mature economics. Our scorecard separates operational progress from a claim that all project value has already been realized. Startup costs of $124 million in Q3 remain above the year-earlier $103 million.

The annual report initially estimated approximately $2.5 billion of 2026 capital expenditure. Nine-month spending of $1.861 billion is consistent with a substantial reduction from 2025’s $3.422 billion, but we do not treat the February estimate as a freshly reaffirmed October forecast. Lower construction spending helps free cash now. The enduring question is whether the completed capacity generates attractive returns after maintenance and normal working-capital requirements.

Capital returns also increased. Nucor repurchased 2.03 million shares in Q3 at an average $247.04 and returned $629 million through repurchases and dividends. Nine-month repurchases of $976 million plus dividends of $386 million total $1.362 billion. Buybacks reduce the ownership denominator, but they create value only when the purchase price is attractive relative to durable value. The fact that the stock later traded higher is not by itself proof that the repurchase price was optimal. Our valuation does not pre-credit future retirements.

The operating culture includes performance-linked compensation and approximately 33,000 employees in the latest annual report, versus 32,700 in 2024 and 32,000 in 2023. Flexible compensation can cushion a downturn, but does not eliminate fixed plant costs or the need for safe operation. The annual report states effective internal control and carries a PricewaterhouseCoopers audit. We distinguish those verified governance facts from unsupported claims about management ownership, customer concentration or guaranteed future returns.

Risks — the bull case can be right before the valuation is safe

The strongest bull case is a more durable domestic steel earnings environment combined with a falling construction bill. New assets could improve product mix, service and efficiency, while better realized prices persist longer than our normalized assumptions allow. The current quarter already demonstrates substantial cash generation. If consolidated EBITDA sustainably exceeds $8 billion and cash conversion supports it, the sensitivity grid moves toward the current share price even before more aggressive multiples. Our $300 bull scenario recognizes that possibility rather than dismissing it.

The central bear risk is simultaneous pressure on prices and fixed-cost absorption. Mill external volume is flat sequentially even as realized prices rise. If demand softens or new supply meets slower orders, a modest decline in price can have an outsized effect on earnings. Higher maintenance, energy or scrap costs could worsen the squeeze. We do not need to forecast a crisis to question a valuation that capitalizes much of the current improvement; ordinary normalization may be enough.

The next risk is execution. Major projects can consume working capital and incur startup expense longer than expected. Lower capex after construction does not mean reinvestment needs disappear. Accounting depreciation may also lag the cash outlays that created the assets, so an EBITDA multiple can conceal the cost of replacing capacity. Our cash model is a counterweight, but it still depends on an assumed reinvestment requirement rather than a guaranteed company commitment.

Trade policy, import flows, energy supply, labor and safety events can alter the cycle abruptly. We do not anchor value to a permanently favorable policy regime. Acquisitions and minority-owned subsidiaries add integration and ownership complexity. A strong balance sheet makes a severe funding problem less likely, but cannot prevent an unattractive purchase price from producing poor returns. Our position is therefore about fresh-entry discipline, with explicit conditions under which stronger source evidence would justify a higher fair value.

Catalysts — test the cash base as the new mills ramp

The next quarterly report should test management’s expectation for earnings generally in line with or modestly above Q3. Our January 2027 review window focuses on realized mill pricing, utilization and seasonal volume. We want to know whether better prices remain sufficient to offset costs and reduced shipments. The date is a monitoring window, not a confirmed issuer appointment. A favorable headline without operating detail would not settle the valuation question.

Full-year cash flow provides a second test. We set a green checkpoint of at least $3.5 billion of accounting FCF, and separately at least $3 billion after minority distributions. Those are analyst thresholds, not management guidance. The ownership adjustment prevents a strong consolidated result from obscuring cash claimed by outside shareholders. If annual cash misses those levels because working capital reverses, the model’s first forward cash assumption deserves another review.

By the first-quarter 2027 review, we expect better evidence on the duration of startup expense and the economics of capacity entering service.
