Stock price and investment thesis

Charged Alpha stock call: AVOID at $108.72, the November 13, 2026 regular-session close. Base present fair value: $48.28. Present scenario range: $4.25–$97.53. Research cut-off: November 15, 2026. This is an assessment of the shares at a dated price, alongside a constructive view of the potential usefulness of fast inference.

Cerebras is moving from selling AI systems toward supplying high-speed inference through its own cloud and partners. Demand is advancing: Q3 GAAP cloud and services revenue grew 264.7%, and the company reported $26.1bn of remaining performance obligations. Faster inference can improve user experience, enable applications with demanding response times, and spread infrastructure costs over more useful output.

The investment question is whether those benefits create durable cash per share. A large contract requires delivered capacity, equipment, hosting, power and customer acceptance. Some contract revenue passes through to infrastructure providers; some service consideration repays customer financing; customer warrants and employee awards transfer part of the upside. A software-like revenue multiple without these items would misstate the economics.

Our main model follows the equity cash waterfall, including the initial cash buckets, deployment spending, customer-loan settlements and share claims. It allows the 2027 tripling ambition and strong subsequent growth. Its lower value is not based on perpetuating the present GAAP loss. It reflects the cost of producing that growth and the portion retained by current shareholders.

Business, industry and customer demand

This is a capital-intensive AI inference platform with proprietary hardware. Its competitive advantage must survive comparison on latency, throughput, model compatibility, reliability, customer integration and total economic cost. A benchmark showing faster generation does not by itself establish greater realized revenue or a lower all-in cost after equipment, energy and hosting.

Cerebras offers hardware, dedicated capacity and on-demand services. Dedicated contracts are generally take-or-pay, which improves revenue visibility if the company delivers the promised capacity and meets contract requirements. On-demand economics also depend on usage and pricing. Large customers have bargaining power; the filing describes preferential terms, manufacturing-capacity commitments and milestones in hyperscaler arrangements. New distribution can expand reach without automatically guaranteeing attractive margins.

The company says heterogeneous disaggregated inference can increase tokens delivered per data center. We treat that as a mechanism to investigate, not a one-for-one revenue forecast. Actual monetization depends on workload mix, quality, utilization, pricing and competitive responses. GPU supply, power availability, site commissioning, wafer supply and next-generation system delivery can all constrain deployment.

The largest two customers represented 38% and 34% of Q3 revenue. One customer accounted for 64% of receivables. These concentrations make delivery, collection and commercial terms important even as the customer roster expands.

The OpenAI arrangement commits 750 MW of inference capacity over a multiyear rollout and includes an option for an additional 1.25 GW. Optional capacity is not booked as certain deployment in this report. Separately, Cerebras said it had secured 700 gross MW of data-center capacity for delivery by the end of 2027. These figures refer to different obligations and definitions; they are neither added together nor represented as currently operating capacity.

The $26.1bn RPO includes deferred revenue, expected future invoicing and estimated variable pass-through amounts. Management expects roughly 33% to be recognized over the initial 24months to September 2028,44% in months 25–48, and the remaining 23% thereafter. Timing can change. Multiplying these percentages gives approximately $8.613bn/$11.484bn/$6.003bn; these are portions of the current obligation balance, not annual Core revenue forecasts or free cash flow.

Pass-through infrastructure costs have minimal net margin and are excluded from Core reporting. The filing excludes some future pass-through amounts for capacity not yet associated with recognized lease arrangements. Therefore RPO and Core sales are not interchangeable. The base model's Core revenue path should not be presented as a complete reconciliation of future GAAP revenue to the current RPO.

Cash earning power and deployment model

The model uses FCFE and a 13% required equity return. It is deliberately organized around the funding and ownership mechanics rather than a generic enterprise-value multiple. No net-debt subtraction is applied after debt settlement has already been charged in equity cash flow.

The November 13 opening cash estimate starts with September 30 unrestricted cash and investments of $7,360.778m, less an author estimate of $650m interim cash use, leaving $6,710.778m. Restricted cash of $444.042m remains separately tracked and available only for permitted infrastructure. We assume $100m is used within stub-period capex and the balance during 2027. It is not separately added to distributable excess cash. The interim cash use is an estimate, not a reported November balance.

The remaining 2026 stub includes $5m cash EBITDA,$350m capex,$30m working-capital investment and $60m loan settlement, plus modeled cash/loan interest. These are explicit author assumptions. From 2027 onward, cloud and hardware sales and cash gross margins are separate. Corporate cash costs include research, selling and administration, excluding employee compensation paid through shares because new share grants are separately modeled.

Physical depreciation is modeled at 14% of economic sales as an installed-equipment proxy, while gross capex is forecast separately. Working-capital investment is 6% of the change in revenue. These author approximations are exposed for sensitivity; they are not claimed as issuer guidance. The company’s leases and power costs remain inside cash unit economics. We do not subtract the multiyear $8.5bn lease-payment headline a second time.

Cash taxes use 21% on positive operating EBIT, without capitalizing an uncertain NOL asset. Unrestricted cash earns an assumed 3% pretax yield; restricted funds earn no modeled interest. Interest income is taxed at 21%. These assumptions differ from quarterly reported tax accounting and are normalized forecasts.

A $500m minimum operating cash balance is maintained. Base and bull return cash above that balance from 2030 after the early buildout, while the weaker bear retains cash until 2036. These distributions are valuation-policy assumptions, not announced dividends or buybacks. No scenario requires additional external equity under these particular paths. The calculation would raise equity at a stated 50% discount to the current quote if cash fell below the reserve; this is an explicit stress rule, not a committed financing source.

Independent capital cross-check

Reported equity of $9,158.913m less $1,091.974m of noncash customer-warrant assets gives $8,066.939m of adjusted accounting capital. Dividing by the existing 305.84856m non-PRSU claim basis gives approximately $26.38 per claim. This uses independent balance-sheet evidence rather than a second DCF with the same revenue forecast.

This is neither liquidation value nor a guaranteed downside floor. Equipment can become obsolete or be deployed at poor returns, while valuable intellectual property is incompletely reflected in book assets. The calculation distinguishes currently recorded net capital from the future franchise premium investors pay. Our DCF is above that capital check; the stock requires substantially more future value. The cross-check does not independently validate our cloud growth assumptions.

Liquidity, customer financing and ownership

The loan balance of $829.082m is settled through $60m of stub-period credits,$650m in 2027 and $119.082m in 2028. These reduce cash available because service value used to repay financing does not also arrive as fresh cash. The loan is not then deducted again as an enterprise-to-equity adjustment.

The 6% stated interest is modeled as an economic settlement burden, with tax relief only when modeled operating earnings are positive. The contract can permit waiver or deemed payment, and reported financing-component interest is noncash and increases deferred revenue. The forecast revenue basis excludes that accounting uplift; we do not add imputed financing revenue as a new cash inflow. More favorable settlement treatment would improve cash compared with this assumption. The latest contractual maturity is December 31,2032; the model assumes earlier settlement through service activity, so no 2030 refinancing is required.

Actual November 5 common shares are 160.084168m Class A,69.203688m Class B and 13.715508m Class N, totaling 243.003364m. The modeled existing non-PRSU claim bound adds 19.503m options,17.139m RSUs,0.095m ESPP claims,23.411518m remaining OpenAI warrants and 2.696678m AWS warrants, producing 305.84856m claims.

This is a full-face, zero-exercise-proceeds bound for non-PRSU claims only, not an all-claims upper bound or an assertion that all awards immediately vest. The remaining OpenAI warrant includes optional-capacity tranches not currently deemed probable; AWS warrants have a $100 strike and vesting conditions. Already-exercised OpenAI and G42 shares are included in common and are not counted again. Early-exercised shares already in the actual common count are not added again.

The accounting customer-warrant asset is not added to cash value. Existing customer-warrant dilution replaces a second economic deduction for its contra-revenue amortization. Employee awards likewise enter ownership once. New employee grants increase the share count 1.5% annually from 2027, and the model does not also charge their cash-equivalent compensation in expenses. The continuing dilution assumption persists after the explicit forecast.

Nine million executive PRSUs sit separately from this basis. Their three equal 3m tranches require $75bn/$150bn/$250bn market-value hurdles, measured using the 90-trading-day trailing price times Class A shares, along with service conditions. Unvested awards expire on the ninth IPO anniversary in May 2035. The central paths assume no settlement before expiry; this is conditional, not a claim the awards cannot vest. Base and bear total-company modeled values remain below the first hurdle through 2035. In the bull, sufficient conversion of Class B/N shares toClass A and market pricing could permit the first tranche before expiry. Annual forecasts do not resolve the actual 90-day condition.

The calculation therefore separately tests 3m shares issued in 2035 with operating economics unchanged. Present values become $4.21/$48.00/$96.84, versus central $4.25/$48.28/$97.53. A maximum 9m claim stress in 2030 gives $4.14/$46.98/$94.90; that is a dilution exposure test, not a forecast that all market hurdles are achieved. No additional SBC cash expense is charged for those grants. The distinction is important:305.849m is a non-PRSU claim basis, while the separate annex shows contingent performance ownership.

Exercise proceeds or fewer vested customer warrants would increase value. These uncertainties should remain visible; none of the quantified claim cases closes the base gap to $108.72.

What changes the investment thesis

Evidence that strengthens the case includes on-time capacity delivery, better realized revenue per dollar of installed equipment, rising cash margins after hosting and power, lower capital intensity, broader paying customers, and contained employee/customer dilution. Evidence that weakens it includes deployment ahead of demand, deteriorating commercial terms, customer collection problems, delayed systems or higher capital needs than the cash forecast supports.

The current quarter provides genuine demand evidence. It does not settle the eventual return on the physical buildout. AVOID at $108.72 reflects that price-and-risk balance. A lower price or stronger evidence of durable cash productivity can change the call.

The important reconciliations run in both directions. Revenue quality begins with the company’s GAAP-to-Core bridge. Reported revenue falls when customer-warrant assets amortize, even though that amortization does not consume current cash. Adding it back is appropriate for operating activity only if the shareholder ownership transfer is captured elsewhere. Conversely, a pass-through billing can increase reported sales with very little incremental profit. Removing pass-through amounts from both sales and costs should not remove a large positive cash margin that never existed.

Funding quality begins with the loan and restricted-cash records. An undrawn revolver is contingent financing capacity, not an asset that can be added to the share price. Restricted balances can finance eligible construction, so excluding them from distributable cash does not mean they have no economic utility. The model consumes those funds within the capex schedule. It does not subtract both the restricted balance and the project expenditure as separate penalties. The existing customer loan is discharged through a reduction of cash receipts, so no additional end-of-model debt subtraction is needed once the balance reaches zero.

The share denominator also needs a dated bridge. Quarterly weighted-average shares of 238.885m are useful for reported earnings per share, but they are not the current ownership count after share issuances and conversions. We use the November 5 actual total of 243.003m before adding unexercised claims. A conversion betweenClass A andClass B changes voting and hurdle measurement but does not create a new economic share by itself. Net settlement, withholding and award forfeiture can change the eventual count. The full-face non-PRSU basis exposes that uncertainty rather than disguising a future dilution assumption inside a rounded multiple.

Capital productivity should be measured over deployment cohorts. An asset commissioned late in the year can absorb cash before generating a full year of revenue. That does not automatically make the project uneconomic. However, delayed commissioning extends the period during which the company bears equipment, lease and financing exposure without the expected receipts. Our explicit capex path and cash reserve provide a place to change this timing. An investor should not repair a delayed rollout merely by increasing a distant terminal multiple while leaving near-term funding unchanged.

A credible upside revision could come from more billable service delivered by the same installed equipment, rather than requiring every new dollar of revenue to carry identical capital intensity. That is why the market-implied solve increases cloud monetization while holding the physical capex program fixed. It represents an efficiency question grounded in the company’s technical proposition. The separate operating sensitivity links volume and capex, which asks a different question: what if the company simply builds a larger or smaller version of the same economics? Keeping these two tests separate prevents technological efficiency and physical expansion from being treated as interchangeable.

The scenario outcomes should not be mistaken for investment certainty. A13% required return is a judgment about risk, not a guaranteed return an investor receives. A higher required return reduces present value without predicting lower future revenue. A lower actual risk profile could support a lower rate, but it needs evidence such as dependable delivery, diversified contracts, better cash conversion or more predictable capital needs. The sensitivity tables make that dependence visible. They should not be used to select whichever combination best matches a desired stock call.

Finally, the capital-return schedule is an analytical convention. A company might retain surplus funds, repurchase shares, pay dividends or undertake new projects instead. Those choices can create or destroy value. The model distributes cash above a defined reserve after the initial construction phase so that returns to current owners can be calculated explicitly. If management retains the cash instead, the valuation should reflect the return earned on those additional investments and the resulting ownership claims. Moving a dividend date without examining what happens to the cash would be an incomplete update.
