The engine is government solutions

Kratos Government Solutions supplied $397.2 million of revenue, up 52.5%, including 35.8% organic growth. Its adjusted EBITDA reached $46.3 million, compared with $24.4 million. Hypersonic and rocket work, turbine technologies, microwave products and space-related programs all matter to the investment case. The largest named growth rate was 149% for Defense Rocket Systems, but that is a business-line growth rate, not the growth rate of consolidated revenue. We do not assign an undisclosed revenue base or profit contribution to that figure.

Unmanned Systems moved in the opposite direction: revenue fell to $74.6 million from $87.2 million, a 14.4% decline. Last year included a $16.1 million international Valkyrie shipment. That comparison explains part of the decline, but does not turn the current quarter into broad drone acceleration. Segment adjusted EBITDA fell from $6.4 million to $4.6 million. Book-to-bill was only 0.8, compared with 2.5 in KGS. The company’s overall defense opportunity therefore should not be reduced to a single drone story.

Consolidated backlog rose from about $2.084 billion to $2.650 billion, up 27.2%. Approximately $2.067 billion was funded and $582.9 million unfunded. The 10-Q expects 16% of remaining performance obligations to become revenue during the rest of 2026 and another 50% during 2027. Those expectations give useful timing context, but they are neither cash receipts nor a guarantee against cancellation or schedule changes. The $15.5 billion bid pipeline is a still earlier category: opportunities being pursued, not awarded backlog.

The demand evidence is unusually strong for KGS. The key underwriting problem shifts to conversion: can contracts become delivered systems, recognized profit and collected cash without requiring disproportionately more shareholder funding? Large prime-integrator opportunities can increase revenue while reducing percentage margins because more supplier and subcontractor content passes through the company. Management explicitly discusses that possibility. A margin forecast based only on rising production volume would miss the effect of contract mix.

From adjusted EBITDA to the net loss

The reconciliation starts with the $0.2 million net loss and removes $8.7 million of net interest income, then adds tax, depreciation, stock compensation and other disclosed adjustments to reach $50.9 million of adjusted EBITDA. The largest additions include $18.1 million of stock compensation, $14.0 million of depreciation, $11.0 million of intangible amortization and $12.3 million of amortization of capitalized contract and development costs. These categories have different economic meanings. They should not all be described as one-time acquisition charges.

Interest income is a genuine economic benefit of the cash balance. It is not evidence that the operating business is self-funding. The quarterly tax expense of $4.8 million exceeds pretax income of $4.6 million; the resulting 104% effective rate is mathematically correct but not a useful normalized tax assumption. Our longer-run operating-earnings route assumes 21% tax, explicitly as an analytical input. We do not extrapolate the quarterly effective rate or apply an untaxed adjusted profit indefinitely.

The current release contains a small segment-table inconsistency: Unmanned operating loss is shown as $0.9 million and total operating loss as $4.8 million in that table. The consolidated statement and 10-Q give $4.9 million; the 10-Q segment note gives a $1.0 million Unmanned loss. We use the 10-Q GAAP values and retain management’s reported adjusted EBITDA. The difference is immaterial to valuation, but preserving the distinction prevents a false claim that every table ties exactly.

Unbilled receivables of $454.3 million are about 70% of total billed and unbilled receivables. Over nine months, growth in unbilled receivables consumed $153.6 million of operating cash. Contract accounting can recognize progress before billing milestones permit collection. That is not, by itself, evidence of fictitious revenue. It does mean that rapid reported growth and positive adjusted EBITDA can coexist with a funding requirement. Contract milestones, collections and aging deserve more attention than the headline backlog alone.

The receivable and inventory day measures are deliberately simple diagnostics. They use ending balances divided by the current quarter’s revenue or cost of sales, multiplied by 91. Acquisitions and seasonality make them unsuitable as stand-alone fraud or efficiency scores. We do not publish a Beneish or Altman number without a complete, comparable input set. The useful question is whether collection improves as projects mature, while inventory bought for anticipated production is actually sold rather than left carrying capital costs.

Basic weighted shares increased 10.7% year over year. Revenue per basic weighted share therefore increased about 22.6%, below the 35.7% headline growth rate. The calculation is a shareholder-scale diagnostic, not an alternative accounting revenue figure. Dilution has financed capabilities and liquidity, so it may create value if the investments earn adequate returns. The burden of proof is that future profit and cash per share grow faster than the share count, rather than simply showing a larger company.

The cash balance was financed

Cash and equivalents reached $1.404 billion, versus $560.6 million at year-end. This is a substantial liquidity cushion. The principal financing source during the first nine months was $1.348 billion of net proceeds from common-stock issuance. Over the same interval, operating activities used $43.6 million, cash capital spending used $67.3 million, and acquisitions used $346.8 million net of acquired cash. The cash increase does not demonstrate operating cash generation. It demonstrates that investors supplied capital faster than the business deployed it.

There are no drawn bank borrowings in the quarter-end balance sheet, but there are $190 million of finance-lease obligations and $56 million of operating-lease obligations. Our enterprise-value bridge subtracts finance leases from cash and leaves operating leases within operating expenses. Provider total debt includes both types, so copying it would produce a different convention. Consistency matters: capitalizing rent obligations while also charging full rent in the forecast can double count the same burden. The company also incurred $93.6 million of noncash finance-lease obligations during the nine months. Cash capex alone therefore understates additions to committed productive assets. The base model gives credit for current net cash but does not treat the entire cash balance as a distributable special dividend. Facilities, engines, inventory, development programs and acquired operations compete for that capital. The robust balance sheet lowers near-term financing risk; it does not remove investment risk.

Three routes, one conservative center

Our three routes are intentionally different. First, a forward operating-value benchmark capitalizes the company’s 2027 adjusted EBITDA midpoint of $235 million at 25 times, adds $1.214 billion of finance-lease-adjusted net cash, and divides by 188.274 million common shares. The result is $37.65. The multiple is an author assumption, not an observed peer median or a company target. It gives considerable credit to growth while acknowledging that adjusted EBITDA omits compensation, reinvestment and several amortization categories.

Second, the cash-flow route forecasts enterprise free cash flow of negative $40 million, then $60 million, $150 million, $240 million, $330 million and $400 million during 2027–2032. These are our scenarios after operating costs, cash tax, reinvestment and working capital, not management guidance. We discount at 10%, use 3% perpetual growth after 2032, and place the first flow 1.14 years from the November 2026 valuation date. Adding the same net cash and dividing by the same shares gives $27.64. Compensation remains an economic cost in those assumed flows.

Third, the operating-earnings route assumes $3.3 billion of 2030 revenue and a 13% operating margin. Applying 21% tax produces $338.91 million of after-tax operating earnings. We apply a 25-times operating-value multiple and discount that enterprise value 4.14 years at 10%, then add current net cash. This gives $36.78 per share. Because this is an enterprise-value proxy using after-tax operating earnings, it is not a conventional price-to-earnings calculation. Interest income is excluded, preventing the cash balance from being counted both as an asset and as capitalized interest earnings.

The equal-weight center is $34.02, rounded to a $34 published base. Our bear, base and bull values are $20, $34 and $52 with probabilities of 25%, 50% and 25%, giving a $35 probability-weighted value. Scenario endpoints are author judgments informed by growth, margin, dilution and funding risk; they are not statistical confidence limits. The base value and weighted value answer different questions, so both remain visible. High uncertainty leads us to seek a 25% discount to base, or $25.50, before considering a new purchase, and only if the operating thesis remains intact.

At the selected price, computed enterprise value is $5.394 billion, or about 23.0 times the 2027 adjusted EBITDA midpoint. Holding the first five cash-flow amounts and other DCF assumptions fixed, a reverse calculation requires approximately $560.5 million of 2032 cash flow to support the quoted value, versus our $400 million assumption. The solved amount replaces both the explicit sixth-year cash flow and the base for terminal growth. This is a narrow sensitivity, not a complete market forecast. It shows how heavily value depends on eventual cash conversion. A different discount rate, dilution path or terminal growth rate changes the answer materially.

Capital allocation deserves a mixed assessment. The equity raises provide resources for a demanding industrial expansion and reduce reliance on borrowing. The best bull and bear arguments

The strongest bull case is that Kratos has already built specialized capabilities that customers increasingly need, and the backlog now validates that investment. Production scale can improve fixed-cost absorption, and the cash balance allows management to fund facilities and long-lead materials without an immediate liquidity squeeze. If the initial 2027 forecast proves conservative and working capital unwinds as milestones are achieved, our $34 base could understate the business. The $52 bull scenario requires sustained growth, better cash conversion and continued operating leverage, rather than simply a higher market multiple.

The strongest bear case is that growth continually demands new inventory, facilities and development spending while adjusted earnings exclude real shareholder costs. Large programs can shift timing, use substantial subcontractor content and generate weak percentage margins. Unmanned’s quarterly order intake is below revenue, and the cash-use outlook has deteriorated. More capital may eventually be required even with a healthy starting balance. The $20 bear scenario reflects slower conversion, lower profitability and valuation compression; it does not require the defense opportunity to disappear.
