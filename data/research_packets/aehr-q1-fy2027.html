The tape

Aehr reported results on November 2. Our reference is the completed regular close of $51.47, down 6.23% from the preceding close of $54.89. This packet does not attribute that daily decline to the earnings report. The saved quote timestamp is November 2 at 21:00:01 UTC. The primary evidence does not establish an exact release time and timezone. We therefore use the completed regular-session close as a neutral valuation reference.

The economic question is whether a small equipment supplier can turn an exceptional order cycle into a much larger, durable cash-producing franchise. The share price already assigns meaningful value to that outcome. Using the quarter-end outstanding share count gives a computed equity value of $1,681.2 million. Subtracting unrestricted cash produces enterprise value of $1,576.3 million, or 10.51 times management’s new annual revenue target. These are computed reference values, not a claim that a buyer could acquire the company at that price.

The price chart uses actual saved daily closes. The fair-value line is our estimate and the cash-per-share line is an accounting reference; neither is a trading floor. A sharp historical decline does not by itself establish cheapness, particularly when the starting valuation reflected an aggressive growth path. Equally, a high multiple can be earned if reliability testing becomes a structural requirement across several leading AI architectures. Our call weighs that upside against the cash, concentration and qualification evidence available today.

The comparative first quarter ended August 29, 2025. Management explicitly says it has not supplied a September 26, 2025 same-calendar comparison. Year-over-year growth therefore describes the reported fiscal periods, not identical seasonal windows. There was also a separate one-month transition period from May 30 through June 26, 2026. We show it separately and never fold it into the current quarter.

The current release gives the previous full quarter’s revenue as $18.835 million. Products and demand

Aehr supplies systems that stress semiconductors before deployment to expose early failures. It participates at wafer level and at packaged-device level. The annual report describes one reportable segment; product families are useful commercial categories, not separately disclosed operating segments with allocated profit. Current-quarter product revenue and product profit are not supplied in this release. We cannot responsibly turn management’s discussion of AI demand into a precise quarterly revenue mix.

The latest annual mix already shows a shift. Fiscal 2026 full-wafer revenue was approximately $31.5 million and packaged-part revenue $18.5 million. The comparable fiscal 2024 amounts were $64.6 million and $1.6 million. That history helps explain the transition from silicon-carbide and electric-vehicle demand toward AI testing. It does not prove that every new application will generate recurring high-margin revenue. Systems can be ordered in batches, and installed testing capacity may be consumed for some time before another large purchase is required.

Bookings of $46.7 million exceeded current-quarter revenue. Reported backlog was $100.9 million on September 25. Management’s effective backlog was $109.3 million on November 1, including orders after quarter end. The $8.4 million difference is a change between those two stated measures; it is not a complete postquarter gross bookings total, and it is not recognized revenue. Backlog supports visibility, but shipment timing, acceptance, cancellations and customer deployment schedules still determine its conversion.

The company reports a new major AI supplier passed benchmarking and ordered a fully configured FOX-XP system for deployment at an outsourced semiconductor assembly and test provider in Taiwan. Installation is expected in early 2027, followed by several months of validation before a production decision. This is a meaningful qualification milestone, not a guaranteed high-volume production award. Customer identities remain undisclosed. Naming a famous AI designer would add certainty that the filing does not provide.

Profit bridge

The central earnings improvement occurs above the interest and tax lines. Revenue less cost of revenue produced $15.663 million of gross profit. Research and development of $4.143 million and selling, general and administrative expense of $5.473 million left $6.047 million of operating profit. That is a computed operating margin of 18.3%. Scale helped absorb operating costs, while the product mix and manufacturing economics produced gross margin of 47.5%.

Interest income, net, added $0.899 million and other expense removed $0.019 million. The tax line was a $0.254 million benefit, taking pretax income of $6.927 million to net income of $7.181 million. Neither the interest contribution nor the tax benefit is evidence of stronger equipment demand. The tax benefit also means that the effective tax rate is negative; it is not a sensible perpetual tax assumption for valuing a mature profitable business.

Adjusted net income of $9.357 million adds back $1.880 million of share-based compensation and $0.296 million of acquisition-related adjustments. Those acquisition adjustments include acquired-intangible amortization and accretion of an escrow payable. They are not all new cash spending in the quarter. Share-based compensation, however, transfers economic value to employees and can dilute common shareholders. We keep a dilution allowance in the valuation and use a lower normalized net margin than the adjusted annual target.

The reconciliation does not remove the tax benefit. Investors should therefore avoid assuming the adjusted figure is automatically a fully normalized, cash-taxed earnings measure. The release’s GAAP diluted EPS is $0.21 and adjusted diluted EPS is $0.28, using 33.520 million diluted weighted-average shares. That denominator differs from the 32.663 million common shares outstanding at quarter end. Market capitalization uses outstanding shares; valuation uses our explicit future diluted assumption.

Earnings quality

Positive accounting profit and negative operating cash flow coexist in this quarter. That is not proof of misstatement. Rapid equipment growth often requires inventory purchases before customer collections arrive. It does mean the investor must distinguish booked profitability from cash that is available for reinvestment or distribution. We mark cash conversion as the most important near-term weakness because the working-capital build is large relative to the profit just reported.

Receivables absorbed $10.686 million and inventory absorbed $9.037 million in the cash-flow statement. Together those two uses total $19.723 million. Payables supplied $6.230 million, and deferred revenue supplied $1.275 million, partly offsetting those demands. The $19.723 million figure is therefore not total net working-capital use. The full cash-flow statement includes other movements and noncash adjustments. Operating cash ended at negative $3.054 million despite $7.181 million of GAAP net income.

Our receivable and inventory days are deliberately labeled ending-balance proxies. We use the actual thirteen-week quarter, or 91 days, rather than an annual revenue denominator. A large shipment near quarter end can lift receivables and depress collection ratios temporarily. A strategic inventory build can support future deliveries. Those explanations should eventually show up as cash collections and revenue conversion, which is why we set numerical signposts rather than pronounce the balances permanently good or bad today.

The annual cash record also matters. Fiscal 2026 operating cash was negative $3.310 million, followed by $2.066 million of capital expenditure. Fiscal 2025 cash generation was weaker still. Cash-rich balance sheets can obscure this distinction when recent equity issuance supplies liquidity. We use a free-cash-flow definition of operating cash less purchases of property and equipment, excluding acquisitions and financing. It is transparent and reproducible, but it does not eliminate the economic cost of stock compensation.

Cash and capital

Unrestricted cash was $104.902 million at September 25. The headline release rounds cash including restricted cash to $105.1 million. We use the unrestricted balance for valuation. The balance sheet shows no conventional financial borrowing, but it does show lease obligations: $0.653 million current and $9.151 million long term. Our enterprise-value convention subtracts unrestricted cash and does not capitalize leases; the valuation cash flows are assumed to include lease-related operating costs. Readers using lease-adjusted enterprise value should keep the cash-flow convention consistent.

The comparable unrestricted cash balance was $112.308 million at June 26 and $116.358 million at May 29. The June transition was a separate cash-use period before this quarter. Current-quarter operating cash outflow of $3.054 million plus only $0.049 million of capital expenditure gives free cash outflow of $3.103 million. The very low capital expenditure is not a license to assume every future quarter converts at the same investment intensity. Manufacturing capacity, supplier commitments and product development can consume cash through other lines.

Financing cash includes $4.474 million of shares withheld for employee tax settlement. This is not an open-market buyback program and should not be presented as a discretionary return of excess capital. The fiscal 2026 annual statement records $97.395 million of net at-the-market equity proceeds. That issuance explains much of the balance-sheet strength. Liquidity is real and lowers immediate financing risk, but it was substantially funded by shareholders rather than accumulated operating free cash.

Cash per outstanding share is approximately $3.21, a small part of the $51.47 reference quote. Most of the market value is therefore a claim on future operations. This supports a balanced reading: the company has a substantial buffer to fund an expanding order book, yet that buffer does not independently support today’s share price. The next useful evidence is faster collection and inventory conversion while orders continue, rather than simply another high quarter-end cash headline.

Valuation

Our fair-value anchor is $25 per share, with Very High uncertainty and a broad $12–$60 outcome range. The central value is the rounded average of three explicit routes. All routes use 35 million future diluted shares, compared with 33.520 million in the current quarter. The allowance recognizes employee equity and future dilution without assuming a specific unannounced offering. This is an analyst assumption, not company share guidance. More dilution would lower the per-share values.

The cash-flow route uses normalized forward annual periods from the valuation date. Year one adopts the $150 million revenue scale of management’s fiscal 2027 target; it is not a forecast of the remaining fiscal-year stub. Revenue then rises to $195 million, $244 million, $293 million and $337 million in model year five. Growth tapers thereafter to $494 million in year ten. Free-cash-flow margins rise from 12% to 20%, after recurring operating costs and reinvestment. We discount at 13%, use 4% perpetual growth, and add $104.902 million of current unrestricted cash. Explicit-period cash flow is worth $297.394 million and terminal value $336.328 million today, producing $21.10 per share. Even this restrained outcome requires substantial successful growth and sustained cash profitability.

The earnings route uses fiscal 2028 revenue of $195 million and an 18% normalized net margin, below the current annual adjusted-margin target to allow for recurring economic costs. Multiplying $35.1 million of net profit by 30, dividing by 35 million shares and discounting one year at 13% yields $26.62. Cash is not added again because this is an equity earnings multiple that includes the balance-sheet contribution. The sales route uses five times the same revenue, adds current cash, divides by the same shares and discounts one year, giving $27.30. Five times sales is a generous operating assumption for an equipment business; it is not justified by revenue growth alone.

The arithmetic average is $25.01, rounded to $25. A 30% margin of safety would put an initial watch threshold near $17.50, provided the operating thesis remains intact. That is a conditional research threshold, not a standing trade instruction. At the quoted price our reverse cash-flow model would require about 59.4% annual revenue growth across the first four model growth steps after the $150 million starting scale, reaching roughly $969 million in model year five, with the same margin path and discount assumptions. Alternatively, investors need materially higher margins, a lower required return, more durable growth, or some combination. The reverse case shows what is embedded in this particular model, not a prediction that the market has only one possible thesis.

Versus Wall Street

The saved target snapshot reports an average target of $132.50, a median of $122.50, and a $110–$175 range. Those numbers are substantially above our independent anchor. They are not freshly reaffirmed responses to this release. The dated target-news records are from September, August and July, while the ratings feed reports a Hold consensus from three analysts. The target and ratings collections do not necessarily cover the same brokers or dates. Presenting them as one coherent post-earnings vote would overstate the evidence.

Our disagreement is primarily valuation and cash conversion. We accept that AI reliability testing can drive very rapid growth and that current bookings are strong. We require a clearer route from customer commitments to collected cash before capitalizing many years of large expansion at a premium multiple. Other analysts may use a longer period of exceptional growth, higher margins, a lower discount rate or more favorable customer adoption assumptions. The saved public feed does not expose their complete models, so we do not attribute specific assumptions to them.

The consensus estimate collection also retains the old May fiscal-year labels after Aehr’s move to a June year end. Its fiscal 2027 revenue estimate is $135.169 million with two analysts, but the date is May 29, 2027. Management’s current target is $150 million for the year ending June 25, 2027. This is useful evidence that the provider snapshot is stale or differently aligned; it is not a clean like-for-like forecast gap. We keep primary guidance separate and avoid silently relabeling third-party fiscal periods.

Management scorecard

Management raised its annual revenue target to $150 million from a prior range of $130–$150 million. The new point target is above the old midpoint, but it does not raise the previous upper bound. The accompanying target is at least a 20% adjusted net margin. Together they imply at least $30 million of adjusted net profit if the revenue target is achieved. They are not GAAP earnings guidance and should not be treated as guaranteed cash generation.

After the reported first quarter, the revenue target requires $116.993 million across the remaining three quarters, or an average of approximately $38.998 million per quarter. That is roughly 18% above the current record quarter. Backlog makes the task plausible, but the target still requires execution. Lead times, customer readiness, acceptance and product mix can shift revenue between periods. A single exceptionally large shipment is not necessarily evidence of a sustainable quarterly run rate.

The capital-allocation score is mixed. Raising equity strengthened the cash buffer ahead of the ramp, reducing immediate financing pressure. Existing owners paid for that flexibility through dilution. The quarter’s employee tax withholding further used financing cash, while stock compensation remained material to the earnings bridge. We give management credit for stronger operating income, a larger order book and disclosed qualification progress, while reserving judgment on durable cash conversion and customer diversification.

Five questions would sharpen the next report: how much of the quarter-end receivable balance has been collected; which inventory purchases relate to firm customer commitments; how much backlog is scheduled for delivery within the current fiscal year; what production decision follows the new customer’s validation; and how much operating cash is required to support the annual target? None requires management to reveal a confidential customer name. Each helps translate commercial momentum into the economics available to common shareholders.

Risks and competing cases

Customer concentration is the leading business risk. In fiscal 2026, the five largest customers represented 70% of revenue; the three largest represented 26%, 14% and 11%. Those are annual figures, not a disclosed current-quarter concentration table. The present AI ramp can therefore make growth more exciting while leaving the economic dependence on a small number of capital-budget decisions intact. A long prospect list is not the same as a diversified revenue base.

The strongest bull case is that AI device complexity makes burn-in a standard reliability requirement across multiple architectures. Existing system installations can create follow-on demand for testing capacity and consumables, while qualification can make replacement inconvenient. If several customers enter volume production and cash conversion improves, revenue could exceed our base path and a higher margin could be defensible. The current backlog and demonstrated operating leverage are substantive evidence supporting that case.

The strongest bear case is a burst of capacity ordering that gets mistaken for a smooth long-term revenue stream. Customers may finish equipping a production line, delay a chip program or validate a competing approach. Inventory can remain elevated just as orders slow, while a premium valuation compresses. The risk is particularly acute when market expectations are extrapolated from a small base and a handful of customers. The equity can be volatile even if the company remains well funded.

Our SELL is a valuation judgment with moderate conviction, not a claim that the technology lacks value or that an immediate operating collapse is likely. Very High uncertainty reflects the unusually wide gap between successful multiyear adoption and a more uneven equipment cycle. The bullish outcome can produce substantial upside; it simply does not carry enough probability in our present assessment to make the reference price attractive. We would update that assessment as production wins, cash conversion and diversification become measurable.

Catalysts and signposts

The next quarterly report is the first major opportunity to test whether the record revenue quarter is converting to cash. We use January 2027 as a monitoring window, not an announced release date. The provider calendar contains a January 8 estimate, but the primary release does not confirm that date. Any official investor-relations announcement should replace the provisional calendar before scheduling coverage.

Early 2027 installation of the new FOX-XP system is another concrete milestone. The more important event comes afterward: completion of validation and an actual production decision. A successful installation alone should not be counted as a production ramp. The company also expects additional AI programs and Sonoma demand to develop through 2027, but customer forecasts remain subject to their own product schedules and investment decisions.

We will judge the annual target at the fiscal year ending June 25, 2027. Until then, revenue pace, bookings, cash collection and share dilution should be tracked together. A revenue beat accompanied by a much larger cash drain would not automatically strengthen our valuation. Conversely, modest revenue growth with repeat orders, lower receivable balances and positive operating cash could improve the quality of the business more than a spectacular headline quarter.

Our signposts are analyst thresholds designed to make this call falsifiable. They are not company promises or hard covenants. The $17.50 valuation watch level reflects a 30% discount to our current $25 anchor and must be recalculated if the facts change. A lower price caused by a damaged order book is not the same opportunity as a lower price with stable customer economics. This distinction is why the operating and valuation tests appear together.
