The margin loss is larger than the sales loss

The fourth quarter ended July 31, 2026. Revenue was $2.312B, down 8.4% from $2.524B. Gross profit fell much faster, to $285.6M from $370.9M. The company reports a 12.4% gross margin against 14.7% a year earlier, a decline of 230 basis points. That gap is the economic center of the release: lower volume, less favorable mix, promotions and material costs combined to consume a larger share of every sales dollar.

GAAP diluted earnings fell to $0.78 from $2.36. Net income attributable to THOR fell to $40.8M from $125.8M. It is tempting to regard that decline as a single operating signal, but the prior year also included favorable asset-sale and insurance items, and the effective tax rate changed. We separate those bridges rather than crediting or blaming current manufacturing operations for the entire EPS move.

For the full fiscal year, revenue edged higher to $9.608B, while diluted EPS fell to $3.38 from $4.84. Flat annual sales therefore did not preserve annual earnings. Adjusted EBITDA declined to $544.4M from $659.1M despite the broader geographic footprint and growth in owned supply activities. The recovery thesis must repair profitability, not merely restore shipment volume.

Formal fiscal 2027 guidance is absent. Management expects a relatively flat retail environment and intends to publish guidance later this fall after industry events and further work on savings timing. That is a useful description of its process, but it is not an EPS range. We do not insert an invented company forecast into an empty guidance box.

Europe cushions the North American reset

North American Towable revenue fell 22.7% to $687.3M. Unit shipments fell 19.7%, and fifth-wheel shipments fell 34.7%. Towables are especially sensitive to entry-level affordability, financing costs and household discretionary spending. Management says it deliberately produced below retail demand to improve dealer inventories. That protects the channel, but lower factory throughput still leaves a difficult fixed-cost absorption problem.

Motorized revenue fell 10.4% to $499.3M. Shipments declined 13.1%, while favorable mix raised net price per unit. This segment had supported full-year growth, but its fourth-quarter gross margin fell to 5.3% from 11.3%, and it recorded a $5.2M pretax loss. Market-share gains did not protect quarterly profitability. Revenue share and economic return are different measures.

Europe grew revenue 5.0% to $969.2M and increased pretax income to $55.0M. Its fourth-quarter currency contribution was small, whereas full-year growth was 9.0% reported and 3.1% at constant currency. We preserve that distinction. European customers tend to rely less heavily on financing, according to management, and the business benefited from motorized product mix and a healthier promotional environment. Nevertheless, its annual margin also declined and restructuring remains relevant.

Dealer inventories are more constructive than reported manufacturer inventory. North American dealers held roughly 64,000 THOR units against 73,300 a year earlier; European dealer inventory was approximately 20,500 units. THOR's own balance-sheet inventory increased. Those observations can both be true: one measures retail-channel stock and the other company-owned materials and vehicles. Confusing them would turn a measured channel improvement into an unsupported claim about cash release.

Follow the bridge below gross profit

Quarterly gross profit of $285.632M less selling, general and administrative expense of $206.416M and intangible amortization of $28.616M leaves $50.600M of operating income, computed from the release. Net interest expense then consumes $8.744M, while other income adds $9.912M. The resulting pretax income is $51.768M. Taxes reduce this to $41.306M, and noncontrolling interests take $0.468M, leaving $40.838M attributable to THOR.

Other income was $50.761M a year earlier. That comparison matters almost as much as the operating bridge. Towable pretax income in the prior quarter comparison included $21.4M of fixed-asset gains, and Motorized included an $11.2M insurance settlement benefit. A reader who annualizes the prior $2.36 EPS as ordinary factory economics overstates the quality of the comparison.

Adjusted EBITDA is helpful for separating selected unusual items, but it is not spendable cash. Current quarterly EBITDA of $130.004M becomes adjusted EBITDA of $131.737M after offsetting additions and deductions. Strategic initiatives add $16.901M, while investment-related gains, other gains and LIFO/foreign-exchange effects are removed or reversed. The small net adjustment disguises large gross movements in both directions.

The annual bridge is even more instructive. Strategic initiatives add $45.924M to EBITDA; other gains including property dispositions subtract $45.285M, and investment gains subtract $23.309M. Stock compensation adds $26.578M. We therefore use the reconciliation line by line and do not treat the proximity of EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA as evidence that unusual items were immaterial.

Cash conversion needs a second look

Compared with last year's $577.923M of operating cash, however, it represents a large deterioration. Cash conversion must be read both against current profit and against the company's own history.

Depreciation and amortization are substantial noncash additions. Working capital moved the other way: the cash statement shows a $73.282M inventory use versus a $29.122M source a year earlier. Capital expenditure also increased. Property disposals brought in cash, but our free-cash-flow definition subtracts gross capital spending and does not net recurring investment against asset-sale proceeds. That keeps the measure comparable and avoids presenting a footprint reduction as recurring cash productivity.

Receivables declined modestly while annual revenue was nearly flat. Inventory increased, leaving computed ending-balance days of 61.9, using annual cost of goods sold. Receivable days are 26.6, using annual sales. These are simple indicators based on ending balances, not average-balance turnover calculations. Seasonality and geography limit what can be inferred from a small movement.

The effective quarterly tax rate was 20.2%, against an unusually low prior-year rate. Noncontrolling interests are small and do not materially redirect the quarter's earnings. Stock compensation was modest relative to annual revenue, but it is still a real ownership cost. Finally, guidance quality remains ungraded for fiscal 2027 because no formal numerical outlook has been issued; an eventual savings ambition is not a substitute for an accountable annual range.

Three routes converge near seventy dollars

All forecasts in this section are Charged Alpha assumptions, not management guidance. The discounted-cash-flow route assumes unlevered free cash flow of $260M, $290M, $320M, $350M and $375M over the next five years. These are after modeled tax and reinvestment, before financing. Discounting at 10.5% and using 2.0% terminal growth gives an enterprise value of $3,903.9M. Subtracting $393.780M net debt and dividing by 51.614361M shares produces $68.01 per share. The recovery is assumed gradually; we do not insert the entire ultimate savings target in the first year.

The second route assumes normalized adjusted EBITDA of $650M and a 6.2-times enterprise multiple. That yields $4.030B enterprise value and $70.45 equity value per share after net debt. The EBITDA assumption is above fiscal 2026 and close to the prior-year adjusted outcome. It requires cost savings and stabilization to outweigh pricing concessions, rather than requiring a return to pandemic-era demand.

The third route assigns fourteen times $5.00 of normalized diluted EPS, giving $70.00. The EPS is explicitly an analyst assumption. It is above fiscal 2026's $3.38, so even this apparently conservative multiple capitalizes an earnings recovery. A rounded $70 base value sits inside the three-route cluster; it is not derived by attaching a premium multiple to the weakest trailing quarter.

At $69.94, the market implies about $5.00 of sustainable EPS at fourteen times, or $645.8M normalized EBITDA at our 6.2-times multiple. Those reverse calculations explain the HOLD: a substantial recovery is already embedded, while an improved channel prevents us from treating the current depressed earnings as permanent. With high uncertainty, we would seek a materially wider discount, roughly twenty percent below base value, before considering a stronger entry case on unchanged fundamentals.

A savings promise without a schedule

Management now describes more than $100M of annual earnings-profile improvement once strategic initiatives and restructuring are fully implemented. Its Q&A explicitly says that upside and phasing still require work, including how much will arrive in fiscal 2027 versus later periods. That qualification is central to the investment case. We cannot capitalize a full annual run rate and simultaneously assume it arrives immediately without new implementation costs.

North American coordination is moving into a single RV operating group. Ken Walters leads that group, supported by Mike Ritchie on finance. The proposed mechanisms are concrete: aggregated purchasing, better data visibility, footprint optimization, supplier alternatives and stronger owned-component content. Those are more credible than a generic call for efficiency, but execution across a historically decentralized organization remains a real operating challenge.

The shareholder-return record is mixed. Maintaining the dividend through the downturn supports income holders, and buybacks near depressed prices could prove valuable. Yet dividends and repurchases exceeded current free cash flow. We would rather see repurchases judged against future cash generation and required liquidity than treat their presence as proof the stock is cheap. Remaining authorization of $264.174M is permission, not a commitment.

The management questions we would prioritize are specific. How much of the ultimate savings is already in the current run rate? How much will be absorbed by price concessions? Which procurement categories have signed agreements rather than negotiated intentions? What cash cost remains to finish implementation? And what quarterly gross-margin evidence will demonstrate success if retail units remain flat? These questions turn a strategic story into a falsifiable operating plan.

The strong bear case and the credible bull case

The largest risk is prolonged affordability pressure. A recreational vehicle is a discretionary purchase with a financing burden, storage cost and ongoing fuel expense. A customer can postpone replacing a unit for longer than a manufacturer can avoid factory costs. Management's belief that demand is deferred rather than lost is plausible, but a deferred purchase does not pay current wages or suppliers.

The second risk is that savings are competed away. THOR says it is absorbing material-cost increases to protect retail price points. Procurement improvements may therefore stabilize margins rather than lift them by the entire gross savings amount. Our base case already assumes improved earnings, so disappointing realization would harm value twice: lower cash flows and potentially a lower multiple.

The bear case also includes dealer concentration and intangible-asset exposure. FreedomRoads accounted for roughly thirteen percent of fiscal 2026 consolidated sales and receivables. A large customer's inventory choices or financing stress can affect shipments quickly. Goodwill and acquired intangible assets provide no cash protection if demand weakens, and their carrying values remain sensitive to long-term assumptions.

The bull case deserves equal care. Dealer inventories are healthier, Europe provides geographic diversification, net leverage is modest and production can respond if retail stabilizes. A flat demand environment could still produce higher earnings if purchasing, footprint and mix improve. We assign a twenty percent bull probability and a $100 value, while a thirty percent bear case receives $45. The weighted value is $68.50, slightly below the $70 base. The distribution is deliberately wide because execution and the consumer cycle can move together.
