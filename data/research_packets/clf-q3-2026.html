The important change from our Q2 episode is the question being asked. That episode examined a return to positive cash flow and a jump in EBITDA. This quarter puts a stronger pricing recovery against a large financing burden and a much larger conditional outlook for next year. A positive operating turn can be real while the equity still offers an inadequate margin of safety after a sharp rally. We keep those two judgments separate.

The price-reaction series deliberately does not claim that earnings caused every move. Broader steel prices, interest rates, trade policy and market risk appetite move CLF too. Historical releases can occur before or after the close, and a simple release-date comparison does not adjust for those clocks. The current observation is especially provisional because the market session is incomplete. A later price can change the prospective return without changing any reported quarter figure.

The operating engine

One dominant steel business; use the actual segment bridge.

Steelmaking generated $5,447 million of revenue and $685 million of cash margin. Depreciation, depletion and amortization of $231 million leaves $454 million of segment gross margin. Cash margin is useful for understanding the manufacturing spread, but it excludes depreciation and does not pay corporate costs, financing costs or capital spending. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA is therefore not interchangeable with segment cash margin.

Realized selling price rose 14.1% year over year while shipments rose 3.0%. That mix helps explain why revenue and cash margin improved much faster than physical tons. It does not establish how much came from contract repricing versus product mix. The release gives market and product categories, not a full price-volume-mix reconciliation. We do not label the entire average-price increase a pure market-price benefit.

The current Steelmaking sales mix was 34% distributors and converters, 28% direct automotive, 28% infrastructure and manufacturing, and 10% steel producers. Direct automotive exposure makes contract resets important, while distributor behavior can magnify inventory swings. These categories describe market exposure, not the identities or concentration of individual customers. They do not prove that any single customer is diversified away.

Stelco improved enough for management to describe its strongest quarter since acquisition, while galvanizing lines are being idled because of Canadian oversupply. Management expects hot-rolled production to preserve total tonnage. That is a portfolio response with execution and employment consequences, not evidence that all Canadian markets have recovered. We credit the reported improvement and retain a separate risk for the remaining product-market imbalance.

Where profit goes

The operating recovery survives GAAP, but debt absorbs a large share.

The headline is not solely an adjusted-accounting recovery. GAAP operating income is positive, and consolidated net income is positive after interest and tax. Nevertheless, the $612 million adjusted EBITDA headline travels a long way before reaching common shareholders. Depreciation of $259 million and interest expense of $148 million are substantial. Treating EBITDA as cash available to the equity would ignore both the assets needed to make steel and the financing of those assets.

Below operating income, pension and other benefit credits added $64 million, derivative losses subtracted $15 million, and other nonoperating income added $1 million. Together with $148 million of interest expense, these produce pretax income of $213 million. The tax provision was $46 million, followed by a $2 million discontinued-operations loss. Pension credits improve accounting earnings but do not represent a customer payment collected this quarter.

For the first nine months, consolidated operating income was only $49 million and attributable net loss remained $230 million. That cumulative view matters when testing the sustainability of a $4 billion-plus outlook for next year. A recovery can accelerate, but the burden of proof increases with the distance between the latest run rate and the proposed destination. We require operating and cash evidence rather than capitalizing the destination immediately.

The adjusted net-income gap is comparatively small: $161 million adjusted attributable income versus $152 million GAAP. The reconciliation removes losses and also removes gains, including the disposal gain. Showing only positive add-backs would exaggerate the adjustment. Our chart uses the contribution needed to move from GAAP attributable income to adjusted attributable income, so the disposal and tax items appear with negative signs.

Quarter operating cash flow of $224 million exceeded consolidated net income, but capital spending absorbed $152 million. The remaining $72 million was essentially flat against Q2’s $73 million even as adjusted EBITDA more than doubled. Receivables used $116 million and inventory used $170 million of cash, partly offset by $135 million from payables and accrued expenses. Higher sales can consume working capital before the cash arrives; the next quarter must demonstrate collection and inventory discipline.

First-nine-month operating cash was $129 million against capital spending of $461 million, leaving computed free cash flow of negative $332 million. Property sales can reduce debt, but sale proceeds are investing cash and are not operating free cash flow under our definition. This prevents a financing improvement from being mistaken for recurring cash generation. It also explains why we do not treat the liquidity headline as surplus cash.

The 2025 annual filing corrected immaterial employment-cost accrual errors in prior periods. We use the latest comparative figures where supplied and explicitly retain original-release presentation elsewhere. Corrected prior-quarter adjusted EBITDA is negative $179 million for Q1 2025 and positive $94 million for Q2 2025. The correction deserves attention, but the current filing evidence does not support labeling the issuer’s present statements nonreliable. No standalone quarterly SBC amount is manufactured from the provider’s zero field.

Balance sheet and capital

Available borrowing capacity is not cash in the bank.

The balance sheet contained $77 million of cash and $7,645 million of long-term debt. Our simple net-debt calculation is therefore $7,568 million. Management’s $3.2 billion total-liquidity measure includes available financing capacity; it cannot replace the $77 million cash balance in an equity-value calculation. The business has access to liquidity, but drawing that capacity creates or maintains a liability.

Net debt fell by a computed $65 million from Q2, a useful but modest move relative to the balance. The quarter’s operating-income-to-interest ratio was only 2.1 times. That is a narrow accounting coverage measure, not a covenant calculation and not a forecast of default. It shows why financing costs still deserve substantial space in an otherwise encouraging operating discussion.

Legacy pension, environmental and retirement obligations also matter. We do not simply add every gross liability to net debt while ignoring associated assets or cash-flow effects. Our normalized cash-flow assumptions and cautious multiples recognize recurring burdens, and the packet shows the reported balances so readers can stress them separately. The equity is sensitive to assumptions because a large fixed financing claim sits ahead of it.

Capital allocation has changed across the cycle. The company spent $733 million on repurchases in 2024, repurchased no common shares in 2025, and raised $951 million from common issuance in 2025. The annual report retained $1.4 billion of authorization, but authorization is neither an obligation nor evidence of current purchases. In this balance-sheet position, debt reduction and cash retention deserve priority over treating unused authorization as a near-term catalyst.

Valuation

Three routes cluster near $15; the wide scenario range stays visible.

Our HOLD is a valuation judgment, not a denial of the recovery. We use a $15 base value, conviction three out of five, and High uncertainty. The three routes below land between roughly $13.84 and $15.62. We round the central judgment to $15 rather than pretend model precision is price precision. The scenario range is wider, $6 to $27, because cyclical operating earnings and leverage create asymmetric equity outcomes.

At the fixed quote, enterprise value is $15.441 billion. At our selected 5.5-times normalized EBITDA multiple, that price implies approximately $2.808 billion of sustainable annual EBITDA. Q3 annualized is $2.448 billion, whereas management’s conditional 2027 outlook exceeds $4 billion. The market-implied figure sits between them. Annualizing one quarter is a reference point, not a forecast, and the company’s outlook is not assured merely because the implied figure is lower.

1

Normalized EV / EBITDA: $14.69

Our assumption: $2,900M normalized EBITDA × 5.5 = $15,950M enterprise value. Subtract $7,568M net debt and divide by 570.54M shares: $14.69. The multiple reflects a capital-intensive cyclical business; it is an analytical assumption, not a reported peer median.

2

Normalized earnings: $15.62

Start with the same $2,900M EBITDA. Deduct assumed $1,000M depreciation and $580M interest, then apply a 25% tax rate: $990M normalized common earnings. Divide by 570.54M shares and apply 9 times earnings: $15.62. This is a normalized common-economic model, not a projection of reported GAAP net income.

3

Cash-flow cross-check: $13.84

Assume five annual free-cash-flow-to-firm amounts of $1,100M, $1,200M, $1,300M, $1,400M and $1,500M. Discount at 11%, with 2.5% terminal growth. Enterprise value is $15,462M; subtract $7,568M net debt and divide by 570.54M shares: $13.84. These future cash flows are our recovery assumptions, substantially above current cash generation.

The cash-flow route is intentionally demanding. It requires operating recovery to turn into cash after maintenance investment and working capital. Its terminal value carries substantial weight, so the result is sensitive to discount rates and sustainable cash generation. We do not present it as an independent proof of the EBITDA model: all three routes share an economic recovery premise. Agreement among related assumptions reduces arithmetic inconsistency, not business uncertainty.

The EBITDA starting point follows the company’s adjusted measure, which excludes EBITDA attributable to noncontrolling interests. Our normalized earnings and cash-flow routes are framed on that common-economic basis too; we do not subtract a second minority claim without a consistent consolidated denominator. Actual future share issuance, acquisitions, debt reduction or asset-sale proceeds would require refreshing the model. Holding net debt constant makes the present comparison transparent.

A $15 base value offers only a computed 8.7% upside to $13.80, before fees, taxes or timing. We require a 25% discount to base value for a new BUY under High uncertainty, which gives an illustrative $11.25 entry threshold, provided the operating thesis remains intact. A falling price caused by deteriorating fundamentals does not automatically qualify. Better verified cash conversion or durable contract economics could instead justify raising the estimate.

The covered security is NYSE-listed Cleveland-Cliffs common stock, with no ADR conversion in the per-share model. The annual report identifies Deloitte & Touche as auditor. Likelihood and impact are analyst judgments, not statistical probabilities.

The bull case deserves a fair hearing. Contract resets could make the latest quarter a stepping stone rather than the peak. Higher realized prices, steadier volumes and better costs could allow EBITDA to approach the company’s outlook while working-capital needs normalize. Cash would then retire debt and increase the value left for common shareholders. That combination explains why a large upside scenario remains plausible despite our cautious central call.

The bear case is equally concrete. Index forecasts can weaken, customer orders can fall and inventory can absorb cash. Fixed operating and financing costs leave less room for error. If normalized EBITDA is only $2.2 billion and investors pay five times that amount, subtracting present net debt leaves roughly $6 per share. The scenario does not require the business to disappear; it requires a less favorable operating cycle and a constrained equity claim.

We land between those cases because the reported recovery is convincing but the financing and cash evidence has not caught up. Our scenarios use a constant present net-debt balance and rounded share values. They are decision tools, not price predictions or a complete distribution of outcomes. Extreme operational disruption, new issuance or a major transaction could produce values outside the displayed range.
