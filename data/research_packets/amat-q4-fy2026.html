The call and the price

Our call is BUY with moderate conviction at the November 13, 2026 regular-session close of $370.50. The main model estimates present fair value at $409.59 per share. Its dated base outcome is approximately $521.99 at the end of fiscal 2029, plus modeled dividends, producing a 12.84% annualized return against a 12% requirement. That is enough for a measured positive call, but the difference from the hurdle is small. This is not a deep-discount thesis or a guarantee of a particular market price.

Applied Materials is enjoying an exceptional semiconductor-equipment expansion. Revenue grew 53% in the latest quarter, and operating profit more than doubled. Artificial intelligence increases demand for advanced logic, high-bandwidth memory and the increasingly complex manufacturing steps that connect them. The investment question is whether this spending creates enough durable cash for each share after capacity expansion, working capital and compensation. A booming equipment order cycle can still disappoint shareholders who pay for permanently exceptional conditions.

Our model therefore separates Semiconductor Systems from services and the remaining businesses. It permits strong growth and higher process value, includes a cyclical pause, and charges the capital required to deliver the equipment. We do not force a historically low multiple onto an economically stronger company. We also do not assume that today's growth rate lasts forever or that every future customer investment becomes Applied revenue.

The current estimate, a dated future value and the Street's one-year target answer different questions. Approximately $410 is our estimate of value today. Approximately $444 is our base value at the end of fiscal 2027. The $566.14 Street aggregate is a one-year target reported by the market-data provider. The distinction prevents a misleading comparison of a discounted current estimate with an undiscounted future target.

The reported financial scorecard

Applied reported fourth-quarter revenue of $10.409 billion for the period ended October 25, 2026, compared with $6.800 billion a year earlier. Gross profit rose from $3.265 billion to $5.251 billion. GAAP gross margin improved from 48.0% to 50.4%, while operating income increased from $1.712 billion to $3.658 billion. The resulting 35.1% operating margin shows genuine operating leverage, not merely a larger sales denominator.

Net income was $2.718 billion, with GAAP earnings of $3.40 per diluted share. Adjusted earnings were $4.16 per share. These are distinct measures: below-operating-line investment, tax and other items can produce a larger EPS difference than the operating-income adjustment. GAAP operating income and adjusted operating income were only $18 million apart. We use an operating cash model and do not capitalize a temporary investment remeasurement as recurring equipment earnings.

Quarterly operating cash flow was $3.300 billion. Subtracting $684 million of capital expenditure gives $2.616 billion of free cash flow. The comparison with the full year matters: fiscal 2026 revenue rose to $34.446 billion, while free cash flow was $6.196 billion versus $5.698 billion, an increase of about 9%. Cash conversion did not keep pace with the latest quarter's extraordinary sales growth.

Full-year capital expenditure was $2.672 billion, and working-capital movements used $1.936 billion of cash. These are economically meaningful investments. They may enable future delivery and growth, but they are unavailable for immediate distribution. A good analysis should neither label every investment a permanent failure nor erase it from shareholder economics. Our forecast retains meaningful capital and working-capital demands before counting owner cash.

Where the economic engine sits

Semiconductor Systems generated $7.996 billion of quarterly sales, up from $4.879 billion. Its reported operating margin was 38.8%. Applied Global Services generated $1.896 billion of sales, with $578 million of operating income and a 30.5% margin. Other activities contributed $517 million of revenue and a $21 million operating loss. Segment profit and consolidated profit are not interchangeable without the issuer's reconciliation, and the main model preserves the separate revenue and margin paths.

The equipment business sells capability into a changing manufacturing process. More demanding transistor structures, interconnects, materials and packaging can increase the number and value of required steps. That supports a share-of-customer-spending thesis beyond simply shipping more wafers. Nevertheless, qualification, reliability and customer yield remain decisive. A product announcement creates an opportunity, not an automatic revenue entitlement.

Within Systems, foundry and logic represented 58% of quarterly revenue, DRAM 33% and flash 9%. That exposure makes both leading-edge logic and the memory cycle important. AI demand can support these markets together, but their timing and economics differ. Memory customers can sharply change equipment spending when supply, pricing or utilization moves, so an AI label does not eliminate cyclicality.

Services adds a different source of value. A larger installed base can generate recurring maintenance, upgrades and productivity work after an initial tool sale. It is not perfectly insulated from utilization, customer budgets or mix, but it generally has a different demand pattern from new factory equipment. Our base permits services to grow through the modeled equipment pause. The test is actual revenue and operating contribution, not simply calling every service dollar recurring.

Margins, investment and sustainable cash

The main driver model starts with revenue and operating margins for Systems, services and other activities. It keeps stock compensation inside those operating margins. This matters because Applied's reported non-GAAP operating measure also retains stock compensation; automatically adding it back would overstate the economics and would misunderstand the issuer's actual reconciliation. Fiscal 2026 stock compensation was $719 million, and the latest quarter included $174 million.

Our base projects fiscal 2027 revenue of $48.0 billion, with Systems at $38.0 billion, services at $8.4 billion and other activities at $1.6 billion. Systems operating margin starts at 40%, rises with mix and scale, and eventually settles at 42%. Services begins at 31% and matures at 33%. These are analyst assumptions. They are more optimistic than simply freezing fiscal 2026, but they still need to be earned through execution and customer demand.

After operating profit, the model charges taxes, adds back depreciation, and subtracts capital expenditure and incremental working capital. Tax rates start at 13%, move to 15% and then normalize to 17%. Capital expenditure falls from 6.5% of revenue in fiscal 2027 toward 3.5% as expansion matures. Depreciation rises from 2% toward 2.8%. We are not assuming that the company can support a vastly larger revenue base without additional assets.

Incremental working capital consumes 15% of the change in revenue, with a release when sales contract. That convention is a forecast approximation, not an accounting identity. It makes the cost of rapid growth visible. The base produces $11.296 billion of economic FCFF in fiscal 2027, rising to $30.355 billion by fiscal 2036. These are modeled unlevered cash flows, not issuer guidance or the company's reported free-cash-flow measure.

An independent market comparison

Lam Research supplies a useful independent reference because it also participates in complex semiconductor manufacturing and generates meaningful service revenue. At the November 13 close of $225.92, its diluted quarterly share basis, cash and financial claims imply enterprise value of approximately $280.7 billion. Dividing by four times its September-quarter GAAP operating income produces a multiple of about 21.37 times. Annualizing one quarter here is a comparison convention, not a claim that the quarter is normal or a forecast of the following year.

Applying that observed multiple to Applied's own quarterly GAAP operating income annualized, then applying Applied's cash, investment, operating-cash, debt and existing tax-claim bridge, gives approximately $396.73 per share. This provides a market reference near the main model's current value. It does not mathematically confirm the DCF, and we do not average the two numbers to create a more convenient target.

The differences are important. Lam's quarter ends in September, Applied's in October. Their memory exposure, product portfolios, margins and service economics differ. Lam's balance-sheet comparison uses carrying debt and finance leases, and cash classifications are not identical. Both can benefit from the same investment cycle, so a favorable peer multiple can itself embed optimistic industry expectations.

The stronger quality question is whether Applied sustains customer relevance, process share and attractive returns through the cycle. Services growth, R&D productivity, repeat qualification and the cash cost of expansion matter more than a temporary premium or discount to one peer. A broad-based downturn can make both companies appear inexpensive on peak earnings. The comparison is a check on valuation scale, while the forecast remains responsible for what happens after the current quarter.

Balance sheet, debt and ownership

At October 25, Applied reported $7.239 billion of cash and equivalents, $2.100 billion of short-term investments and $4.756 billion of long-term investments. Restricted cash of $57 million is excluded from the liquid-asset bridge. We retain a $2.5 billion operating-cash reserve. The remaining cash and investments are added once to operating enterprise value; investment gains are not also capitalized as recurring operating profit.

Debt carrying value was $6.532 billion. The model uses $6.600 billion of face obligations, consisting of $6.5 billion of notes and $100 million of commercial paper. Detailed note terms come from the July Form 10-Q because the fiscal 2026 Form 10-K had not yet been filed when this analysis was prepared. The October balance sheet and cash-flow statement reconcile the headline debt position. Existing income-tax payable of $1.080 billion is separately deducted once and assumed paid in fiscal 2027.

The first material note maturity is $1.2 billion in April 2027, followed by $700 million in June 2029. Coupon expense and dated principal repayments enter the future cash bridge used for horizon equity value. They are not subtracted again from unlevered operating cash flow and from enterprise value as duplicate expenses. The model assumes no incremental borrowing and no future discretionary repurchases; retained cash remains an asset instead.

Quarterly repurchases were $2.000 billion and full-year repurchases $3.177 billion. Full-year dividends were $1.571 billion. These distributions are real, but a repurchase authorization is not additional cash. Weighted diluted shares averaged 798 million in the quarter, compared with 799 million for the year and 808 million in the previous year. Since a fresh current fully diluted share count was unavailable, the model explicitly uses the 798 million weighted proxy. A 790–810 million sensitivity produces base values of roughly $414–404. Future compensation stays charged in operating profit; buybacks are not assumed to create a second free benefit.

Main model, scenarios and return test

The principal valuation is a ten-year, segment-driven discounted FCFF model covering fiscal 2027 through fiscal 2036. The discount rate is 9.5%, terminal growth is 3.5%, and the terminal incremental return on capital is 25%. Sustainable terminal reinvestment is therefore 14% of next-period NOPAT. The terminal calculation already supplies its investment charge; we do not subtract a second perpetual capex amount from that same terminal cash.

Base present value is $409.59 per share. The bear is $216.71 and the bull $627.57. Subjective weights of 25%, 50% and 25% produce approximately $415.86. The main base case leads the stock call; the weighted value is a scenario summary, not a statistical confidence interval or a separate model. Approximately 59% of base enterprise value comes from the terminal period, a material source of uncertainty that is visible rather than hidden behind the point estimate.

The base does not extrapolate a straight line. Systems revenue reaches $47.5 billion in fiscal 2028, declines 5% in fiscal 2029 and then recovers. Its margin also contracts during that pause. Services continues expanding, and later equipment margins fade from their peak. The bear combines a more serious investment slowdown and weaker profitability; the bull supports stronger process intensity, demand and execution. The forecast tables show exactly which cash flows produce each outcome.

The base horizon is October 28, 2029. We value the remaining operating cash flows at that date and add cash retained after operations, after-tax coupons, debt maturities, the existing tax claim and modeled dividends. Remaining debt is then deducted. The resulting price is approximately $521.99. Dividends of $2.12, $2.226 and $2.3373 per share are assumed at the fiscal 2027–2029 endpoints. Together they produce about 42.7% total return and a 12.84% dated annualized IRR. These dividends are forecasts, not declarations, and the actual trading price may differ substantially.

Street expectations and the reverse test

The market-data provider showed a $566.14 average one-year analyst target from 39 analysts, updated November 13. Our base value at October 31, 2027 is approximately $444.15, a broadly comparable horizon about two weeks earlier. Differences can arise from growth, margins, discount rates, valuation methods and assumed capital allocation. The aggregate target does not tell us which precise assumptions each analyst is making, and it should not be treated as a guaranteed destination.

Our reverse valuation asks a narrower, reproducible question. Keeping services, other activities, margins, taxes, investment ratios, shares and discounting unchanged, what uniform scale of Systems revenue across all forecast years supports the actual $370.50 quote? The result is approximately 88.4% of the base path. At the end of the forecast, that implies about $67.53 billion of Systems revenue rather than $76.36 billion.

That apparent cushion has limits. A revenue shortfall caused by weaker customer demand may also bring lower margins or worse mix. The controlled reverse test holds those variables fixed to isolate one driver; real operating outcomes need not cooperate. Our operating sensitivity therefore changes both Systems revenue and its margin. A 20% lower revenue path with unchanged margins produces approximately $341.96. Combining that reduction with a three-point margin shortfall gives approximately $321.69.

Discount-rate sensitivity is also significant. Base value is approximately $548.97 at 8%, $409.59 at 9.5% and $325.92 at 11%. The point is not to choose a rate that makes the stock look appealing. It is to understand how long-duration cash expectations interact with risk. Observable customer spending, execution and margins should drive revisions before small changes in a chosen terminal input are used to defend an existing opinion.

Guidance and the industry setting

Management guided fiscal first-quarter revenue to $11.1 billion, plus or minus $500 million, and adjusted EPS to $4.35, plus or minus $0.20. Prepared remarks indicated approximately $8.7 billion of Systems revenue, $2.0 billion of services and $400 million of other revenue at the midpoint. Adjusted gross margin was expected around 50.8%, operating expenses around $1.7 billion and the tax rate around 13%.

The calendar matters. Fiscal first-quarter 2027 contains 14 weeks, and the fiscal year contains 53 weeks. A comparison with a conventional 13-week quarter can otherwise exaggerate the organic acceleration. Our model uses actual fiscal endpoint dates and does not confuse the extra week with a permanent increase in the business's growth rate. The issuer's guide is a quarter-specific expectation; our multiyear sales path is a separate forecast.

Management expects industry wafer-fab-equipment demand to grow more than 30% in calendar 2027. It also plans to double Systems output capacity from the fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 level by 2028 and to more than double manufacturing and laboratory capacity by 2030. Those plans respond to a strong demand outlook, but capacity is not the same as recognized revenue. Customer cleanroom readiness, components, tool delivery and qualification can all influence when the opportunity converts into sales and cash.

The longer-term targets include increased equipment share, higher services revenue per installed tool and improved margins relative to the stated 2025 baseline. They help frame management's ambition, not establish a guaranteed outcome. We charge the investment supporting that ambition and allow a cyclical interruption. The useful monitoring task is to connect customer forecasts and new process adoption to actual shipments, service activity, working capital and return on investment.

The strongest bull and bear arguments

The strongest bull case is that AI infrastructure changes process complexity as well as chip volume. More demanding logic, memory and packaging can increase Applied's addressable spending per wafer. A larger installed base then supports service activity, while scale and product value improve margins. This argument is stronger than simply saying AI spending is large: it identifies the technical work customers must purchase and the mechanism that can create recurring economic value for Applied.

The strongest bear accepts that semiconductor demand may remain healthy while questioning the timing and distribution of the equipment profits. Customers can build ahead of utilization, encounter cleanroom constraints or delay equipment acceptance. Memory investment can swing. Applied can spend on capacity before the expected revenue arrives, leaving lower margins and weaker cash conversion even without a collapse in end-market demand. The base's planned pause provides some cycle recognition, but the bear is meaningfully worse.

China accounted for $2.642 billion of quarterly revenue, or 25%, and $9.330 billion, or 27%, for fiscal 2026. Export restrictions, customer mix and substitution can affect that business. Exposure should be linked to reachable sales, product mix and margins rather than converted into an arbitrary additional discount after those same effects have already been modeled. A previously disclosed $253 million BIS settlement is a resolved historical compliance matter; it should not be described as an unresolved allegation without new evidence.

The announced increase in the Besi stake to approximately 20% offers advanced-packaging strategic exposure, subject to the announced conditions. The reviewed release does not disclose the purchase price. Our model therefore treats the cash-for-investment exchange as value-neutral, without a free additional stake or unearned synergies. Execution and price paid can ultimately make the transaction better or worse. The present BUY call depends on operating cash and the stated return test, not on assigning speculative value to an undisclosed deal.

Evidence that changes the call

The next quarterly report is the immediate operating checkpoint. Compare sales with the $10.6–11.6 billion guide, but also account for the 14-week quarter. Stronger reported revenue is more useful when it accompanies healthy segment margins and manageable receivables, inventory and cash investment. A shipment burst that produces unusually slow collection or a build-up of equipment awaiting customer readiness should be examined rather than automatically celebrated.

Services deserves its own checkpoint. Continued growth with stable contribution would strengthen the claim that the installed base provides resilience through equipment swings. Weakness in both tools and service activity would challenge the degree of diversification assumed in our base. Management's ambitions for revenue per tool are useful only when subsequent disclosure shows economic progress rather than a favorable denominator or mix change.

Capacity expansion, customer plans and the timing of memory investment are the next set of catalysts. We would look for evidence that the company can deliver more systems while protecting quality and capital efficiency. The base already assumes exceptional growth in the next two fiscal years, so meeting a strong industry narrative is not necessarily enough to exceed the model. Conversely, a temporary pause does not automatically destroy the thesis if the longer-run cash and process position remain intact.

The April 2027 debt maturity, future capital returns and Besi transaction disclosure are ownership checkpoints. Update actual cash, investment value, shares and financing instead of assuming that all available funds are simultaneously retained, repurchased and invested. The signposts below make the decision process falsifiable. A higher price without better economics reduces the expected return; better durable cash economics can justify a higher value even before the stock moves.

Sources, accounting conventions and forecast detail

Primary evidence is the fiscal 2026 earnings release furnished with the November 12 Form 8-K, the issuer's prepared remarks, the latest available detailed Form 10-Q and the Besi announcement. The regular-session stock prices and dated analyst aggregate are market-data references. Lam's reported financial statements supply the peer comparison. The release was timestamped at 4:01 p.m. Eastern on November 12, 2026, with a matching issuer-wire record.

All financial amounts in the forecast tables are US dollars in millions unless otherwise stated. Share counts are millions. Fiscal years end on the last Sunday in October, so dates differ from calendar-year endpoints. The fiscal 2026 close was October 25; the reference stock quote is November 13. The model estimates the intervening 19 days of operating cash from its fiscal 2027 run rate, less after-tax coupon accrual. That interim cash is assumed, not observed.

Fiscal 2027 contains 371 days. The first 19 days enter the cash-at-valuation bridge, while only the remaining 352 days of that fiscal year's FCFF enter discounted future cash flows. This avoids counting the same cash twice. The future cash bridge deducts coupon, maturity and dividend payments at the model's stated dates and removes claims when paid. Existing tax payable is distinct from taxes on future forecast operating income. Investment assets are counted once, without assumed interest yield or future remeasurement gains.

The latest detailed share-award note was dated July, and the fiscal 2026 Form 10-K was not yet available. The diluted-quarter proxy and its sensitivity are a disclosed limitation, not a claim that precise current fully diluted ownership has been independently observed. Future stock compensation remains an operating cost. There is no separate free buyback assumption, and there is no second future-grant dilution charge on top of the same modeled compensation cost.

Reported facts, management expectations, market references and author assumptions are separated in the source record and calculation.
