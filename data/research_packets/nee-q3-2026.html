Price the continuing parent

NextEra Energy enters this print with a strong operating story and a complicated capital story. Our valuation anchor is the October 20 regular-session close of $78.86, before the October 21 release. The saved live quote was already moving during the next session, and the provider’s daily price endpoint contained an unfinished October 21 row. We exclude that row from close-to-close calculations. This packet therefore does not claim a completed market reaction to the new results. A dated price is more useful than a falsely precise reaction that blends different clocks.

The common shares represent the continuing NextEra parent, which owns Florida Power & Light and NextEra Energy Resources. They do not yet represent a completed combination with Dominion. The proposed enlarged company would retain the NextEra name and NEE listing, but its eventual earnings, share count, debt and regulated mix would differ. This distinction matters when comparing a historical trading multiple with management’s combined-company growth ambition.

Our HOLD is a valuation judgment, not a statement that the business lacks attractive projects. At the reference close, shares trade at about 19.6 times the high end of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance. That starting multiple already rewards a utility for above-average growth. A sound franchise can remain a demanding investment when it needs continuing access to external capital and the price assumes execution. We require a larger price concession because project economics, regulation and transaction conditions can all change together.

A near doubling is not the operating growth rate

The quarter produced $8.090B of operating revenue, $2.624B of operating income and $4.775B attributable net income. GAAP diluted EPS was $2.28, versus $1.18 a year earlier. Adjusted earnings were $2.592B, or $1.24 per share, compared with $2.348B and $1.13. The adjusted per-share increase is 9.7%, while attributable adjusted earnings grew 10.4%. Weighted-average diluted shares rose from 2,071M to 2,098M, absorbing part of the earnings increase.

Revenue increased only 1.6%, which could look inconsistent with the earnings result if one treated this as an ordinary volume-times-price business. That interpretation is too simple. Regulated fuel recovery, generation mix and market-sensitive accounting affect the reported lines. Operating income grew modestly, while the extraordinary move below operating income came predominantly from hedge effects. A revenue multiple or a headline GAAP EPS multiple would miss the economics we are trying to value.

Management maintained 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $3.92–$4.02 and continues to target the high end. It also reaffirmed at least 8% compound growth through 2032 from a 2025 adjusted EPS base of $3.71, with the same targeted through 2035. Those are forward-looking expectations with assumptions about capital access, policy, demand and regulation. They are not guaranteed earnings, and the reported quarter does not eliminate the need to finance the assets that support them. We do not label a beat versus consensus where the saved earnings record has not established a fresh, comparable actual-versus-estimate pair.

Two engines, with a corporate funding cost

FPL earned $1.620B, up from $1.463B, as regulatory capital employed grew approximately 9.2%. The company described roughly $2.4B of quarterly capital expenditure and continues to expect about $12B for the full year. Investment can enlarge the regulated earnings base when the regulator permits recovery and a return, but cash goes out before all benefits arrive. Customer affordability and reliability are therefore economic constraints as well as service commitments: they influence the support for continued investment and the terms on which it earns.

NEER’s adjusted earnings increased to $1.247B from $1.102B. It originated 3.7 GW of renewables and storage, including 2.4 GW of battery storage, and reported a 36.7 GW backlog after 1.8 GW entered service since the July results call. Backlog is an operating indicator, not cash, recognized revenue or a guaranteed margin. Interconnection, construction, customers and financing must still align before those projects produce the expected return. We therefore prefer continued commissioning alongside new origination to a larger backlog alone.

Corporate and Other reported a $275M adjusted loss versus $217M a year earlier. Investors who add FPL and NEER earnings and ignore corporate financing costs overstate what belongs to common shareholders. Our sum-of-parts model capitalizes the corporate drag explicitly. The reported corporate GAAP profit looks radically different because non-qualifying hedge gains flow through that segment; it does not mean headquarters has become a recurring profit engine.

The federal-hub opportunities also need a separate label. The release describes definitive agreements totaling roughly 16.5 GW and $5.7B of initial funding year to date, with development, construction and operation for fees without NEE investing capital. Those fee projects are different from the large-load assets NEE expects to own. We do not add every announced gigawatt to the 36.7 GW renewables backlog or assign the same margin and capital requirement to unlike opportunities.

Follow the hedge bridge and minority allocation

The reconciliation removes $3.005B of pretax non-qualifying hedge gains. After tax, the removal is $2.246B. It adds back $38M for nuclear decommissioning fund effects, $6M for the XPLR investment item and $19M of merger expense, producing $2.592B adjusted earnings from $4.775B GAAP earnings. The net gap is $2.183B. Treating that entire gap as a recurring operating gain would materially overstate current earning power. Conversely, excluding hedge marks does not remove every economic risk associated with financing and market exposure.

The income statement’s line labeled interest expense is positive $1.929B this quarter, compared with negative $1.153B a year earlier. Corporate and Other alone shows a positive $2.228B on that line. The hedge reconciliation explains why simply annualizing the face of the interest line would be misleading. A lender still charges for capital even when changes in derivative values create a favorable accounting entry. We use adjusted earnings and separately assess debt, funding and rate sensitivity instead of declaring the company’s financing cost negative.

There is a second accounting distinction below tax. Consolidated net income is $4.367B. Net losses allocated to noncontrolling interests add $408M to the amount attributable to NEE, giving $4.775B. This is not a cash dividend received from outside investors. Differential membership and other project structures can allocate earnings or losses differently from cash distributions. The legal structure and financing arrangement matter when interpreting how much reported income turns into parent cash.

FPL and NEER also carry different tax profiles. Consolidated tax expense is $662M against $5.029B of pretax income, a computed 13.2% rate, while NEER reports a tax benefit. Tax credits, deferred taxes and regulatory accounting mean that one quarter’s effective rate should not be projected mechanically. We retain these differences in the evidence rather than substituting a normal industrial-company tax rate for every calculation.

Earnings quality requires several tests

The quality scorecard deliberately separates accounting volatility from business weakness. The adjusted growth rate is supported by both operating segments, which is favorable. The size of the hedge bridge and the reliance on external capital nevertheless deserve amber flags. A single green adjusted EPS number cannot settle whether growth creates value after financing costs, customer concessions and new equity.

Nine-month operating cash flow rose to $12.322B from $9.986B. This is useful evidence of cash generation, but it does not make the company self-funding. Cash conversion measured against attributable earnings is complicated by noncontrolling allocations, regulatory recovery and noncash marks. We therefore show the actual cash alongside a consistent broad investment measure, rather than turn one conversion percentage into an all-purpose quality grade.

Customer receivables were $4.841B at quarter end. Dividing that ending balance by quarterly revenue and multiplying by ninety gives a 53.9-day screening proxy. This is not the company’s collection-days KPI: fuel recovery, unbilled balances, seasonality and segment mix can move it. Materials, supplies and fuel inventories were $2.836B. We avoid a misleading inventory-days ratio because a single standardized cost-of-sales denominator does not capture this utility and generation portfolio consistently.

Stock-based compensation was $185M in 2025, $138M in 2024 and $139M in 2023, verified in the audited stock-compensation note. The provider’s cash-flow feed shows zero for this field, illustrating why an absent standardized value must not become a claim of no dilution expense. We also preserve share-count changes separately from SBC because equity units, financing issuance and the proposed transaction can affect shares through other channels. There is no automatic adjustment that makes dilution disappear from per-share economics.

The growth plan needs continuing capital access

At September 30, 2026, cash and equivalents were $5.579B. Debt as defined here totals $113.932B: commercial paper, other short-term debt, current maturities and long-term debt. Subtracting cash gives $108.353B net debt, up $15.546B from year-end. Restricted and special-use funds are not freely interchangeable with parent cash; we do not subtract the $11.677B of special-use funds from this debt measure. The resulting figure is a transparent screen, not a credit-rating agency leverage calculation.

The investment definition is central. Nine-month cash uses include $8.160B of FPL capex, $19.044B of independent power and other investments of NEER, $341M of nuclear fuel and $9M of other capex. Together they total $27.554B, compared with $12.322B operating cash. The $15.232B difference grows to $19.139B after common dividends. This is a broad funding gap before asset sales, tax-equity participation, new debt, equity issuance and other financing sources. It is not a claim that $27.554B is maintenance capex or that all project investment destroys value.

That distinction is why we reject the provider’s narrow free-cash-flow field as the main story. Its capital-expenditure definition omits the independent-power investment line. Using only that field would make the business look far more self-funding than its own statement demonstrates. The primary cash-flow table preserves the full lines and lets readers judge what is growth spending versus ongoing support of the asset base.

A utility can rationally invest more than it generates internally when the new assets earn attractive regulated or contracted returns. The risk is the spread between those returns and the complete cost of capital, including equity and minority financing. Refinancing rates, project delays, recovery lags and contractual protections all matter. Rising net debt is therefore a reason to examine returns and liquidity, not a standalone solvency conclusion. The counterargument to our caution is that today’s cash outflow builds tomorrow’s earnings base; the required proof is profitable conversion on financing terms that protect common shareholders.

Three routes and a visible margin of safety

The primary route values adjusted earnings at a multiple appropriate to a growing utility with material financing requirements. Using the $4.02 high end of management’s 2026 range and twenty times earnings gives $80.40 per share. Our published base is rounded down to $80. This is an analyst-selected multiple, not a company target or an assertion that utility multiples cannot fall. At the reference price, the implied multiple is 19.6 times, so the market already pays for a substantial portion of the expected execution.

The segment route uses trailing adjusted earnings of $5.452B for FPL, $3.998B for NEER and a $1.043B corporate loss. We grow the two positive segment earnings bases by an assumed 8%, apply twenty-three times to FPL and sixteen times to NEER, and capitalize the un-grown corporate loss at eighteen times. Dividing by 2,098M diluted shares gives $88.53. These are equity-earnings multiples: allocated financing costs are already in earnings, so subtracting all net debt again would double count the financing burden. The separate debt analysis is still necessary because the multiples assume continued access to capital.

The dividend route starts with the declared quarterly rate of $0.6232, annualized to $2.4928. We assume 6% growth for two years, then 4% perpetually, discounted at a 7.2% required equity return. The present value is $84.11. Management’s 6% dividend-growth expectation runs through 2028; the perpetual rate and discount rate are our assumptions. The terminal value dominates, so modest changes can move the estimate sharply. This route is a cross-check, not independent proof of a precise fair value.

Together the models support an approximate eighty-to-high-eighties zone before uncertainty. We select the lower $80 anchor because the funding program and transaction conditions remain unresolved. High uncertainty warrants a 20% margin of safety, giving a $64 review threshold. Our bear case uses $4.00 forward adjusted EPS at fifteen times for $60. The bull case uses $4.55 at twenty-two times for approximately $100. These assumptions are deliberately visible. Equal 25% tails around a 50% base produce an $80 probability-weighted value; the probabilities express judgment, not measured odds.

The basic enterprise-value bridge is model equity value of $165.448B plus $108.353B net debt, or $273.801B. It excludes separate adjustments for minority interests, leases and pensions, so it is not advertised as a complete acquisition price. We do not value the company with an EV multiple and then compare it unqualified with an equity multiple. Most importantly, no Dominion earnings or prospective synergy is inserted into a standalone denominator before closing.

Grade capital allocation and transaction promises

Management’s strongest evidence is the operating consistency of FPL and the continued development activity at NEER. The company ties FPL’s result to disciplined capital investment and emphasizes relatively low customer bills and reliability. Those comparisons are management claims in the release; we do not independently recreate every industry benchmark. They matter because affordability can support regulatory acceptance, but low bills today do not guarantee future approvals or remove storm costs.

The pending Dominion combination would give each Dominion share 0.8138 NEE shares. Existing NEE holders keep their share count, but are expected to own about 74.5% of the combined company, with Dominion holders owning about 25.5%. A $360M one-time cash payment to Dominion holders is also part of the announced terms. Keeping the same number of NEE shares is not the same as avoiding ownership dilution. The relevant economic test is whether the additional earnings and cash flow justify the newly issued shares and obligations.

Shareholder approvals on September 3 satisfied an important condition, but do not replace the state and federal approvals. The October release describes Virginia and South Carolina hearings in November and December, with final orders expected by the companies in January 2027, and North Carolina expert hearings beginning in February. Closing remains expected in the second half of 2027. Management’s combined-company ambition of at least 9% adjusted EPS growth from the 2025 base is a prospective case, distinct from the standalone at-least-8% framework.

Customer and community commitments can facilitate approval while absorbing economic benefits. The updated Virginia package extends residential bill credits, adds shareholder-funded assistance and workforce commitments, and includes supplier spending. These should not be ignored when valuing synergy. Our model gives no immediate earnings credit for the transaction; later research must examine approved conditions, financing, purchase accounting and the actual diluted denominator before upgrading the growth case.

The bull case is scale; the bear case is funding

The strongest bull case is not the GAAP EPS headline. It is that a well-positioned regulated utility and a large development platform can meet growing electricity demand with operating expertise, procurement scale and access to attractive projects. FPL investment can compound the regulated base, storage can complement renewable generation, and large-load customers can support long-duration demand. If capital is deployed at adequate returns, today’s negative cash after investment can be a rational precursor to a larger earning base.

The strongest bear case is that demand alone does not guarantee shareholder returns. Projects must be permitted, connected, built and financed, and customers or regulators must accept prices that recover the cost. Rising interest rates or weaker equity valuations can make otherwise attractive growth less accretive. Development delays can leave debt outstanding before earnings arrive. Policy changes, tax-credit restrictions and supply-chain constraints can also alter the original investment case.

The combination adds a separate layer. Regulatory remedies could reduce expected benefits; approvals could be delayed; integration could consume management attention; and a much larger share count changes comparisons. The fixed exchange ratio protects the number of shares exchanged per Dominion share, not the market value of those shares. We therefore keep the continuing parent’s reported period analyzable while refusing to treat the deal as already completed.

Weather creates another timing issue. Hurricane Isaias occurred after the September quarter end. The release discusses restoration and grid resilience, but this packet does not book an unreported storm charge or assume all restoration costs will be recovered. Future costs, insurance and regulatory recovery need their own evidence. Our qualitative risk ranking is a way to focus research attention, not a numerical default-probability model.

Use dates to test the thesis

The next important checkpoint is full-year delivery against the unchanged adjusted EPS range. We will examine the bridge between segment earnings, corporate financing and the share count, not just whether the headline lands near the high end. Operating execution without a credible funding plan would not justify the same valuation as equally strong earnings supported by better internal cash coverage.

Regulatory milestones create information events before the expected merger close. Virginia and South Carolina hearings, the expected early-2027 orders and North Carolina’s February hearings can clarify which benefits belong to customers, employees and shareholders. The company’s expected order dates are not guaranteed commission decisions. A favorable ruling also needs to be read for conditions; the word approved alone is not enough to value the economics.

NEER’s year-end development update will test whether the pipeline of potential hubs moves toward the stated forty-hub ambition from thirty discussed in the release. Potential hubs remain development opportunities, not a booked earnings forecast. The Duane Arnold restart is still expected no later than the first quarter of 2029, and its recently closed loan of up to $1.9B supports the project without proving its completion. Point Beach’s new power-purchase agreement remains subject to Wisconsin regulatory approval.

We would become more constructive if a lower entry price or a demonstrably stronger earnings-and-funding path created a durable margin of safety. We would lower value if financing becomes less favorable, approvals impose materially worse terms, or backlog conversion weakens. The price threshold is conditional: a falling share price does not automatically make a damaged thesis attractive. Each checkpoint should refresh the model rather than preserve an old target by habit.

Audit trail, definitions and limits

Financial amounts are US dollars in millions unless explicitly labeled per share, billions, percentages or gigawatts. The nine-quarter audit runs from the third quarter of 2024 through the third quarter of 2026. Every release table was extracted with its actual column header: FPL, NEER, Corporate and Other, and consolidated NextEra. The source dataset retains complete operating-expense, below-the-line, reconciliation, segment, balance-sheet and cash-flow rows, so a simplified chart does not erase the details behind it.

The historical exhibits are preliminary unaudited releases. The three annual reports provide audited cash-flow and structural evidence for 2023, 2024 and 2025. Historical release values can differ from later standardized provider values because of revisions, rounding or mapping. We retain and label the chosen primary series rather than silently splice inconsistent definitions. For example, the saved Q2 2026 release shows $1.50 GAAP EPS, while the provider field shows $1.51; the chart uses the release series. The current quarterly statement and September balance sheet are available, so no June balance is disguised as September.

Quarterly cash flow is derived by subtracting year-to-date periods. The first quarter in the nine-quarter window lacks the preceding half-year in this normalized series, so its standalone quarterly cash calculation remains blank; its actual nine-month table is retained. Annual cash-flow figures are not derived from that missing quarter. Broad deployment includes FPL capital expenditure, the complete NEER independent-power-and-other-investment line, nuclear fuel and other capex. It is deliberately distinct from free cash flow, maintenance spending and the provider’s narrower capex field.

NEE is a US common equity with no ADS conversion. Deloitte & Touche is the identified auditor. Project VIEs, differential membership interests and related-party cash arrangements require careful consolidation and parent-liquidity analysis; they are not assumed to be an offshore ownership structure. The annual report identifies a historical share-repurchase authorization, but we do not infer a current remaining amount or a cash repurchase figure from an absent cash-flow line. Employee counts shown are FPL and NEER segment disclosures, not a made-up consolidated headcount.

The release is the basis of this packet’s current-period commentary. A scheduled conference call is not evidence that we reviewed its remarks, and no unsaved transcript claims are included. Historical return observations use saved provider prices and the first regular close on or after each release, which cannot isolate the causal impact of earnings. The current unfinished session is excluded. This is a new exact-period research snapshot: it does not invent prior signpost grades or reopen already published prior-quarter work.
