The print — operating growth and an investment-mark EPS decline

Third-quarter revenue was $127.577 billion, up 24.65% from $102.346 billion. Operating income was $40.576 billion, up 29.93%, with a 31.81% operating margin. Common-share net income was $26.170 billion and diluted EPS was $2.12, down 26.13%. These are compatible results because a large change below operating income overwhelmed the improvement in the underlying operating businesses. Treating the EPS decline as evidence that operations shrank would be misleading.

The retained FMP consensus row shows $127.962 billion revenue and $2.97 EPS. On that dated provider basis, reported sales were 0.30% below the estimate and EPS was 28.62% below. The estimate was retrieved after the release and is not our independently captured final pre-release poll. The provider does not supply a complete accounting reconciliation for the EPS expectation. We therefore show the comparison without claiming a precisely comparable adjusted-earnings miss or using it as a substitute for the filed profit bridge.

Cost of revenue was $51.419 billion, research and development $20.640 billion, sales and marketing $8.804 billion, and general and administrative expense $6.138 billion. Their sum leaves the reported operating profit. R&D is a real operating expense even when it builds future AI capabilities. We do not remove it from a normalized valuation merely because management views the spending as strategic. Similarly, depreciation is the cost of previously installed infrastructure, not an optional accounting inconvenience.

Segments — Cloud includes a different revenue mix

Cloud revenue increased from $15.157 billion to $30.709 billion, a 102.61% increase. Segment operating income rose from $3.594 billion to $11.121 billion and margin reached 36.21%. The issuer identifies strong Google Cloud Platform demand across enterprise AI infrastructure, AI solutions and core services. It also says TPU system sales meaningfully contributed to revenue growth. Hardware product revenue should not automatically receive the same recurring-revenue interpretation as consumption of hosted cloud services.

Wiz was acquired in March and is included in Cloud. The current release does not provide a quantified organic bridge that separates acquired revenue and TPU hardware sales from other Cloud activity. We therefore call the result reported growth, not organic growth. The investment case can still be strong without a clean bridge, but the burden of proof is higher when a very large growth rate combines several business models. Customer adoption, repeat purchasing, gross profit and cash collection matter more than a headline alone.

Google Services generated $96.302 billion revenue and $39.157 billion operating income, a 40.66% margin. Search grew approximately twelve percent to $63.350 billion; YouTube advertising reached $11.207 billion; Network advertising $7.455 billion; subscriptions, platforms and devices $14.290 billion. Services remains the largest operating-profit source. Cloud’s growth does not make advertising irrelevant, and a change in Search economics would affect the cash available for infrastructure investment elsewhere in the group.

Sequentially, Cloud operating profit increased $2.307 billion while Services declined $387 million and Alphabet-level costs increased $1.866 billion. Other Bets also lost more. Consolidated operating income consequently slipped slightly from Q2 despite Cloud’s progress. This is not an argument that Cloud lacks value. It shows why a sum of attractive segment headlines cannot be substituted for the actual consolidated result. Shared AI research and the broader portfolio consume part of the segment gains.

Profit bridge — the common shareholder receives the residual

Start with $40.576 billion of operating income. Other income and expense subtracts $8.221 billion, producing $32.355 billion of pretax income. Taxes of $5.887 billion leave $26.468 billion of consolidated net income. Preferred dividends of $298 million then reduce the amount available to common holders to $26.170 billion. Dividing by the reported diluted weighted-average denominator supports the filed $2.12 EPS, subject to the issuer’s per-share calculation and rounding.

The release identifies $8.664 billion of losses on equity securities. Management quantifies their after-tax effect at $6.7 billion, or $0.55 per share. That is useful attribution, but adding fifty-five cents to GAAP EPS does not create a company-issued adjusted EPS metric. Other components of the income statement remain. A durable earnings estimate requires a consistent treatment of investment returns, financing, taxes, compensation and reinvestment across favorable and unfavorable periods.

The asymmetry is visible in the history. Q2 common net income exceeded $112 billion as other income approached $98 billion; Q3 common net income was only $26 billion even though operating income was nearly unchanged. Summed trailing quarterly EPS is $19.16, but much of that earnings base reflects investment marks. A seemingly low trailing price-to-earnings ratio can therefore provide false comfort. Our $13 normalized EPS assumption deliberately differs from the marked trailing result and is an analyst judgment, not an issuer forecast.

Preferred stock adds another distinction. The balance sheet carries $18.023 billion of preferred equity, while the stated liquidation preference is approximately $19 billion. These securities have a 6.25% dividend and mandatory conversion features. They are neither ordinary debt nor currently identical to common shares. Our equity cash models are explicitly after financing and preferred distribution needs, so they do not mechanically add gross cash or subtract the same preferred claim again.

Earnings quality — cash conversion is the demanding test

Operating cash flow was $42.290 billion, down 12.65% year over year despite the rise in operating income. Capital expenditures were $51.355 billion, up 114.40%. The resulting issuer-defined free cash flow was negative $9.065 billion. This is not a claim that the infrastructure has no future value. It establishes that the current quarter did not internally fund all of its capital spending. The return on that spending must be earned in later periods.

Stock compensation was $8.630 billion, or 6.76% of revenue. It is added back in operating cash flow because it is noncash at the time of recognition, but common shareholders still bear an economic cost through dilution or cash spent to offset it. Subtracting SBC from the already negative free cash figure produces negative $17.695 billion as a conservative sensitivity. This is not a second GAAP cash-flow measure, and it should not be combined with a valuation that separately charges the same dilution twice.

Receivables were $72.784 billion. Using the ending balance divided by quarterly revenue and ninety days gives an approximate 51.35-day collection indicator. Inventory was $13.308 billion, equivalent to approximately 23.29 days of quarterly cost of revenue on the same ending-balance convention. These are directional indicators rather than average-balance turnover ratios. The historical exhibits did not always show inventory separately, so missing earlier rows remain unfilled rather than becoming zeros.

Working capital also absorbed cash. Year to date, receivables, inventory and other assets used $10.821 billion, $10.959 billion and $17.757 billion respectively. Those items should not all be described as overdue customer bills: inventory and infrastructure-related assets have different economic causes. The relevant question is whether the larger operating investment produces profitable sales and timely collections. The scorecard therefore treats operating growth, cash conversion and investment-mark earnings as distinct dimensions of quality.

Balance sheet — financing expands alongside the asset base

Cash and marketable securities totaled $265.098 billion at September 30, while the separately presented long-term debt line was $125.102 billion. The simple difference is approximately $140 billion. That is not a complete net-cash distribution value: current debt can sit within accrued liabilities, preferred holders have senior claims, some liquidity supports operations, and the business has substantial future commitments. Nonmarketable securities of $135.230 billion are not interchangeable with immediately distributable cash.

During Q3, common stock issuance generated $12.371 billion of net proceeds. Debt issuance generated $28.996 billion and repayments consumed $3.626 billion. Buybacks were zero. The issuer reports twelve million Class A and twenty-four million Class C shares sold through the ATM program, with $27.6 billion capacity remaining. The June documentation describes the ATM proceeds as primarily intended to meet employee-equity tax obligations. We therefore do not assert that every dollar of new equity directly paid a specific data-center invoice.

The broader pattern is still relevant. Year-to-date common issuance, preferred issuance and gross debt proceeds totaled $42.870 billion, $19.063 billion and $85.222 billion respectively. Cash is fungible, but those financing sources have different costs and claims. A larger cash balance funded partly by new securities is not equivalent to the same cash accumulated entirely from retained operating cash. Per-share valuation must account for the financing route as well as the productive assets purchased.

The latest filed June 10-Q disclosed $811 billion of purchase commitments and other contractual obligations, including $200.7 billion short term. Those are dated June figures, not a newly disclosed September balance. They include infrastructure, inventory, energy and content commitments with different terms; they are not all debt or current payables. We also do not add the $707 billion longer-term fixed commitments to $811 billion, because the categories overlap. They demonstrate the scale of future execution obligations without pretending every commitment is an immediate cash liability.

Valuation — three common-equity routes

The earnings method uses $13 of sustainable diluted EPS and a twenty-three-times multiple, giving $299 per share. Thirteen dollars is our normalized assumption, below the provider’s 2027 mean and below the marked trailing EPS sum. It allows operating growth while refusing to perpetuate unusually large investment gains. The multiple recognizes the quality of Search and the Cloud opportunity but also the capital intensity, governance and financing uncertainty. Neither input is management guidance.

The owner-cash method assumes $140 billion of sustainable annual cash available to common equity after required reinvestment, financing costs, preferred distributions and an economic allowance for compensation. At a four-percent equity cash yield, divided by 12.368 billion diluted shares, it produces $282.99. This assumption is demanding: trailing issuer free cash flow is only $19.747 billion. The route is a normalized future capacity estimate, not a claim that $140 billion is available today or that gross operating cash belongs to shareholders.

The discounted-cash method uses five annual common-equity cash flows of $60 billion, $95 billion, $130 billion, $165 billion and $200 billion. At a 9.5% discount rate and four-percent perpetual growth, the explicit-period present value is $474.856 billion and the terminal present value $2,402.316 billion. Dividing their sum by the same diluted denominator gives $232.63 per share. Terminal value supplies most of this result, which is why the growth and reinvestment assumptions must remain visible.

The three methods average $271.54, rounded to a $270 anchor, with a $233–$299 method range. These are equity models after financing claims, so no second gross-cash addition or debt subtraction is applied. The denominator is held constant for modeling; material future issuance or conversion changes the per-share result and must trigger a refresh. The methods deliberately disagree: earnings can look attractive while cash conversion remains distant. Averaging them does not remove that uncertainty; it exposes the range of assumptions behind the call.

Management — evaluate execution and the claims on it

Management deserves credit for translating AI demand into large reported revenue and operating-profit growth. Search still grew approximately twelve percent and Cloud more than doubled. The official slides show a broader TPU commercialization model, while the current release identifies hardware systems as a meaningful contributor. Those are tangible results. Our caution concerns how much recurring, capital-efficient cash the expanded system ultimately produces, not whether management has built commercially relevant products.

Capital allocation is a more difficult score. The company has moved from substantial repurchases to no buybacks in the first nine months of 2026 while issuing common and preferred securities and substantial debt. Financing can be rational when investment opportunities exceed internal cash. It can also shift risk toward existing holders if returns take longer than expected or dilution expands faster than earnings capacity. The evaluation must follow return on investment and per-share outcomes, not just the gross size of the spending program.

Governance limits the outside shareholder’s influence. The 2025 annual filing says Class B has ten votes per share and the founders held approximately 52.7% of common voting power at year-end. The current common share classes and newly issued preferred securities have different rights. The capped-call transactions associated with the preferred issuance are intended to reduce potential conversion dilution; they are not a guarantee of no dilution under every future share price or contract condition.

The next questions are concrete. How much Cloud growth is recurring service consumption versus systems delivered? What is the cash and margin profile of those systems? How will financing, preferred conversion and employee awards affect diluted shares? What portion of the investment becomes productive within the next two years? And how should investors reconcile backlog with the changed definition? The June filing added short-duration contracts to backlog beginning in Q1, so the $519.5 billion June total cannot be compared mechanically with older definitions or presented as a September update.

Risks — a strong franchise can still be an expensive claim

The bull case is that Alphabet’s full-stack capabilities reinforce one another. Search supplies distribution and cash, Cloud monetizes AI infrastructure and enterprise software, TPU systems extend the addressable market, and Gemini improves products and developer engagement. A larger revenue base could absorb shared research and infrastructure costs over time. If the new assets earn attractive returns and cash conversion improves, today’s funding burden can become tomorrow’s durable advantage.

The bear case is that the capital cycle remains expensive for longer than shareholders expect. Hardware revenue can be less recurring than service consumption, depreciation and operating costs follow the installed base, and customers can change spending plans. Large supplier, energy and infrastructure commitments can reduce flexibility when demand or technology changes. Ordinary acquisitions such as Wiz and Intersect add integration and accounting complexity even though they do not prevent coverage of the continuing listed parent.

Competition and regulatory proceedings are additional risks. The latest annual filing describes adverse Search rulings, appeals and potential advertising-technology remedies; the latest June filing and subsequent verified submissions remain the dated source set available for this review. We do not announce a new court outcome or assume every risk is resolved. Restrictions on distribution, data use or commercial arrangements could affect monetization and costs. This uncertainty belongs in valuation and monitoring rather than being replaced with a dramatic but unsupported legal prediction.

Our scenario model assigns twenty-five percent to a $190 bear value, fifty percent to the $270 base and twenty-five percent to a $390 bull value. The bear uses $10 normalized EPS at nineteen times; the bull uses $15 at twenty-six times. Weighted value is $280. These probabilities express analyst judgment, not measured frequencies. High uncertainty supports a meaningful margin of safety, while the observed strength of the core businesses argues against treating the financing shift alone as proof of permanent impairment.

Catalysts — dated checkpoints, not invented event dates

The next-quarter report is the most useful operating checkpoint, but its date was not confirmed in the primary material reviewed. We use February 15, 2027 as an analyst monitoring deadline rather than presenting it as an announced earnings date. At that check we will examine Cloud mix, Services profitability, infrastructure spending, operating cash, financing and the diluted share count. Thresholds below are our decision rules, not management targets.

Before then, a November 15 research check will look for the Q3 10-Q and any official transcript or additional earnings materials. That filing could update commitments, backlog, legal proceedings and financing details that are currently dated to June. A later source should replace an earlier uncertainty transparently, not be backfilled as if it had been known at the original publication cutoff. The September statements already provide the core earnings and balance-sheet result; the missing detail does not justify inventing it.

The board declared a $0.22 common dividend payable December 14 to holders of record December 7. This is a cash distribution, not evidence that total shareholder cash generation is positive after capex. Preferred distributions have their own November schedule and claims. The continuing ATM capacity also creates a practical monitoring item: future issuance can support obligations while changing the denominator investors own.

We will reconsider the HOLD if normalized earnings and cash capacity improve enough to support a higher fair value, or if the price offers the required cushion without a damaged thesis. Conversely, persistent negative cash, declining Services economics or material dilution without adequate returns can lower the value estimate. The checkpoints make the call falsifiable. They are designed to prevent a strong product story from becoming a permanent exemption from ordinary capital-allocation arithmetic.
